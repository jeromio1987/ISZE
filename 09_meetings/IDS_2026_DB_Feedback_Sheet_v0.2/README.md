# Isuzu Dealer Standards 2026 — dealer feedback sheet (v0.2)

A working document for distributors to use with their dealers after the 2026 IDS
evaluation. It turns the evaluation result into something a dealer can read in
two minutes and act on the same day.

This is a **draft for review (v0.2)** — not yet a released pack.

**Built for five markets, every evaluated dealer:**

| Market | Dealers with a sheet | Market average | Good | Medium | Needs improvement |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| United Kingdom (LCV) | 45 | 81.5% | 25 | 17 | 3 |
| Poland | 23 | 84.8% | 18 | 5 | 0 |
| Greece | 34 | 73.8% | 7 | 26 | 1 |
| Belgium | 34 | 78.5% | 16 | 16 | 2 |
| Netherlands | 9 | 76.1% | 2 | 7 | 0 |

Every evaluated dealer in these five markets counts towards its market average —
none of them has an incomplete evaluation to set aside.

Belgium and the Netherlands are built from their **own** market reports and are
kept separate. Neither is derived from the combined Benelux report, which does
not reconcile to the sum of the individual markets.

## What is in the folder

| Layer | What it is | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Executive summary** | One printed page per dealer: total score, band, position against the market average, top 3 strengths, top 3 gaps | Open the review. Hand it to the dealer principal. |
| **B — Dealer workshop pack** | The executive summary plus the section scorecard A–E and a Kaizen action table | The working session with the dealer. |
| **C — Distributor rollup** | Network cover for the whole market: average, band distribution, section averages, full dealer list linked to each dealer's pack | Your own planning: where to spend visit time. |
| **D — Commitments** | The block the dealer fills in and returns | Follow-up. All returned files can be merged into one list. |

Where a market lists the same company more than once, each entry keeps its own
sheet and the dealer list shows the address underneath the company name so the
rows can be told apart. Where the report carries no address for those entries —
as in Belgium — the rows cannot be distinguished, and the repeat is listed in the
table at the end of this file for the market to confirm.

## How a distributor uses it

1. **Prepare.** Open the rollup (layer C) and read the network average, the band
   distribution and the dealer list. That is where you decide who to visit first.
2. **Print the one-pager.** Open the executive summary in a browser and print it,
   or save it as PDF. It is designed to fill exactly one A4 page.
3. **Run the review.** Walk through the section scorecard as a structure for the
   conversation, then agree **3 to 5 Kaizen actions**. The actions are pre-filled
   from the individual checklist items where the dealer lost the most points —
   reword them so the dealer recognises the action as its own. Every action needs
   an owner (role), a target date and the proof you will accept as done.
4. **Close the loop.** The dealer keeps the Commitments sheet. When the file comes
   back, the commitments of every dealer can be merged into a single list for the
   whole network.

### Working in Excel

The workbook has six sheets:

| Sheet | Content |
|---|---|
| Read me | The same instructions, inside the file |
| Dealer fiche | The printable one-pager |
| Sections A-E | Section scores against the market average |
| Kaizen actions | 3 to 5 actions, with owner / target date / proof |
| Commitments | The block the dealer returns |
| Master data | The full market table, ready for filters and pivots |

Yellow cells are for you and the dealer to fill in. Everything else comes from
the evaluation.

## How the numbers are built

| Figure | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| Total score, section scores, checklist items | 2026 IDS evaluation result report for the market |
| Evaluation date, address | The same report, exactly as the market entered it |
| Maximum score | 280 points across sections A–E (A 46, B 80, C 80, D 47, E 27) |
| Market average, section averages | Calculated from the dealers evaluated in that market — see the note below |
| Rank | Position among **all** evaluated dealers in the market, highest score first |
| Top 3 strengths | The heaviest checklist items scored in full |
| Top 3 gaps | The checklist items losing the most points |

**Bands:** Good 80% and above · Medium 60% to under 80% · Needs improvement
below 60%. The band is printed together with the scale on every sheet, so the
result can always be traced back.

### How to read sections A–E

> In the 2026 results the five sections move closely together — a dealer who
> scores well on one section usually scores well on the others. Use the section
> table as **structure for the conversation**, not as five independent
> capabilities, and do not read a single section as a verdict on that part of the
> business. That is why the actions are built from the individual checklist items
> where points were actually lost, and not from a section total.

The section table is ordered lowest average first because that is a practical
place to start a conversation — not because a low section average diagnoses a
weakness in that part of the business.

### Note on the average

> The market average is calculated on the dealers evaluated in that market. Where
> a market contains dealers whose evaluation was not completed, those are excluded
> **from the average calculation only** — they remain part of the network, stay in
> the dealer list and keep their own feedback sheet.
>
> **In all five markets in this folder there is nothing to exclude:** every
> evaluated dealer counts towards the average. Each sheet states this, for example
> *"45 of 45 dealers in average"* for the United Kingdom (LCV).

A section average can rest on fewer dealers than the total average when a market
left that section blank for some dealers. The rollup therefore shows a **Dealers
scored** column next to each section average.

### Fields shown as N.A.

Where the 2026 evaluation data does not contain a value, the sheet shows **N.A.**
or is left as a blank fill-in line on purpose. Nothing is estimated or filled in
by assumption.

The evaluator column of the source report is not reproduced on these sheets:
these documents are shared with the dealer, and naming the individual who carried
out the evaluation adds nothing to the conversation.

## Rebuilding the sheets

Generator: `C:\Users\jerom\Desktop\claude\ISZE\04_tools\ids_dealer_ranking\build_db_feedback_sheet.py`

```powershell
# the five markets in this folder, every dealer, with the rollup
python build_db_feedback_sheet.py build --country "UK LCV" --country Poland --country Greece --country Belgium --country Netherlands --all-dealers --rollup

# a single market
python build_db_feedback_sheet.py build --country Belgium --all-dealers --rollup

# one named dealer only
python build_db_feedback_sheet.py build --country Greece --dealer "Sofia France Auto"

# the blank template
python build_db_feedback_sheet.py template

# merge the Commitments sheets of returned workbooks into one list
python build_db_feedback_sheet.py consolidate --in-dir <folder with returned files>
```

`_build_manifest.json` records, per market, the source report file, the dealer
counts, the average and its basis, the band distribution and the section
averages with the number of dealers behind each one.

## Relation to v0.1

`IDS_2026_DB_Feedback_Sheet_v0.1` (France worked example) is unchanged and stays
in place. v0.2 exists because the client-facing wording changed, so the two
should not carry the same label:

- Sections A–E are now presented as **conversation structure**. The per-section
  band verdict has been removed from the scorecard, and every sheet carries the
  note on how to read the sections.
- The average note is now **written per market**, so a market with nothing to
  exclude no longer reads an explanation about markets that do.
- Evaluation date and address are now filled from the report where the market
  entered them, instead of always being blank lines.
- Each section average is shown with the **number of dealers scored**.
- Dealer files sit in a `dealers/` subfolder per market, filenames are plain
  ASCII, and repeated company names are kept apart per site.
