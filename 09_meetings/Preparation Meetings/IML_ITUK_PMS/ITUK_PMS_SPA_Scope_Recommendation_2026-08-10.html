The BVP study

BVP ("Best Value Parts") is Isuzu's own budget OEM range — not third-party aftermarket. Losing a sale to BVP keeps the revenue in-house at lower margin; losing it to IAM loses it entirely. The two are not the same threat, and the difference changes which categories an SPA — and an IML purchase-price ask — actually defend.

Brake has no BVP fallback for N75 — genuine price is the only lever

Isuzu's brake_pad BVP programme only pairs to TF* (D-Max); brake_shoe BVP only pairs to NHR/NKR/NLR/NMR — never NPR (N75). At ITUK, brake pad and brake shoe both run 0% BVP today. If genuine brake isn't price-competitive against IAM, ITUK's dealers have nowhere to go except third-party — there's no in-house cushion the way there is for filters. This is the sharpest version of the case for discounting brake specifically, and for asking IML to help fund it: every unit lost here is lost completely, not partially.

Filters and clutch already have a BVP cushion — nuance the IML narrative accordingly. Air, fuel, oil filter and clutch kit all show real BVP uptake (15–56% of units at ITUK CY2025). The standard IML-brief line — "without SPA pricing, the distributor routes to IAM and IML/IMIT loses the volume entirely" — is fully true for brake, but only partly true here: some of what looks like defection is actually landing on Isuzu's own BVP line, not external aftermarket. Worth correcting before it goes to Japan as a blanket claim.
Clutch: the BVP data changes the recommendation, not just confirms it. BVP already carries 56.5% of clutch-kit units and 73.4% of clutch-kit revenue at ITUK — more revenue than genuine. Because BVP is Isuzu's own line, a genuine SPA discount on clutch mostly reshuffles which Isuzu product captures the sale — it doesn't clearly recover volume from IAM the way brake does. The "clutch deferred" call from the phase-one decision holds; this is why. Recommend dropping clutch from SPA v2 rather than pruning it to a handful of PNs.
