## 1. Duncan Young → Masumi (+ Jerome Cc) — Fri 24 Apr 2026 11:49

Hi Masumi,

It was a pleasure hosting you and the team during your visit and always good to meet face to face, we really need to do that more often.

I have reviewed the attached spreadsheet in detail alongside your explanation. I noticed an error with the N75 2024 data in cell “F9”. This seems to include N55/55,N62/65,N75 & UNSPECIFIED. The 417 units in UNSPECIFIED needs to be removed as we are not sure what vehicles are in this category.

Overall, the rest of the structure provides a useful baseline for future parts sales potential, and I agree with the general approach of linking UIO development, parts spend per unit, and market share.

A few observations and points for consideration:

**UIO and Attrition Assumptions**  
The use of re-tax data as a foundation is sensible. From a UK perspective, I would recommend we refine this further using the actual data we have on attrition rate. (refer attached spreadsheet “Attrition Rate”

**Parts Sales per Unit Assumptions**  
The values applied (£400 / £1,000 / £1,200) align broadly with my internal benchmarks, however there are a lot of assumptions in those calculations.  
That said, there are two areas we should consider:

- Variance by vehicle age (older vehicles typically generate higher spend)
- Dealer penetration and retention performance, which can materially influence realised revenue per unit

We may be able to improve accuracy by segmenting spend assumptions further over time with more data.

**“Stretch” Target for 2026**  
The additional ~£500k opportunity equating to a 1% share increase is realistic in principle. However, the error in unit (cell “F9”) calculation will affect the forward calculation  
Delivery will also depend on:

- Clear identification of target categories (IML vs non-IML)
- Strong dealer engagement and execution
- Availability and competitiveness of PMS promotional support

I agree that PMS activity will be an important lever, but we should also ensure focus on higher-value parts categories where we have greater upside potential.

**Isuzu IX Growth Assumption (Orange Cells)**  
The 76% growth versus the 2023 baseline is notably ambitious.  
While I understand this has been incorporated into wider planning assumptions, from an ITUK perspective:

- This level of growth would require a significant change in both vehicle parc and parts competitiveness in the UK market against factors.
- It may raise questions unless supported by a clearly defined roadmap (product, network expansion, retention strategy, etc.)

I am happy to with ISZE to continue refining this model. In particular, I suggest we:

- Review and agree a more representative attrition methodology
- Define the key levers required to achieve the proposed share increase

I’ll use this thinking for the strategy discussion for the meeting on the 27th of April.

Duncan Young  
Parts Director, Isuzu Truck (UK) Ltd

## 2. Masumi Hayakawa → Duncan (+ Jerome Cc) — Tue 21 Apr 2026 16:55

Hi, Duncan,

Thank you first of all for hosting us during your visit two weeks ago.

We used your actual UIO (re-tax data) and future unit sales plan (as per Kosha) to estimate the future UIO projection.

One challenge we encountered was the attrition rates for vehicles registered before 2010.

We therefore applied an arbitrary 10% reduction for pre 2008, 2008 and 2009.

If we were to apply IML’s attrition rate (100% after 16 years), a big dip in 2026 will appear, as the reduction in parc would exceed the newly injected units.

We then applied the following parts sales per unit by model (previously estimated based on I-Maintain data)

- 3.5t: 400 GBP  
- 7.5t: 1,000 GBP  
- F-series: 1,200 GBP  

Please refer to the attached excel file for the detailed calculations.

Firm/actual figures are marked in yellow, estimates based on the above criteria are in blue.

The green cells shows the “stretch” idea – additional 500k GBP or so for 2026 was mentioned during the conversation if you recall.

According to the model, it represents 1% share increase from 2025.

IML’s PMS parts promotion is a part of the key levers to support the incremental sales.

As discussed, ITUK and ISZE can also focus on other parts categories with higher sales potential, for which ISZE can consider providing support.
