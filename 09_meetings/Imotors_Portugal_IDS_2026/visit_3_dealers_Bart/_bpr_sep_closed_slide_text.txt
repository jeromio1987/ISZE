=== SLIDE 10 ===
YOUR DATA
How we use the data I Motors shares, and what it can do for I Motors
Sales to dealer by part number and month  |  vehicle registrations  |  quantities; euro values at the ISZE price list
Sources: I Motors sales-to-dealer PN file (January 2025 to August 2026); KPI Check Sheet for Sep 2026 sales-to-dealer euros; I Motors registration file (to June 2026); ISZE billing; ISZE PR00 list 10 Aug 2026.
WHAT YOU SHARED
•  Sales to dealer PN file: 26,966 lines, 7,262 part numbers, 91,153 units, January 2025 to August 2026 (month × PN × qty). September euros come from the KPI Check Sheet (€416.8k), not yet from a Sep PN extract.
•  Registrations: 14,412 vehicles with chassis, model and first-registration date, 2005 to June 2026.
WHAT WE DID WITH IT
•  Grouped part numbers into PMS families with the ISZE parts catalogue.
•  Compared units sold to dealers with units ISZE billed to I Motors, family by family and month by month.
•  Measured PMS cover on both stages against the same fleet demand.
WHAT WE DID NOT ASSUME
•  No dealer prices and no I Motors margins: euro values use the ISZE list price, the same for both stages.
•  No split by dealer: the file is at network level.
•  Nothing sold outside the dealer file.
WHAT IT DOES FOR I MOTORS, IF THE FILE KEEPS COMING EVERY MONTH
Replenishment
ISZE can size supply on what your dealers actually buy, not on past orders: fewer back orders on fast movers, stock kept at the 3.0 MOS target, and an early warning when a family runs low.
SPA on selected part numbers
Where dealer demand lags the fleet, ISZE can lower its price for a period, provided I Motors lowers its price to dealers as well. The monthly file is how both sides measure whether dealer sales grew.
OBSERVATIONS / QUESTIONS
Sales to dealer is the closest measure of real demand we have. Distributor purchases also include stock decisions.
Comparing the two shows when I Motors bought ahead and when it ran stock down, family by family.
The next slides use this to separate stock timing from real changes in dealer demand.
Request: keep the monthly file coming, ideally with a monthly stock snapshot. It is the basis for both replenishment and any SPA.

APPENDIX  ·  FLEET
Why the 10-year count is higher: scrapping inside the ten years outweighs the older vehicles still counted
FY2026  |  bridge from the 10-year car park to the scrap-rate model  |  vehicles
+149
OBSERVATIONS / QUESTIONS
The scrap-rate model removes 1,285 vehicles from the last ten years (for example, a truck aged 10 counts as 0.55, a D-Max as 0.40), but only adds back 149 older vehicles.
Portugal registered few vehicles in the crisis years: 2,193 in FY2011 to FY2016, against 5,333 in FY2021 to FY2026. There are few old vehicles left to add back.
In a market with a large older fleet the result would flip, and the scrap-rate model would be the higher of the two.
Neither method sees vehicles that were exported, imported used, or scrapped early. The file counts first registrations only.
Source: I Motors registration file (vehicle level, to June 2026); ISZE fleet model survival curves.

APPENDIX  ·  FLEET
Each registration year: what the two methods count in FY2026
Registrations by fiscal year and the vehicles each method counts in FY2026  |  FY2026 = April 2025 to March 2026
OBSERVATIONS / QUESTIONS
The 10-year car park counts every vehicle from FY2017 onwards in full and stops there. The scrap-rate model thins out each registration year as it gets older.
The two methods agree on the newest vehicles. They differ most on vehicles aged 6 to 10, which the scrap-rate model already reduces by 5 to 45% (trucks) and 16 to 60% (D-Max).
Vehicles older than ten years only count in the scrap-rate model, and in Portugal there are few of them.
FY2027 registrations (April to June 2026) are in the file but not used here: the fleet is measured at the end of each full fiscal year.

=== SLIDE 35 ===
APPENDIX  ·  METHOD
How these figures were built
Data used, what was assumed, and what was not
Fleet
I Motors registration file, one line per vehicle, first registrations 2005 to June 2026. Light trucks, F-Series and D-Max are counted separately. Older N-series codes without a tonnage flag are counted as light trucks.
10-year car park
All vehicles first registered in the last ten fiscal years, each counted once. Nothing older is counted.
Scrap-rate model
Each registration year is reduced with the survival curve used in the ISZE fleet model: trucks 100% in year 1, 55% at age 10, 0% after age 15; D-Max 100% in year 1, 40% at age 10, 0% after age 11.
Genuine parts share
All ISZE billing to I Motors in the fiscal year, warranty parts included, divided by target spend: fleet × €739 (light truck), €887 (F-Series), €250 (D-Max) per vehicle per year.
FY2027 pace
April to August 2026 billing × 12/5 (rule of three), against the €1.858M FY2027 target and the FY2026 fleet.
Stock movement
Units billed by ISZE to I Motors against units I Motors reported as sold to dealers, per part number and month (sales to dealer from January 2025). Parts are grouped into PMS families. No prices or margins of I Motors are used.
Campaign price
Lines billed at least 20% under list price are marked as bought at campaign price.
