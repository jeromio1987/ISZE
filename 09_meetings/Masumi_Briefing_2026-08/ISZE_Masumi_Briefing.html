3. UIO — V20 spec

The new UIO V20 spec will have no influence on the GPS meetings, as the targets are fixed. V20 is to increase the reliability of our model and to refine it. Two kinds of improvement — they are not the same job.

1. Corrected method

In June a 3% yearly attrition step crept into the 2026–2030 forecast. We do not know who put it there. The history in the same file already assumes about 10% of trucks leave each year. Keeping 97% makes the fleet grow even when registrations are falling. That is a method error, not new data.

2. Better data

Where a customer (or a government register) has given us actual trucks, we can see the real parc — including the 3.5 t vs 7.5 t split. Ireland (SIMI) and Portugal (registrations) already went in at V18. UTI has now given Ministry totals. That is better data, not a formula change.

IUK — the method, drawn

UK pickups, 2025 actual = 47,783. From 2026 the live file applies the 3% step. UK D-Max registrations are falling (IMIT 2026 about 3,020). A shrinking inflow cannot grow the parc. The locked correction (30 July) is 47,783 × (1 − 0.123) + 3,020 = 44,926.

Green 2026 = locked correction. Terracotta 2026 and 2030 = live V19. The 3% step does not only miss 2026 by 4,354 units — it keeps adding every year, so 2030 is 52,804 and still rising. GPS 3.1 does not wait for this rebuild.

I-Motors Portugal — 3.5 t vs 7.5 t, from better data

Government registrations already corrected the size of the Portugal parc in V18. What V20 still adds is the split of the current car park. The VIN / shipment file shows about 68% of Portugal’s N-series are 3.5 t Grafters. Today the model treats every “LD” truck as a 7.5 t N75 (€739 wallet). A 3.5 t truck should sit at €296. The model is improving because we can finally see that mix.

Indication from 2020–26 shipments applied to the 2025 fleet — not a restated Portugal GPS % for the meetings. Ireland’s 3.5 t share is only about 6%; there the wallet barely moves. Portugal is where the split matters.

UTI — the government numbers we now have

UTI’s July parts slides show the Ministry of Transport registered fleet: 14,019 at 30 June 2026, against V19’s 10,760. Gap about a quarter, in every year — a missing block of vehicles, not a scrap-rate tweak. By class: 6.5–7.5 t 8,610 · 7.6–10 t 2,485 · 10.1–12 t 2,697 · 12.1–16 t 227. V20 rebuilds UTI on those classes, year by year — not a flat scale-up. We still need the extract by year and weight class, not only the slide totals.

Also in the V20 spec — not yet a government file

ISD / Germany does not have a government register. It is the weakest cell in V19: 697 trucks in 2025 growing to 3,756 by 2030, and the 2019–2024 history was filled backwards from one 2025 point. Sebastian is refreshing their own parc for the GPS talk. The Japan VIN list has only 183 German shipments (not registrations). V20 flags Germany as indicative until a real source exists — we do not invent one.

Belgium, Cyprus, Malta, Greece are still on the old IML estimate. Same VIN list: 49 / 114 / 130 / 4 shipments. Queue them for a registration rebuild, same as Germany. ITUK trucks are already on UK registrations and a survival model — leave that. Medium-duty rows that grow from zero on plan only (Ireland, Portugal, Greece, Cyprus) stay out of the Grand Total until a first real registration shows up.
