Management read
V19 builds its history with a realistic vehicle life but rolls the forecast forward with a flat 3% scrap rate, which implies a 33-year truck life. V20 applies the same survival curve to 2026–2030 that V19 already uses for 1990–2025. With the new-registration plan unchanged, the car park now declines instead of growing.
OBSERVATIONS / QUESTIONS
  • V19 stays the official version until the V20 draft is approved.
  • Italy (MIDI Europe) accounts for 33,055 of the 2030 difference; the current plan has no new trucks for MIDI from 2026.
  • UK pickups account for 18,258: the 2026 value is fixed at 44,926 and the fleet then ages on the pickup curve.
  • Portugal and Poland corrections agreed on 24 September are not yet in these figures (slide 9).
  • Registration data received since V19 for Israel and the UK is not yet used; Israel's government register shows about a quarter more trucks (slides 7–8).
Source: ISZE UIO workbook V19 (18 June 2026) and V20 draft (22 September 2026), sheet 1. UIO Overview.2

V19 switched to a 33-year vehicle life for the forecast; V20 keeps the realistic life for every year

OBSERVATIONS / QUESTIONS
  • In V19 the same market used two methods: a survival curve up to 2025, a flat 3% scrap rate from 2026. That seam is what made the forecast grow.
  • V20 uses one method from 1990 to 2030. There is no step at the 2025/2026 boundary: each row is rebuilt so that 2025 reproduces V19 exactly.
  • New registrations for 2026–2030 follow the sales plan one-to-one on the five-year level, with the annual profile smoothed.

OBSERVATIONS / QUESTIONS
  • The gap is between 3,259 and 3,721 trucks in every year shown since 2020. A steady gap points to a missing block of vehicles, not a scrap rate that is slightly off.
