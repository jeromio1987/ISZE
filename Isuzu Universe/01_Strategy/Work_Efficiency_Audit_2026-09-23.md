# Work efficiency audit — Isuzu Universe, 21–23 September 2026

Written for Jerome. Read-only measurement of the folder; this file is the only thing I created.

## Verdict

About **one word in ten** of the paper writing produced on 21 and 22 September is still in the
current documents. Six papers and **368 pages** were written under the main commercial line;
the main paper and its annex are **40 pages** of that. By body words the figure is **12,138 of
119,074, or 10.2%**; by pages, **40 of 368, or 10.9%**.

**Eight of the ten editions were replaced within 24 hours of being written**, and the middle of
the line is brutal: the 33-page tools edition was the newest edition for 1 hour 42 minutes, the
49-slide deck re-rendered a paper that was withdrawn 8.7 hours later, and one pair of papers was
produced two minutes apart with 90% of the same text in both.

Roughly a quarter of the writing did produce something that stuck: the scope boundary was in the
narrow edition and missing from the wide one, and finding that is what justified the current
paper. Two-thirds of the writing was replaced with no finding recorded against it. That is the
honest split, and it is set out below with the arithmetic.

One thing is worse than the rework, because it is not a judgement call. **The delivered Yellow
Paper v0.5 does not exist.** The file with that name is a distributor billing table, not a PDF.
The file that the folder's own index tells you to open, and the file the launcher opens, is wrong.
The completion record for that build describes a 612 KB PDF and claims 23 screenshots taken and
23 read. Whoever rebuilt it destroyed the deliverable and kept the paperwork.

## 8. Rework that produced a finding, and rework that produced nothing

This is the part that matters, so I will state how I drew the line.

**A rework counts as productive only if all three are true:** a specific gap or defect can be
pointed at in the earlier document; a later document states the correction; and the correction is
still in force in the current documents. Everything else is unproductive, even where the writing is
good and the thinking is sound. I drew the line this way because it is the only test that does not
give credit for effort.

### Rework that produced a finding

**The scope boundary gap — the big one.** The 33-page tools edition states the boundary explicitly:
one heading on the subject, a full table of what sits outside the tools against what sits inside,
and the sentence "Basic service is not the product." I searched the 80-page vision edition for the
same material. **Zero occurrences of the phrase, zero of "what Universe is not", and the table is
not there.** The 25-page paper carries the boundary as its own section, with seven mentions. This is
a real, checkable gap in a document that was 80 pages long, and finding it is what the final rewrite
was for. **This alone justifies the last cycle.**

**The portfolio divergence.** The same day carried two incompatible product ranges: the 33-page
edition's five tools (Atlas, IDS Development Studio, Range & Campaign Planner, Pricing & Margin Lab,
PRISM Market Intelligence) against an 80-page edition that never names the range planner and names
the pricing lab once. The 25-page paper adopts one portfolio, naming the planner 12 times. Also real.

**The Yellow Paper red-team review.** A 117,598-byte adversarial review of v0.2, written the same
morning, that the v0.5 source answers. v0.5 carries a go/no-go test and a stop condition that v0.2
did not have. That is a specific correction with a stated source.

**The engineering verification finding.** Two safety checks were interrogating a module the build
does not use. They were repointed at the module that decides what a distributor receives, and a new
check was added so a check cannot silently load an unbuilt module again. Specific, documented,
closed.

### Rework that produced nothing

**The 21 September pair.** A 58-page paper and a 71-page visual edition, two minutes apart, 90% the
same text, both replaced within two hours. One full render and read cycle for 1,200 words of
difference.

**The 46-page commercial revision.** Written 06:31 on 22 September, replaced the same day, and only
**0.4%** of its wording appears in anything current. 15,720 words.

**The 80-page vision edition itself.** 17,532 words, current for 7.7 hours, and **88% of its words
survive in no current document**. This is the borderline case and I will not pretend it is clean: its
real value was as a comparison object that exposed the boundary gap, not as a document. But as a
document it returned almost nothing, and the 13-page campaign chapter inside it returned nothing at
all.

**The 49-slide commercial vision deck.** 13,480 words, zero charts, zero images, 86% of its notes
copied from a paper that was withdrawn 8.7 hours after the deck was written.

**The tools presentation.** 28 slides, zero charts, zero images, 4,029 words.

**The Yellow Paper v0.5 delivery.** A 282,128-byte file named as a PDF that contains 1,505 rows of
distributor billing data. The completion record beside it describes a 612,197-byte PDF and records
23 screenshots taken and 23 read. **The deliverable does not exist and the record of it does.** I did
not read or reproduce the billing rows; I am reporting what the file is, not what is in it. This one
is not a judgement call about value — it is a missing deliverable.

**Eight orphaned pages, 2,769,289 bytes**, with no launcher and no mention in any current index.

**Eight redundant file copies, 16,223,640 bytes**, of which 11,871,168 is two extra copies of the
same 5.9 MB pricing page and 569,985 is a duplicate showcase page.

**1,281,274 bytes of supplied packages** never applied.

                                                          40 / 368 = 10.9%
```

Stated the other way: **368 pages of paper were produced to arrive at 40 pages.**

**2. Eight of ten editions were replaced within 24 hours, and the median edition lasted 7.6 hours.**

  0.03, 1.70, 1.84, 7.57, 7.66, 8.71, 13.79, 16.18   -> median 7.6 h

```

**3. Fourteen per cent of the produced bytes are named by a current index.**

```
deliverables at root + 01_Strategy              36,659,620 bytes
named by the current folder index                5,400,263 bytes
                                             5,400,263 / 36,659,620 = 14.7%
```

The 5,400,263 bytes are the 25 files the folder index names by name. That count includes the ones
it names in order to say they are withdrawn: the 80-page vision edition (286,008), the 33-page tools
edition (118,020), the superseded showcase (648,021) and the billing file misnamed as a PDF
(282,128). Taking only the files the index treats as current, the figure is **4,066,086 bytes, or
11.1%** — which lands on the same answer as the words and the pages.

Note the difference between figure 1 and figure 3 and why I show both. By words and pages the
survival rate is about 10%. By raw bytes it is 14.7%, because the two surviving papers are the
image-heavy ones — 1.6 MB and 0.6 MB — while the withdrawn papers were mostly text. **Bytes flatter
the result; words and pages tell the truth.**

One further number, because it is the one that would cost money: **the median edition lived 7.6
hours, and the shortest lived 2 minutes.** A paper that is current for two hours is a full
authoring, review and render cycle that returned nothing except a comparison. There were two of
those.

## 10. Where the brief was wrong

I checked each claim rather than confirming it. Six did not survive measurement.

1. **The supersession chain is not one chain.** It is two lines — the commercial line (7 editions)
   and the Yellow Paper line (3) — plus a 46-page revision that is a third, separate document. The
   brief presents them as a single sequence.

2. **The visual edition is not a successor.** `ISUZU_UNIVERSE_WHITE_PAPER_VISUAL_EDITION_2026-09-21.pdf`
   is stamped 19:56:14 and the plain white paper 19:58:15. The "visual edition" is the **older** of
   the two, by two minutes. It is a sibling, not a later edition.

3. **The 46-page revision is not duplicated by the 80-pager.** Only 0.4% of the 80-pager's text
   appears in the 46-page revision. There is no duplicated material between them to speak of.

4. **There are not two `BOOK_B_REVISION_REVIEW_2026-09-22.html` files.** There is one. Three reviews
   of that price work exist, but under three different names with three different contents.

5. **"Thousands of screenshots" in `_qa` undercounts nothing and overstates the pictures.** There
   are 566 image files (538 PNG, 28 JPG). The thousands of files are Chrome browser profile data.

6. **"None of the three supplied packages is applied" is wrong.** The continuation patch is applied
   and committed to version control. Two are unused, one is in use.

Also worth recording, because it is a real fault and not a matter of interpretation:
**`Universe_Yellow_Paper_v0.5_2026-09-22.pdf` is not a PDF.** It is 282,128 bytes of
comma-separated distributor billing data with a PDF filename. The folder index points at it as the
product paper, the launcher opens it, and the completion record beside it describes a different file
that no longer exists.

## What I measured and what I did not

Measured directly: every file size, date and page count above; every word count, after stripping
repeated running heads and page numbers; every overlap figure, using six-word sequences and, where
stated, whole-sentence word overlap; every chart, image and table count inside each deck; every
launcher, by reading it and resolving its target; every duplicate, by hashing the file contents, not
by comparing sizes.

Not measured, and said so rather than described: the number of hours any person actually worked; the
cost of any hour; who wrote which file; and whether a withdrawn document was read by anyone before
it was replaced. Those need time records the folder does not contain. Everything else in this report
has a number or a named file behind it.
