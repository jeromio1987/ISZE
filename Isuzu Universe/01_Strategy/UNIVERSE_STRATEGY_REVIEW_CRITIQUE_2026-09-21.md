**Date:** 21 September 2026  
**Paper:** `Isuzu_Universe_Strategy_Paper_Reviewed_v2.pdf`  
**Purpose:** Internal notes for Jerome. What holds, what undersells the data we already have, what to change before the next edition.  
**Direction locked in this note (Jerome, 21 Sep 2026, two passes):** ITUK is the pilot; distributors first, dealers later; one product that joins IDS, AIM and PRISM; competition price references inside if permissions allow; **tenders inside Universe** (not a side tool); **named-dealer sales to dealer** as the AIM ask (geographic weak spots); IDS feedback packs traded for **Kaizen progress**; PRISM plus **ITUK DLOD** (Don’t Lose Deal — not “Deal of the Day”); aftersales data as the closest dealer connection, with monetisation still open; odometer from warranty treated with hard caution.

---

## Verdict

The paper’s direction is sound: one integrated service, funded by ISZE, that earns data by returning a better monthly review — not another reporting obligation. The strongest parts are the participation model, the split between an analytical extract and an official service book, and the refusal to invent a profit from a modelled gap.

Where it needs a rewrite for *our* reality is not the legal caution. It is three commercial choices:

1. **Pilot market.** Lead with **ITUK**. That is the only distributor where we hold named-dealer sales onward for calendar 2025, and Jerome already holds more ITUK detail than for a typical distributor. The paper’s own inventory supports that choice; it then softens it with “investigate” language. Harden it.
2. **Audience sequence.** Get **distributors on board first**. Dealer workshop extracts come later, after the distributor review is useful without them. The paper already separates value-before-upload for dealers; it should say explicitly that phase 1 is a distributor product.
3. **What Universe is on day one.** Merge **IDS + AIM + PRISM** (and the network map) into one experience with the same distributor still selected. Competition aftersales prices belong in that same view **if** the collection and sharing basis for each source is clear. **Tenders belong in the same product** as an opportunity / alarm panel — not a separate habit.
4. **Every module needs a hard give / ask.** Soft vision without an exchange will not get distributors to send named-dealer files or Kaizen progress. The exchange table below is the commercial spine the next paper edition is missing.

Odometer and “observed mileage” must be demoted from a near-term analytical pillar. Warranty-linked readings are a biased sample. Duty cycles differ wildly. Do not build return intervals or fleet rates on that until the bias is stated on every chart.

## Jerome’s operating choices (bind the next edition)

### 1. Pilot = ITUK

**Decision:** Run the first real Universe pilot with **ITUK**.

**Why this is the right call for us, even with IDS limits:**

- ITUK is the only market with **dealer × part number × period** sales onward for CY2025 (~101k lines, 64 dealers).
- Jerome already works that network with more depth than a “median” distributor relationship (SPA, parts discussions, existing files). The paper should say that openly: the pilot is not a random sample of Europe; it is the market where ISZE can already hold a serious conversation.
- ITUK’s public maintenance / R&M offering is a later *conversation* about a data route — not a feed we already have. The paper already says that; keep it.

**What the next edition must still print next to “ITUK pilot” (so nobody is surprised):**

- IDS for UK trucks is **totals-only** in the supplied HTML path the paper cites; question-level dealer workbooks are **not** assumed for ITUK the way they exist for IUK (45 dealer workbooks) and many mainland markets.
- Dealer **names** in the sales file and in the IDS geography list do not join cleanly today (roughly: ~16 exact matches after normalisation, ~34 loose overlaps, ~20 no plausible match — from the 20 Sep inventory). A “connected dealer review” for ITUK must either (a) ship with an explicit name map Jerome and ITUK approve, or (b) show parts and standards as **two selections** until that map exists. Do not hide the join problem.
- ITUK sales-to-dealer euro on the valued files is reference-priced from ISZE averages where needed; source lines are often quantity-only. Label valuation basis on every chart.
- UTI dealer euros exist as a **network aggregate** in the valued 2025 file; UMI has no dealer-level sales-to-dealer in that set. Do not blend Israel into an ITUK story.

**Do not** switch the pilot to Ireland/Portugal in the next edition unless Jerome changes this decision. Those markets remain good *examples* of national sales-to-dealer without named dealers; they are not the pilot sponsor market.

### 2. Distributors first, dealers later

**Phase 1 — distributor product**

- One login / one review for the distributor: standards (IDS), parts families (AIM), validated calculations and GPS/PMS-style views (PRISM reuse), map/navigation, actions.
- Value to the distributor: a better monthly dealer discussion **without** requiring workshop extracts from every site.
- Data exchange at this phase: what we already have rights to (ISZE billing, agreed sales-to-dealer files, IDS assessments, classification) plus any national maintenance reporting the distributor **already** receives — discussed, not assumed exported to ISZE.

**Phase 2 — dealers**

- Only after distributors use the review and ask for the “why did purchases fall” half that needs workshop records.
- Same progressive contribution rules the paper proposes (value before detailed upload). Keep them; move them out of the critical path for go-live with distributors.

The paper already argues against a circular “upload before you see value” trap for dealers. The critique is that it still centres the **maintenance partnership** as a condition of the initial pilot. For Jerome’s sequence, the initial pilot proves the **distributor monthly review**. Maintenance data is a named phase-2 workstream, still in the strategy, not a day-one gate.

### 3. Merge IDS + AIM + PRISM (and the atlas) — with a real give / ask per module

**Destination product (plain labels):** My network · Parts · Market references · Tenders · Actions · (Service activity later).

The paper talks about integration. Jerome’s sharper frame is an **exchange**: we give a tool that makes the distributor better at its job; we ask for the data that makes ISZE better at supporting that job. Without that table, Universe is a dashboard pitch.

| Module | We give the distributor | We ask in return | Why the ask is fair | Sharp risk if we get this wrong |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **AIM** | Parts families, Genuine vs Best Value view, contested/range context, a common language for the monthly parts discussion | **Sales to dealer by named dealer** (and part number / period) — not only a national total | Named dealers let us see **geographic weak spots** on the map (a dealer cluster buying nothing in a family while neighbours buy). National totals cannot show that. | Asking for named dealers without returning a map + family view they could not build themselves = “more reporting”. Giving AIM without the ask = we stay blind below the distributor. |
| **IDS** | The dealer standards audit / comparison already built (country packs, feedback sheets, workshop packs) — a tool they can use with their network | That Kaizen / improvement work is **recorded in Universe** (owner, due date, status, evidence) so progress is visible, not trapped in a local spreadsheet | We already invested in IDS. Empty Kaizen registers (owner blank, all “Open”) prove the gap. Incorporating Kaizen closes the loop: assessment → action → result. | Treating IDS scores as performance weapons kills trust. Asking for Kaizen detail before the packs are used in real reviews feels like surveillance. |
| **PRISM** | Validated aftersales calculations the distributor can rely on in one place: billing vs expected demand, mix, replenishment signals, GPS-style views — **plus, for ITUK, DLOD** (see below) | More **real aftersales information** flowing regularly (sales to dealer quality, later workshop where agreed) — this is our **closest connection toward the dealer**, even while phase 1 stays distributor-facing | PRISM without a data return is charity software. The commercial return is better targeting (SPA, DLOD, range, stock support), not a fee for “data”. | Over-claiming “PRISM is a customer platform” before access control exists. Shipping all-network HTML behind a login theatre. |

| **Tenders** | Country-relevant open opportunities / alarms with as-of date | Feedback when a tender is won, lost, or ignored (lightweight) — otherwise the alarm is noise | Aftersales footprint on public demand; early warning for fleets and bodybuilders | Letting tender count become the hero while the monthly parts review stays weak. Counting notices as pipeline €. |

**Competition aftersales prices (OEM peer / shop references):**

- Belong **inside** Universe as “Market references”, not as a side HTML someone has to remember.
- Conditions (paper is already close; make them operational):
  - Each observation: seller, page, date, currency, tax basis, pack size, known delivery/core charges.
  - Label as **observed advertised price**, not confirmed transaction price.
  - Product match: confirmed / seller-claimed / unresolved — never silent fitment approval.
  - Collection mode documented per source (curated link vs stored history). EU database rules and source licences still need a short legal check per feed; this is not a blanket ban and not a blanket “public = free to republish”.
  - Competition law: monitoring and reporting ≠ fixing resale prices. Universe supports “what evidence would justify support through this distributor?”, not “what must every dealer charge?”.
  - Vintage must stay visible (e.g. PartsPool/Autodoc scrape date ≠ Eucon UK 2023 workshop GBP). Thin coverage of the billed book is normal; never invent independent-garage **volume** from a price scrape.

If legal/commercial review blocks a specific source, drop that source — keep the module shell. Do not drop the whole merge because one scrape is contested.

### 3b. AIM ask — named dealers and geographic weak spots

Jerome’s ask is specific: sales to dealer **including which dealer**.

That is the difference between “ITUK bought fewer oil filters” and “three dealers in the North bought none while the Midlands held”. The map only becomes commercially useful when the part family and the dealer pin share a key.

**Pilot implication for ITUK:** we already hold CY2025 named-dealer quantities. Universe should **show** that geographic picture first (prove the value of the format), then use it as the template when asking other distributors: “this is what you get when the file has dealer names.”

**Other distributors:** start accepting national totals if that is all they can extract, but the **participation level** that unlocks the map weak-spot view requires named dealers (or a stable dealer code). Do not pretend a national file paints dealer geography.

### 3c. IDS ask — Kaizen inside Universe

We already gave distributors a serious IDS audit surface (dealer compare, country packs, per-dealer feedback / workshop packs). The missing half is **incorporation into their Kaizen activities**.

What we need visible (minimum):

- Which dealer / which gap / who owns it / due date / status / last evidence date.
- Link back to the IDS question or section that triggered the action.
- A progress view for the distributor (and a summarised view for ISZE).

What we must not do:

- Score dealers on “Kaizen compliance” as a league table.
- Treat a blank register as proof the network is inactive — today several registers are scaffolds with empty owners.
- Demand dealer-level Kaizen detail from markets where IDS is totals-only (ITUK trucks) without admitting that the question detail is thin.

For the ITUK pilot: Kaizen may start thinner (fewer question-level hooks). Still useful if actions from the **parts** review (AIM + sales to dealer) are logged the same way — Kaizen is the action discipline, not only the IDS questionnaire.

### 3d. PRISM + ITUK DLOD (Don’t Lose Deal)

**Name check (mail, 21 Sep 2026):** This is **DLOD**, not “Deal of the Day”. Glossary and live mail call it **Don’t Lose Deal / Don’t Lose the Deal** — ITUK’s programme for competitive quotes / defending the job when the dealer faces an alternative. Billing text even carries rebate lines labelled `DLOD` / `DLOD 2023 Q3+Q4`. DocuSign and MOU traffic (Mar–Apr 2026) shows **FY26 ITUK DLOD** as a formal incentive: MOU, rebate credit note, Japan-side sign-off (Yoshimoto on the DocuSign reminder chain), ISZE credit control chasing the signed MOU. One FY26 MOU mail states a **€30k** DLOD incentive (budget of the prior FY, CN already created).

So in Universe / PRISM for ITUK, DLOD is not a slogan. It is:

- a **commercial defence tool** (support so the dealer does not lose the job to a cheaper alternative),
- a **documented incentive** (MOU + rebate),
- and a **data opportunity**: which parts, which dealers, which competitor quotes, how often support is used, and whether sales to dealer moved afterwards.

**Give:** PRISM views that make expected demand, mix, and (where we have it) competitive price context usable in the same review where a DLOD case is discussed.  
**Ask:** structured DLOD case inputs over time (part, dealer, competitor evidence, outcome) — not only the annual MOU. Without case-level feedback, DLOD stays a rebate black box.

**Sharp caution:** DLOD spend is not proof of incremental profit. A credit note proves we paid support; it does not prove we kept the customer or grew Genuine share. Universe should force the **before / after** view on the affected dealers and families, or refuse to call DLOD “successful” from rebate volume alone.

### 4. Tenders — inside Universe, not beside it

**Decision (Jerome):** add tenders to Isuzu Universe.

Not as the product hero. As a **standing panel**: open notices relevant to the distributor’s country / segment, with as-of date, link, and a simple status the distributor can set (watching / bidding / won / lost / not for us).

Why it belongs:

- Same audience (distributor aftersales / commercial).
- Same habit (open Universe for the monthly review → also see what public demand appeared).
- Reinforces that ISZE is useful beyond parts billing.

Why it must stay disciplined:

- Tender **count** is not aftersales €.
- Many notices are vehicles / chassis, not parts — label the aftersales angle honestly.
- Do not require tender feedback as a condition of AIM/IDS access.

Phase for ITUK pilot: show UK-relevant notices; do not block the pilot on tender UX perfection.

### 5. Monetising “more real aftersales info” — push this hard

Jerome’s instinct is right that richer aftersales information (especially anything that gets us closer to the dealer) is strategically important, and that how to monetise it is not settled.

**Pushback (do not soft-pedal this in the next paper):**

1. **Do not sell “data access” as a product to distributors.** They will smell a tollbooth. Universe is funded by ISZE; the return is better commercial decisions.
2. **The money is in the actions the data enables**, in rough priority:
   - Better **SPA / DLOD** targeting (support where a real competitor quote and a real dealer exist — not blanket discount).
   - Better **Genuine vs BVP** placement (stop substituting inside our own book by accident; win jobs that were going outside).
   - **Geographic weak spots** → focused sales effort, stock positioning, training.
   - **Kaizen closure** → fewer repeat IDS failures, measurable network improvement.
   - **Tender** conversion where parts / R&M content is real.
3. **Closest connection to the dealer is an asset, not a consulting invoice.** Phase 1 still goes through the distributor. The strategic prize is that ISZE stops being only a wholesaler of part numbers and becomes the party that sees network aftersales reality early enough to act. That is worth more than a data fee — and harder to copy.
4. **Beware false monetisation metrics:** uploaded row counts, number of logins, reference-price “valuation” of workshop lines, DLOD rebate €, tender notice counts. None of these are incremental gross profit.
5. **A future option (not phase 1):** manufacturer-sponsored service documentation or fleet TCO packs that a leasing/fleet buyer would pay for — only with legal/technical owners, separate from the analytical pilot (paper already warns this). Do not pretend that path funds Universe next quarter.
6. **Internal honesty:** if after two pilot quarters we cannot name one SPA/DLOD/range decision that changed because of Universe, the product failed — regardless of how pretty the merge looks.

### 6. Odometer — be very cautious (hard rewrite)

The paper treats repeated odometer readings as a usable path to “observed distance over elapsed time” and later return analysis. That language is too confident for our sources.

**Why caution is mandatory:**

- **Selection bias.** Only a low share of vehicles appear in warranty / claim-linked odometer trails. Vehicles that claim may run differently from the silent majority (age, duty, operator behaviour, region).
- **Duty cycle.** A refuse truck in Madeira is not a trunk-haul between London and Birmingham. One “km/year” for a weight class hides that.
- **Derivation traps.** Some spreadsheet columns labelled “annual mileage” are unsafe (odometer ÷ shrinking age → latest reading ≈ clock). Only use rates built from **odometer deltas over elapsed time**, with unit checks, and still label the sample.
- **What we already hold is provisional and segment-specific** (examples on disk / prior notes): IUK N35 Grafter ~14.5k km/yr from a large interval study; UTI N75 ~51.7k km/yr from CV warranty odometer; broader N35/N75/F-series warranty-odometer figures remain provisional pending duplicate-sheet checks. Mainland LCV mileage files exist for some markets and are incomplete for others (IUK absent from one IMIT service extract). None of this is a Europe-wide maintenance denominator.

**Rules for the next edition and for any Universe screen:**

1. Do **not** present warranty-linked mileage as “the fleet”.
2. Every mileage figure states **sample, source, vehicle class, and what is excluded**.
3. Do **not** replace approved service intervals with observed visit gaps.
4. Do **not** make odometer a phase-1 pilot dependency for ITUK.
5. Prefer **parts + sales-to-dealer + IDS + expected demand from the service schedule (modelled, labelled)** for the first ITUK reviews. Bring mileage in only as a sensitivity or a named research cut.

The paper’s instinct (“measure only vehicles that had an opportunity to return”) is still right for visit linkage. It does not rescue a biased odometer sample.

## Where the paper undersells what we already have

These are the main gaps between GPT’s supplied pack and the wider workspace. The next edition should acknowledge them so Universe does not look empty on day one.

1. **Distributor monthly review without workshop data.** For ITUK (and any DB with sales-to-dealer), Universe can already juxtapose: ISZE billing, sales to dealers, AIM family, GPS/PMS-style expected demand (modelled), IDS where questions exist. That is enough for “purchases fell — investigate” before any job-card extract.
2. **Billing vs sales-to-dealer signal.** Replenishment / sell-through style comparisons already exist for CY2025 (derived). Missing stock does not erase that signal; it limits how far you can explain it.
3. **Ireland backorders.** Real dated open-order lines exist in archive and are unwired. Out of scope for an ITUK pilot demo, but the strategy should not say “no fulfilment history anywhere”.
4. **Portugal / others.** National sales-to-dealer files exist beyond ITUK. Useful for network coverage metrics; not a reason to move the pilot.
5. **Sales-to-dealer coverage of the GPS billed book.** Rough order of magnitude used internally: five distributors historically ~54% of the €30.8M 2025 GPS billed book in the 18 measured markets; Portugal lifts that; Germany and Belgium files would lift further toward ~75% of that book — those two files are still an ask, not a landed long file in the derived set as of this critique. Always print the % **and** the file list.
6. **AIM is not one settled “approved” list.** Live pages still read one classification file; a corrected dated rebuild (20 Sep 2026) exists because catalogue columns on the master had drifted (~2.8k part numbers). Air-cleaner elements were misfiled on the master side and corrected in the rebuild path. Universe must pin **filename + build date**. Saying “approved classification” without that is ahead of the facts. The contested-per-market commercial layer beside the tree is still incomplete as scored columns.
7. **DLOD / rebate mechanics already exist for ITUK.** Universe should absorb the review of supported deals, not invent a parallel “competitive quote” app.
8. **IDS feedback packs already exist.** The ask is Kaizen discipline on top, not another audit product.

## Challenge list — for this critique, and for GPT on the next pass

Ask GPT (and ourselves) to attack these, not summarise them. Prefer “this fails because…” over “consider also…”.

1. **Is the give strong enough?** If ITUK already has sales-to-dealer files and IDS packs, what does Universe add that Duncan cannot get from email + Excel today? If the answer is only “one screen”, the product is thin.
2. **Is the ask too heavy for phase 1?** Named dealers + Kaizen owners + DLOD case outcomes + tender feedback in one pilot may kill adoption. What is the single non-negotiable ask for ITUK month one?
3. **Geographic weak spots without dealer fleet:** ranking dealers by parts € without trucks-in-service at dealer level can mis-label a small parc as “weak”. How do we stop false accusations on the map?
4. **DLOD conflict:** does publishing competitive quote context inside Universe create pressure toward resale-price outcomes or dealer margin visibility we promised not to collect?
5. **Kaizen surveillance:** will distributors refuse if ISZE can see open actions and overdue owners? What access boundary keeps Kaizen a help, not a scorecard for Japan?
6. **Monetisation honesty:** name one cash mechanism that does *not* require a new discount. If every path is “more SPA/DLOD”, Universe becomes a discount machine with better charts.
7. **Tenders dilution:** if the tender panel is vivid and the parts review is slow, users will live in tenders. How do we keep the monthly dealer review as the home screen?
8. **Dealer phase timing:** “distributors first” can become “dealers never”. What evidence forces the move to workshop extracts — and what evidence says stop?
9. **AIM authority:** can we promise AIM value while classification files still disagree? Or must Universe pin one dated file before any DB demo?
10. **Closest-to-dealer claim:** if phase 1 never touches the dealer, is “closest connection to the dealer” marketing? What is the earliest dealer-visible artefact that does not break the distributor relationship?

Each answer should change a sentence in the strategy — or be rejected with a reason. Soft agreement is a failed review.
