## 1. What the UK programme actually is

### 1.1 Mechanism

A customer is at the dealer with competitive price pressure (independent aftermarket, used parts, internet price, or a rival quote). The dealer cannot win the job at normal dealer net (DN). ITUK cuts an **offer to dealer** below normal DN. The gap — Normal DN minus Offer to dealer, times quantity — is **ITUK support**. ISZE has been refunding ITUK part of that programme cost through **credit notes / rebate material**, typically in round **€30,000** documents tagged DLOD or DLAD in the order reference.

This is a **defence at the counter**, not a catalogue price change and not a standing SPA on a part family. SPA changes the list or the commercial terms for a defined basket. Don't Lose the Deal is case-by-case when a job is about to walk.

### 1.2 Who funds what

| ITUK | Funds the DN-to-offer gap; logs the case in the regional / fleet deal file | Overall Summary £54,922 Total ITUK Support — Measured |
| ISZE | Pays ITUK via CR rebate documents (~€30k) | Order / billing extracts; FY27 budget; VPACC row; Pricing P&L reserve — Measured / Confirmed |

**Important money gap (Inferred):** Duncan's register shows **£54,922** of ITUK support in the workbook period (~€64k at a 1.17 FX assumption — FX rate Proposed for conversion only). ISZE's documented refund is **€30,000** per major credit. So ISZE is **co-funding**, not paying 100% of every deal line. A network design that assumes ISZE pays the whole gap will overstate ISZE cash and understate distributor skin in the game.

## 4. Return focus — what it could bring back

The business question is not "can we afford 0.5%?" It is "what does the 0.5% buy?"

### 4.1 Retained jobs (primary return)

**Measured intent** in comments: stop second-hand / internet / walk-away. Each supported line is a bet that the **workshop job and its parts basket** stay with the dealer.  
**Unknown today:** win rate. Competitor Price is blank on 217/218 regional lines, and there is no systematic won/lost flag tied back to an order reference in SAP.

**Proposed measurement:** for every case, require (a) competing offer price or documented reason code (internet / used / fleet tender), (b) outcome won/lost/unknown, (c) ITUK or dealer invoice reference. Then count won jobs × parts euro on the order — that is the return numerator.

### 4.2 Parts-family retention vs independent aftermarket

Deal-file mix shows defence on **injectors, aftertreatment, driveline, turbos, DEF, sensors** — exactly the families where a lost job trains the customer to buy elsewhere next time.  
**Inferred return:** keeping one turbo or DPF job protects follow-on service euros, not only the supported line. Quantifying that multiplier needs sales-to-dealer before/after by dealer × family — available for ITUK (and a short list of other DBs); not for the whole book.

### 4.3 Aftersales loyalty

Don't Lose the Deal is visible to the dealer principal as "ISZE/ITUK helped me keep Mrs X's truck". SPA and list revisions are invisible at that moment.  
**Unknown:** loyalty score lift. Soft return; do not put a fake euro on it. Track dealer repeat use and whether top users' genuine mix holds (ITUK sales-to-dealer).

### 4.4 Interaction with SPA and price-list revision

| Tool | Job | Conflict risk |
|---|---|---|
| SPA | Structural commercial deal on a basket / fleet | If SPA already covers the part, Don't Lose the Deal double-pays — need exclusion |
| Price list revision | Resets %GM / list for the book | Wrong if used as permanent substitute for list discipline |
| Don't Lose the Deal | Exception when a live competitor quote would lose the job | Right when rare and evidenced; wrong when it becomes the normal DN |

Suzuki's 8 Oct 2026 mail on the Yoshimoto / ITUK PMS promotion thread frames brake competition as "**different from the usual SPA approach**" and as ISZE price strategy. *Measured* (mailbox). That is the same strategic neighbourhood as Don't Lose the Deal, even though that mail does not name DLOD. **Proposed:** any Japan pack separates (1) structured PMS/SPA on brakes and (2) Don't Lose the Deal case fund — one story, two instruments.

### 4.5 Margin leak / gaming risks

| Risk | Evidence | Control |
|---|---|---|
| Asserted competitor, no proof | 217/218 competitor prices empty | Require photo/PDF or typed competitor price before support > threshold |
| Concentration on friendly dealers | Top 3 dealers take a large £ share | Cap per dealer per FY |
| Relabelling normal discount as DLOD | Comments include generic "dealer support" | Approval rule: only with customer + competitive reason code |
| Double dip with SPA | James Marsh mail confusion on DLAD vs NOx SPA (archive) | Exclusion list: open SPA PNs out of scope |
| FX / round-euro credits | £ support vs € credits | Settle in EUR envelope; distributor carries local FX |

### 4.6 Before / after design (sales to dealer where available)

**Proposed** evaluation stack:

1. Case register (Duncan template) — operational.  
2. Link case → credit note / monthly DLOD claim pack → ISZE CR.  
3. For markets with sales-to-dealer files (IUK, ITUK, Ireland, UTI, Greece): monthly dealer × family euros **90 days before / after** won cases — descriptive, not causal proof in year one.  
4. Do not use piece counts as the KPI (gasket ≠ cab). Euros and family only.

**Unknown until year two:** true incremental retention vs jobs that would have been won anyway.
