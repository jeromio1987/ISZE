### Part A — Purpose and definition

**A1. The proposition.**
What Universe is: the distributor's connected aftersales review — one place where parts purchasing,
dealer self-assessment and market context sit beside a recorded action. State plainly that the purpose
is not to put every dataset on one screen; it is to make the relationship between a claim, its evidence
and the next decision explicit.

Then say what Universe is **not**, in the same chapter: not an ERP replacement, not an automated
price-setting system, not a distributor-audit tool. Keep the scope boundary that is already decided: the
portfolio overview and the Genuine/BVP comparison stay; the sales cube stays **removed** (removed, not
deferred for reintroduction); product photography stays deferred. Do not reopen these by implication.

**A2. The three pillars and the possible fourth.**
AIM, IDS and PRISM, each with what it holds and the question it answers. Tender support is a **possible
extension, not an approved fourth pillar** — say that in those words. State which are working names.

**A3. The four commercial outcomes.**
Sales explanation, pricing precision, market comparison, service-range opportunity. Each gets what the
user receives, in one or two sentences. The fourth must be concrete: an applicable part, kit or
documented alternative worth discussing, with a reason it may improve the offer — absence of a purchase
alone is not an opportunity.

**A4. What success would look like, and what is not claimed.**
A distributor opens a monthly review, understands a purchasing change, examines the actual parts,
discusses a response and records the outcome. Then the honest boundary: revenue uplift, time saved and
improved capture are **outcomes to test**, not established benefits of anything built so far.

> The line to keep, in v0.1's own words: *"Universe should make a decision easier to defend, not make
> incomplete evidence look complete."*

### Part B — The operating model

**B1. The shared operating loop.**
Observe, explain, act, review. One population, one reporting clock and one transaction flow per
question. The loop is not navigation between three dashboards; it is preservation of the same question
and evidence as the user moves between them.

**B2. The four destinations and the two views.**
Portfolio, network, commercial review, actions. Then the distinction that must not blur: the
**distributor view** is a commercial workspace (purchasing, covered sales-to-dealer activity, product
mix, market context, agreed actions); the **dealer view** shows only the commercial records legitimately
assigned to that dealer or site, with the source identity and relationship status shown. An IDS summary
in a distributor view is labelled as results from a defined dealer self-assessment population — never as
a distributor audit score.

**B3. The decision patterns.**
Five worked decision classes, each with what is examined and what action is permitted, and each with the
sentence that limits it:

1. a braking decline that can be explained — separate quantity change from price or mix effect, test
   supply, fitment, supersession, promotion and competitor evidence; the action is a targeted
   part-level investigation, **not** a claim that the market is losing brake business;
2. a BVP alternative that is actually applicable — fitment, function, contents and commercial units
   checked; a shared family label is insufficient; **no validated match means no substitute
   recommendation**;
3. a gap between the two purchasing flows — compare like periods, separate returns, valuation
   differences and missing channels; inventory and timing are possible explanations, not measured facts
   without stock information;
4. a dealer-support conversation informed by IDS — validate the dealer/site link, the assessment date
   and the commercial period first; the association does not prove the self-assessed weakness caused the
   commercial result;
5. a stronger SPA or tender-support case — actual historical purchasing, applicable alternatives, dated
   market evidence and explicit volume assumptions, on the correct cost and price basis. A lower gross
   margin percentage can coexist with higher gross profit; the calculation must show it.

Close with the selection principle: prioritise where the signal is material, the evidence is sufficient
and someone can act. **A data gap creates a data-quality task; it is not disguised as a sales
opportunity to fill a top-five list.**

**B4. The standard for every finding.**
A finding is a small, reproducible evidence packet. List its elements and require each: scope and
coverage; monthly evidence with missing months visible; part-level contribution that reconciles to the
selected total with an "other parts" remainder; documented product comparison; interpretation (what is
observed, which explanations are supported, which remain plausible, what would change the conclusion);
action and review with a named owner or accountable role, review date and expected observable outcome.

Then the chart rules: a claimed decline is shown as a monthly time series, never two isolated totals; **do
not draw a continuous line through an unreported month**; a reported zero is different from a missing
observation; do not aggregate pieces, kits and sets without a validated conversion; state whether the
comparison uses a constant part population or includes new and discontinued products; if a common
reference valuation is used, disclose the pricebook date and the priced population; **the chart title
must not call a reference valuation sales revenue.**

**B5. Confidence as a decision status, not a score.**
Use *ready for review, needs validation, context only, withheld* — with the reason shown. **Avoid a
single confidence score whose ingredients are opaque.** Coverage, identity matching, applicability,
price comparability and period alignment are separate checks. A report with no validated BVP match says
so. A report with no dealer-granular feed stops at its supported level. Close with the reproducibility
test: another reviewer must obtain the same total from the same accepted data and metric version, and the
filters, source references and calculation definition are exported alongside the presentation.

**B6. The response obligation — restore this chapter.**
This is the chapter v0.1 lost and the one the €300,000 depends on. It must state, as obligations:

- the **five response classes** the paper itself already lists (routine information, an application
  check, a commercial decision, a supply investigation, a fuller service analysis);
- that **an acknowledgement is not a substantive answer**, and "awaiting technical review" must name the
  next review arrangement and the information still needed;
- that **the paper does not invent response-time targets** — targets are agreed after the existing
  workload is measured, and the measurement design is the deliverable, not an unearned number;
- that the receiving organisation must have **the capacity and the authority to answer**, and that
  capacity is unbudgeted today;
- that there is **no support-case register at case level** anywhere on disk, and only a role name where
  an owner should be.

State the mechanism plainly, because it explains why this fails by default: software is cheap at the
margin and a person's time is not. Data collection is the half that can be specified, budgeted and built
by a project; the answer is the half that needs a named person with hours in the week, and it has no home
in a project budget. Left alone, the cheap half gets built and the expensive half is deferred.

Carry the two verifying facts and do not soften them: the words "Don't Lose the Deal" return **0 hits
across every column of all 538,346 billing rows**, and a mileage question to the ITUK Parts Director was
drafted on 18 September 2026 and **is still unsent**.

> Quote the paper's own two lines here, because they are the argument: *"A low marginal cost for software
> does not mean the response service is free."* and *"Software cannot substitute for an operating
> commitment... Otherwise, better data collection simply makes unanswered requests easier to count."*

### Part C — Evidence architecture

**C1. The transaction flows, and never merge them.**
ISZE→distributor is the distributor's purchasing from ISZE. Distributor→dealer is the dealer's purchasing
from that distributor. Dealer→end customer is a third, separate flow. None substitutes for another.
Missing coverage means unknown downstream activity, not zero activity. The same physical part may appear
in more than one flow; **these values must not be added together as a single market-sales total.**

**C2. The four layers, and why the fourth is empty.**
Layer 1, ISZE billed to the distributor; layer 2, distributor sold to dealers; layer 3, parts recorded in
workshop jobs; layer 4, dealer sold to the end customer. **Layer 4 has no source at all.** Layers 1–3
differ in population, currency and identity, and layer 1 has no dealer column. The four-layer comparison
was deliberately not reconciled and remains `not tested`. An account mapping establishes identity only:
it does not create dealer-level billing, does not establish stock, and is not proof of onward sale.

**C3. Evidence classes and permitted interpretation.**
Recorded quantity; recorded billing; reference-price valuation; modelled demand or potential; hypothesis
or candidate. For each, the permitted reading and the forbidden one. A calculated total inherits the
limitations of its inputs; **a precise euro value does not make a modelled result measured.** Missing
price coverage must be visible; unpriced parts must not silently become zero-valued parts.

**C4. Every comparison carries its scope.**
Persist the transaction flow, population, period, reporting clock, measure, currency, valuation basis,
source version and coverage state. Compare a partial month only with an equivalent cut-off. **Never add
calendar and fiscal views of the same activity.** Use the latest complete month and trailing
complete-month views where supported. If a dealer or channel enters the feed, show its effect separately
from like-for-like development.

Then the rule that governs everything downstream: **coverage precedes ranking.** Establish which
distributors, dealers, months, channels and part populations each source covers before comparing
performance. A declared population is not necessarily the whole market. **Where a reliable denominator
is unavailable, show what is covered rather than inventing a completeness percentage.**

> Keep the line: *"A result should become less certain when its evidence becomes weaker. It should not
> become more definite simply because it has been placed on a dashboard."*

**C5. Fleet, GPS, PMS and warranty — keep the lenses separate.**
GPS is the broad genuine-parts opportunity; PMS is the defined interval and competitive-parts scope.
Their definitions, part populations and denominators are registered separately, and a PMS-family result
is never relabelled as overall GPS performance. A useful purchasing-intensity lens is billing or a
common reference valuation per relevant vehicle — with the numerator, vehicle population, period and
valuation basis compatible. **This is not automatically market share.**

For modelled service demand, expose every assumption: the vehicles in service, age or usage profile
where supported, the service interval, quantity per event, applicability, the observation period and the
value basis. Present a range or a sensitivity where uncertainty materially affects the decision. **A
single exact-looking estimate must not hide a weak fleet or usage assumption.**

Vehicle attribution: the billing system has no VIN, chassis or model-level servicing evidence. Vehicle
attribution is permitted **only through validated exclusive part applicability**. Parts that fit
multiple bands or families remain pooled; they must not be allocated by fleet share to complete the
picture. Maintain three separate views: recorded exclusive-applicability purchasing, modelled expected
service demand by band, and market-level actual purchasing. A market actual repeated beside several
bands is non-additive and must not be summed across bands or divided by each band's expectation. **A
modelled allocation of a market gap is not measured leakage and must not become a dealer ranking.**

State the standing implementation rule where the band file is used: filter on the additive flag, treat
the exclusive-only column as the exclusive numerator and the also-fits column as non-additive, and keep
calendar and fiscal views separate. The corrected tonnage rules supersede the earlier ones.

Warranty: it records claims, defects and settlements and does **not** provide a complete record of
scheduled maintenance. Low warranty value is not evidence of a healthy aftermarket; missing maintenance
claims do not prove that dealers are failing to service vehicles. Warranty may contextualise a product
issue within its coverage; **it must not supply an invented service interval, a maintenance-capture rate
or a leakage conclusion.** Never derive a return interval from warranty, never use it as the fleet, and
never use it as the service population.

**C6. The trade-off nobody wrote down: counted, not modelled.**
This is the chapter v0.1 implies and never states. Write it out: a figure is either **counted** in a
source with a stated population, or **modelled** on assumptions, and the second can never be presented
in the clothing of the first. Every modelled number names its assumptions and carries a range or a
sensitivity. Every counted number names its coverage limit and its direction of bias — **counts and
observed euro are floors; rates and expectations are not.**

### Part D — Data and participation

**D1. The minimum data contract.**
The submission standard in full, as a specification:

- a monthly quantity feed with a declared population, submitted within an agreed number of working days
  after month-end, with a closed month distinguished from a provisional one;
- the preferred level of detail: distributor × dealer × calendar month × part number × unit of measure ×
  transaction type × channel. A more detailed feed may be accepted; invoice prices and end-customer
  information are not required;
- the required elements, each with its control: distributor identifier (stable, never resolved from a
  display name); dealer identifier (stable source-system identifier, mapped explicitly to a legal entity
  or site, with aliases and effective dates); reporting period (calendar month and source time basis,
  with transaction dates preserved separately when supplied); original part number (text, preserving
  leading zeros, with a normalised join key stored separately); signed quantity and unit (piece, set,
  kit or other, with positive sales and negative returns distinguishable); transaction type and channel
  (sale, return or adjustment; direct or wholesale; and an explicit unknown rather than a guess);
  submission identifier (batch ID, revision, extraction timestamp, schema version); coverage declaration
  (included dealers, channels and product scope; excluded records; missing periods; provisional status;
  known limitations);
- the dealer population: a stable dealer roster including zero-activity dealers. **Absence from a
  transaction file alone does not establish zero activity or the end of a commercial relationship.**
- wholesale: declare whether it is included and to which known purchasing entity the sale is attributed.
  **Do not assign a wholesaler's purchases to downstream dealers without a supported relationship.**

Then correction and acceptance: full-replacement revisions for a distributor-month; a corrected batch
**supersedes** rather than adds; reprocessing must not duplicate quantities; earlier versions and the
reason for revision are preserved. Before acceptance, check identifiers, periods, quantities, units,
duplicates, coverage and control totals — and record **accepted and quarantined quantities, not merely
rejected row counts**. An incomplete dealer mapping need not prevent a valid national total, but it
**must** prevent unsupported dealer attribution. Return a receipt with the accepted period, coverage
summary, exceptions and correction route, and rebuild dependent outputs when a revision changes them,
showing that the historical result was restated.

**D2. Participation: start with the existing incentive, then earn the rest.**
State the kickback position **honestly and in one place**, with no hedging footnote:

- a portion of the distributor kickback is tied to providing sales-to-dealer information, and that
  condition **starts** the exchange — it buys frequency and coverage;
- it is **not sufficient on its own**: it gets a file, and a file sent to satisfy a condition is the
  minimum that satisfies it;
- the **commercial terms of the kickback are not confirmed by any file in this workspace** and must not
  be stated as fact. If the paper needs them, it says they are to be confirmed, and by whom.

Then the three reasons a distributor contributes — the requirement, self-interest (the parts team
already has to explain its own numbers every month, and Universe assembles that explanation), and the
defensive reason (a recorded code saying "the customer bought elsewhere at a lower price" protects the
parts manager from being read as a weak seller). Say which one is the engine and which one keeps the
file coming in month six.

Then the exchange itself: **if the participant gives Universe X, Universe can return Y, which helps them
decide Z.** Every ask must complete that sentence. Reject any feature whose only explanation is "better
visibility".

**D3. The levels of contribution.**
Progressive, with the rule stated: a participant sees the whole review experience; the depth of the
dealer and reason picture unlocks as they contribute; nothing is hidden out of spite — the deeper views
cannot be computed from less data.

- Level 0, ISZE data only — billing against target spend per truck, family mix, genuine against value
  tier, self-assessment results with the assessed population and the round stated, position against
  comparable markets, tenders. For the pilot market, the dealer-named picture already held is shown as a
  deliberate demonstration. It must be genuinely useful, not a teaser.
- Level 1, sales to dealer — the mandatory core: dealer identifier, part number, quantity, period. **No
  prices.** Unlocks share of billing that moved onward by dealer and family, stocking position,
  part-number demand below the distributor, and the geographic weak-spot picture.
- Level 2, dealer and network identity — validated account and site relationships and an approved link
  to the self-assessment round. **Say plainly that this link does not work today.**
- Level 3, commercial context — structured explanations rather than documents: competitor price quote,
  availability or backorder, tender involvement, customer requirement, existing stock,
  application or fitment mismatch. Unlocks the reason behind a movement and builds a reason library.
- Level 4, optional operational information, assessed one item at a time — backorders first (the format
  already exists), stock not yet (there is no stock measure anywhere and no engine to use it), the dealer
  service population to be built jointly with dealers rather than requested from the distributor, and
  workshop activity only after two completed reviews produce a repeated, decision-relevant question.

**D4. What is outside the mandatory feed.**
List it and say why the boundary is strategic: selling prices to dealer, selling prices to customer,
distributor margin, dealer margin, distributor stock, dealer stock, customer identities, fleet or
customer account data, VIN, workshop repair orders, technician information, customer invoices, personal
data, competitor invoices. Then the sentence that carries the argument: if Universe begins by requesting
the distributor's prices, margin, stock, customers, vehicles and workshop activity, it will read as ISZE
trying to see through the distributor rather than helping the distributor run the authorised network.
Optional sharing must never become a concealed condition for receiving the basic return. And the hard
rule for every module: **the moment an optional input becomes a condition of access to the core review,
the review stops being a product and becomes a form.**

**D5. What not to ask for yet, and what never to ask for.**
Prices on the sales-to-dealer file; dealer resale prices or anything implying a required resale price
(monitoring and reporting is one thing; anything reading as price coordination is a legal and
relationship risk with no upside); full stock and inventory valuation at entry; warranty odometer as a
fleet or service-population source; dealer-level vehicles-in-service derived by allocating a national
fleet — it looks like a denominator and is not one; customer or VIN-level detail and workshop job cards
before a repeated question needs them; tender feedback as a condition of anything; any standards
compliance scorecard or dealer league table; and uploaded row counts, login counts or the reference-price
value of workshop lines as measures of benefit.

**D6. The dealer proposition.**
The dealer receives no kickback from ISZE, and the paper says so plainly. What the dealer gets: help for
its own business — how its parts purchasing is developing, which families it uses, where genuine and
value-tier alternatives are relevant, what vehicle population may sit behind the demand, and which
follow-ups deserve attention. What it is not: an ISZE dealer audit or league table. The dealer can see
the evidence behind a discussion, correct wrong information, add local context and take part in deciding
the action. The dealer leaves each review with something it chose, and the previous one is visibly
tracked. **The dealer must not become a coloured dot on a map controlled by ISZE.**

**D7. The dividend, and the test of the whole design.**
Three rules: the dividend lands in the same cycle it was earned in; it attaches to a decision, not to a
new chart; and the contributor can audit it — a per-participant list of what they gave, what was
returned, and which decisions it touched. That last one is what lets the exchange survive a change of
distributor staff, which is when most data programmes quietly die.

Then the success condition, quoted: **if the sales-to-dealer requirement disappeared from the kickback
tomorrow, distributors would continue supplying the data because withdrawing it would make their own
Universe output materially worse.** Say how that is measured — through behaviour, not survey scores — and
name the strongest single signal: voluntary data enrichment, a distributor giving something that was
explicitly not required because the previous exchange produced value.

### Part E — The parts and the identity substance

**E1. Identity: the four things that are not the same thing.**
Organisations, purchasing accounts, physical sites and dealers. State what exists and what does not:

- a servicing workshop carries a stable numeric identifier, 84 values, one-to-one with its name, with
  positions 4–8 of the VIN carrying the model code;
- a vehicle operator or lessee carries a stable numeric identifier, 202 values, one-to-one with its name;
- a physical site appears as a depot name, 260 names, **with no identifier**;
- **a purchasing dealer has no identifier of any kind on our side.** ISZE invoices the distributor, not
  the dealer. The two columns a join would need exist in the purchasing file and are blank on all 66,018
  rows, for the concrete reason that there are only three purchasing accounts in scope and all three are
  the distributor's own.

Then the consequence, stated once and clearly: **ISZE's billing system identifies a customer as a
sold-to account, and that account is the distributor's; the distributor's sales-to-dealer file identifies
a dealer as a name. Those are two different namespaces held by two different organisations, and nothing
has ever bridged them.** Every per-dealer price, per-truck comparison and per-dealer measure needs that
bridge.

**E2. What a match is and is not.**
A normalised-name match is evidence to review, not an approval of identity. Where the match is one-to-one
it may be used; where the key is not one-to-one, **do not print a dealer page — print the collision
instead.** State the rule and the counts together: the count is the one for the stated rule, and the rule
is named beside it. The paper must not print a match count without its rule. Name the family of
non-dealer entries that must be excluded from any per-dealer page — head office, national service
entities and roadside recovery operations — because a page built without that exclusion would publish a
page for the distributor's own recovery operation.

**E3. Accounts and sites: one account is not one site.**
One commercial account is not automatically one physical site. A multi-site account's quantities remain
at account level and must not be duplicated across site markers. Where an account covers several sites,
present the account-level result instead of inventing a site allocation. A country, a submission cohort
or a map location does not validate which distributor is responsible for a site: responsibility needs an
explicit, dated relationship, and unresolved sites are shown as unresolved rather than silently assigned.

**E4. Parts: classification, supersession and equivalence.**

- **Classification.** Say there is more than one classification release and no single authority declared
  between them, and that this is an open decision, internal to ISZE, closing the only blocking question
  ISZE can close alone. Say what the drift figure actually describes: a stale one-time copy inside the
  main table, not a live defect, and the corrected rebuild restores almost all of the affected part
  numbers to the right family. **Never print a family label outside ISZE until one release is pinned.**
- **The axis problem.** The same part can honestly be described two ways, because the classification
  carries two trees — a catalogue axis and a workbook axis — and they disagree for many parts. **Name the
  axis.** A sentence that says "the classification says these parts are exposed to competition" is
  reading the catalogue label, and must say so.
- **Supersession.** One part number replaced by another. The table is safe to join — no old number maps
  to more than one current number, so applying it cannot multiply a total. What it does is **split a
  purchasing history across two codes**, which is why it is a pre-build step and not a footnote. Its
  authority is unknown: it carries no date, no source system and no reversal flag, and the paper says so
  and names who must confirm it. **State which sheet of which file was applied wherever it is used.**
- **Equivalence.** A pairing in any source is a **catalogue relationship, not a fitment approval**.
  Nothing on disk is a fitment approval or a homologation. "No pair exists" is not "no product exists".
  And a supersession relationship is not application approval either. **Wherever a substitution is
  proposed, application must be validated separately before the alternative is recommended**, and until
  it is, the relationship is reported as a catalogue fact and nothing more. Make no quality inference
  from tier or country of origin in either direction.
- **One population per calculation.** Every measure is computed on one named part population and the
  population prints beside the figure. Name the three that exist for the worked family — the named page
  numbers, the consumable elements inside the family, and the wider parts tree — and state which of them
  may carry a replacement measure and which may never (the wider tree includes housings, caps, bolts,
  gaskets and an assembly-level item; it is a family statement, never a replacement measure).

**E5. IDS: what it is, and the four limits on presenting it.**
It is a **dealer self-assessment**, one round, and the paper says so every time. The specific facts:

- the network file holds a set of dealer records across nineteen country lists with a distinct-name
  count below the row count;
- **there is one assessment round. No second round exists.**
- **there is no per-dealer assessment date.** The date field exists in the workbook and is empty on every
  sheet, and there are zero date-formatted cells in it anywhere. So **any statement of the form "as at
  date, dealer X scored Y" is not available**;
- a summary of dealer assessments identifies the assessed population and the assessment period, and a
  totals-only record must not generate section or question scores. A blank is not a failure;
- **ISZE does not audit distributors.** A distributor-level presentation of IDS describes an assessed
  dealer population, its response coverage and its assessment dates, and never reads as a
  distributor-audit score.
- The action register sitting beside it is a scaffold, not a working process: it has rows, no owners and
  no due dates, and every row reads open. **A blank register does not prove the network is inactive** —
  say that, because the opposite reading is the easy mistake.

**E6. Israel, and every other blend trap.**
Two Israel distributors, separate systems, separate databases: the Israel country pack is one
distributor's dealer network, and the other **did not participate in that round**. Never average, blend
or present them as one. Likewise the two UK lanes are different entities and are never mixed. Country
labels are spelled out in English in anything shared.

### Part F — Delivery

**F1. The two pilots, and why they are different.**
One national pilot and one dealer-level pilot. The national one delivers a national commercial review
plus a separately labelled view of the defined dealer-assessment population, and **must not allocate
national purchasing to named dealers or infer dealer performance from a national total.** The
dealer-level one tests dealer-level purchasing patterns and an IDS connection, subject to valid
identifiers, period coverage and confirmed dealer/site relationships. Before showing a bridge between
the two purchasing flows, accept and reconcile the relevant billing slice separately from the
sales-to-dealer feed — **the presence of one source does not establish the availability or completeness
of the other. Do not divide a national total among sites to create a dealer view.**

**F2. Exit criteria for both.**
A pilot passes when one useful review can be reproduced, explained to its intended recipient, corrected
through the submission process, and followed into an owned action — **and it must also demonstrate its
unavailable-data state.** A pilot does not pass because a map opens or because every dealer has been
given a score. State the no-forced-symmetry rule: a credible national pilot and a credible dealer-level
pilot are preferable to two visually identical pilots built on incompatible evidence. Say plainly that
passing one pilot does not certify the other's mappings or create data it does not have.

**F3. The workspace experience.**
The four destinations; the portfolio as the entry point for understanding the range; the Genuine/BVP
comparison as a central experience; the removed sales cube and the deferred photography left as
decisions, not reopened. Then the operating details a builder needs: opening a dealer, family, part or
follow-up must preserve the selected period, clock, transaction flow and population where applicable,
and where the next view cannot support that context it explains the restriction before changing it; a
switch from actual billing to a reference valuation must be visible, not hidden in a tooltip; keep a
compact context strip near the headline result showing scope, period, flow, measure basis, freshness and
coverage; unknown and withheld are distinct states and **must not render as zeros**; a commercial review
opens the relevant parts and comparison evidence, creates an action without retyping the scope and
exports a readable evidence packet; actions return to their source analysis; a correction is visible
wherever the affected result appears. The first release favours a small number of complete paths over
many incomplete pages.

**F4. Maps do not establish responsibility.**
The network atlas helps users navigate known sites. A country, a submission cohort or a map location does
not validate which distributor is responsible for a site. Unresolved sites are shown as unresolved.

**F5. The logical data architecture.**
Four layers as logical responsibilities, with the platform choice stated as a separate implementation
decision: retained source submissions; accepted normalised records; versioned business definitions;
published views and actions. Then the six record groups with their responsibilities (source batch and
coverage; commercial facts; product and applicability; network and IDS; market and demand context;
metrics, findings and actions).

Then the rules that stop a join inventing volume: state the expected relationship for every join and test
for duplication; a multi-applicability part joined to several bands must not multiply the purchasing
total; an account linked to several sites must not produce several copies of its result; preserve
original part identifiers alongside normalised keys; keep unresolved mappings in an explicit bucket and
**do not discard their quantities from the reconciliation**; test totals before and after classification,
relationship mapping and valuation; and where a transformation deliberately changes the measure,
document and reconcile that change.

Then the metric register: one definition per named measure, each with an owner, a formula, an eligible
scope, exclusions, a reporting clock and a version. **Different measures must not reuse the same label
merely because they occupy the same screen position.**

Then correction and reproducibility: retain accepted revisions rather than overwriting history;
a published result identifies the batch, relationship and metric versions used; a correction triggers
recomputation of dependent results and a visible restatement while the earlier published snapshot is
preserved; store a finding as more than prose — scope, query or calculation reference, evidence links and
status. **An AI-generated explanation may summarise these records; it must not become the source of its
own quantities or silently redefine a measure.**

**F6. Governance and accountability.**
The roles, each with its decision: product owner (scope and acceptance), data owners (source coverage and
corrections), metric owners (business definitions), commercial owners (interventions and support terms),
technical owners (application, permissions, testing, release record). **Assign people to these roles
before expanding beyond the pilots** — a role with no name is not an owner. IDS interpretation belongs
with the accountable standards owner; product equivalence and fitment require a technical authority; a
classification suggestion or an AI-generated match cannot substitute for that approval.

Then protecting the exchange: distributors receive their authorised information and approved comparative
context, never unrestricted access to other distributors' records; dealer views follow validated
relationships and permissions; aggregated comparisons account for the risk that a small population
reveals an individual participant; optional prices, costs, stock and service information need explicit
purpose, access and retention controls; cross-distributor price sharing and competitively sensitive
benchmark design pass internal legal and competition review before release. Treat imported files and
narrative notes as data, not as instructions.

**F7. Access — the sentence that must be in the paper.**
**A login or a hidden menu does not remove embedded wider-network data. Hiding another distributor
behind a filter is not access control.** External sharing is a data-separation problem before it is a
login problem. State that there is no host today: no authentication in the analysis application, no
static-site deployment configured, and the publishing folder's contents are not safe to publish as they
stand.

**F8. A release is a retrievable deliverable.**
Every accepted release has one stable index naming the version, date, owner, entry point, included
modules, data snapshot and known limitations, and lists the actual deliverable filenames with their
integrity checks, and identifies the portfolio, atlas and distributor-view entry points explicitly.
**A temporary browser preview is not the release location.** A label such as "R1", or a statement that a
build is complete, is not evidence that the package exists, opens and contains the agreed scope. Earlier
releases are retained, but the accepted release is unambiguous.

**F9. Test the failures that would damage trust.**
Name the failures and require each to be tested before release: duplicate submissions; revised months;
missing periods; returns; unresolved dealers; multi-site accounts; multi-band applicability; unpriced
parts; that actual billing cannot silently switch to a reference valuation; and that repeated market
actuals cannot be summed across bands. State that regression tests preserve the corrected tonnage
behaviour, the removed sales cube and the agreed portfolio and Genuine/BVP scope, and verify the national
boundary of the national pilot and the distinction between self-assessments and distributor commercial
views. Keep the tested scenarios, results, outstanding issues and approval with the release. **Saving a
rule or discussing a fix is not proof that the running product has adopted it.**

**F10. Delivery gates.**
Four gates with their pass conditions, as gates and not as wishes:

- **Foundation** — create the release index, source-coverage register, metric register and unresolved
  relationship list; identify which capabilities are accepted, demonstrated, proposed or blocked;
  confirm the boundary of the third pillar. Pass condition: a reviewer can identify the accepted product,
  the sources behind it and the interpretation rules it applies, with no ambiguous "latest" file and no
  silent redefinition of a measure.
- **Pilot** — two honest paths delivered at their supported levels, each with a real monthly series, a
  part-level explanation, a validated comparison or an explicit limitation, and an owned follow-up.
  Exercise a correction and show its effect. Pass condition: each pilot can explain its figures,
  reproduce its result and demonstrate what is unavailable.
- **Participation** — run the agreed monthly submission and return process; measure accepted coverage,
  exceptions, correction turnaround and the work needed to produce a usable review; ask whether the
  distributor used the output in a commercial conversation, not merely whether someone logged in. Pass
  condition: participants can maintain the feed, recognise its limitations and receive a useful review
  without a bespoke rebuild each month. **Targets are agreed after the pilot baseline is known.**
- **Expansion** — add markets or optional exchanges only after the relevant controls work, and only when
  the benefit and the maintenance ownership are clear. **Do not restore deferred features merely to
  create the appearance of progress.**

**F11. What should be measured.**
Track data reliability, review usefulness and commercial outcomes **separately**. Useful measures:
valid mapped coverage, restated periods, review preparation effort, actions reviewed or closed, and
realised gross profit for bounded interventions where it can be calculated. **Do not treat logins,
modelled opportunity euros, or the number of generated findings as realised commercial value.**

## 4. The hard rules that override everything

These are absolute. They are not style preferences. If a chapter cannot obey one of them, the chapter
changes — not the rule.

**Measurement.**

1. **Keep the flows distinct.** Billing to the distributor, the distributor's sales to dealers, and any
   reference-price valuation are three different things. No sum of the two sales layers as total revenue.
2. **Never add, average or reconcile the two sales files by assumption.** Name the source for every
   measure. Where the two give different numbers for the same dealer and part, both are reported with the
   difference visible, and neither is declared the winner.
3. **No national fleet divided across dealers, anywhere, in any form, including as a rate or a per-truck
   benchmark.** The market fleet figure is a market figure; it is not a dealer fleet and there is no
   dealer-level fleet anywhere on disk.
4. **No whole vehicle's demand allocated to one part number, family or dealer.**
5. **A missing source is never a zero.** No usable observation is `unknown` or `missing_source`. A month
   with no row is a confirmed absence of rows — not a confirmed zero sale and not a confirmed missing
   report. The same rule applies to classification absence, price absence and identity absence, each with
   its own distinct label.
6. **Currency, price basis, reporting period and population are stated wherever a figure is used.**
7. **Every figure is quoted with its own clock.** There is no single reporting date. State the horizon of
   the source beside the figure. **A later file does not extend an earlier file's horizon — the only clock
   a later sales file moves is its own.**
8. **Never annualise a partial year.** Where a period covers part of a year, say so and do not present a
   total as a year.
9. **A month is complete only with source-backed coverage evidence.**
10. **An observation is not a cause.** A before/after view is a monitoring tool. A causal claim requires a
    defensible comparison and an assessment of competing explanations.

**Presentation.**

11. **Every output states its scope, period, value basis, release pin and coverage limitations, with the
    measurement differences visible next to the result — not hidden in a help page.**
12. **One population per calculation, printed beside the figure.**
13. **No dealer page where the identity key is not one-to-one. Print the collision instead.**
14. **No score that aggregates unrelated checks into one number with opaque ingredients.**
15. **No ranking that rewards size and calls it performance.**
16. **Nothing is presented as achieved because a proposal showed it, and nothing is presented as verified
    because a narrative said so.**

**Status and version.**

17. **Never write "fully verified".** Use the six status words and no others.
18. **Never overwrite a version.** Write a new file; ask which label it carries.
19. **Never state a commercial term as fact that no file in this workspace confirms.** That includes the
    kickback terms.

**Language.**

20. The banned-word table in §2 applies to every page, every table and every caption.
21. **Every acronym is glossed on first use.**
22. No internal codenames, no agent vocabulary, no term of art that needs a glossary to land.

## 6. The do-not-publish list

None of the following may appear in the paper, in a footnote, in an appendix or in a chart caption. Where
a chapter would want one of them, it describes the position in words instead.

1. The superseded ITUK capture percentage that predates the split that moved ITUK to a higher figure —
   pairing it with the current parts base mixes two methods.
2. Any all-models mileage pair presented as a nested comparison. The two mileages come from two
   different fleets, not one fleet at two levels of detail, and the all-models figure is mostly composed
   of vehicles with a blank model field.
3. Any single capture rate above 100% plotted at face value. Name it as unplottable and why, or leave it
   off.
4. Any sales-to-dealer coverage share that cannot be re-derived from a cited source.
5. The coverage denominator that matches no total in any file. Replace the denominator with a real one or
   omit the number.
6. The earlier fleet count of 2,526. The figure is 2,402.
7. The earlier mixed fitted-against-bought comparison. It mixed a wide fitted population with a narrow
   purchase population, and the purchase figure in it was not a reading of the consumable family.
8. The superseded classification drift figure in part numbers and euro, and the unsupported
   competitive-flag figure — its definition is not stated and the population it names does not reproduce.
9. Any statement that a pipeline check is currently failing. No check is failing; the warning in the live
   report is a staleness warning about a model that is current to an earlier cut-off than the billing.
10. Any figure whose definition or population is not stated in the paper itself.
11. Any statement that the two sales files agree cleanly, and any bare statement of a median ratio as
    evidence of agreement.
12. Any statement that an identity match is evidence that goods moved, stock was held, or a dealer resold
    anything.
13. Any claim that a mapped name proves a dealer and a workshop are the same legal entity.
14. Any dealer-level figure for a market other than the one market and the one year that carry dealer
    names.
15. Any per-dealer rate, per-dealer expectation or per-dealer fleet figure.
16. Any market-share statement, and any statement about what a dealer sold to its own customer.

## 7. Format, length and file discipline

- **Markdown is the source of record.** Write one file. Do not attempt to produce a PDF on this machine:
  there is no office suite installed here, and the existing editions were authored and printed elsewhere.
  Say in your handover that PDF production happens wherever the authoring tool lives.
- **Shape.** Front matter, six parts, four appendices. Aim for a document a commercial reader can finish
  in one sitting: roughly 25 to 40 pages of A4 at normal body size. Prose where the argument runs; tables
  where the content is genuinely parallel — field lists, status lists, gate conditions, the decision list.
  **Do not** turn the paper into a bullet dump. A three-sentence paragraph beats three bullets.
- **Front matter carries no content that is not repeated in the body.** It exists to place the reader.
- **Every table has a header row that states what it is**, and every table that carries a figure carries
  the figure's basis in the same row or an immediately adjacent footnote.
- **One idea per paragraph.** Lead with the point.
- **No changelog, no build notes, no agent commentary** anywhere in the document. Those belong in a
  separate internal note, not in a paper that may be forwarded.
- **Absolute paths only where a reader has to open something.** Where you cite a file, a path, or a
  recorded check, read it from the source before you cite it. Do not cite from memory.
