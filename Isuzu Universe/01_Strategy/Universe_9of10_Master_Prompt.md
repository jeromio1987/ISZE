# Universe: develop a proposition that can earn 9/10

## The assignment

Act as the product strategist and critical research partner for Isuzu Motors Europe's Universe initiative. Develop the proposition itself, not merely a more persuasive description of the existing concept.

Universe has three intended pillars: AIM, IDS and PRISM. Tender support is a potential extension, not an approved fourth pillar. Keep these working names for this exercise.

Your task is to produce a substantially stronger product and commercial paper, supported by a realistic operating model, a focused pilot and separate implementation instructions for Cursor. Do not change the application or rebuild dashboards, price lists or data processes during this assignment.

The review supplied with this request identifies four weaknesses:

| Assessment | Starting assessment | Weakness to resolve |
|---|---:|---|
| Overall proposition as currently specified | 6/10 | The paper explains navigation more convincingly than the decisions and commercial benefits it enables. |
| Clarity of the pillar boundaries | 4/10 | AIM and IDS are recognisable, but PRISM's unique responsibility is unclear. |
| Proposition for getting distributors on board | 6/10 | A monthly management use case exists, but the return for sharing data is insufficiently concrete. |
| Proposition for getting dealers on board | 4/10 | Distributors have a reason to review dealers; dealers have a weaker reason to participate or use Universe themselves. |

Treat these as the supplied review's diagnostic scores, not measured adoption results. Aim for at least 9/10 on each dimension individually. An average of 9/10 is not sufficient.

Do not achieve the target by changing the rubric, flattering the concept, adding impressive terminology or declaring success. Fix the underlying weaknesses. Where evidence or decisions are missing, distinguish a strong proposed specification from a validated product.

The central question is:

**What will each participant decide or do better because Universe exists, why will they participate voluntarily, and how will we establish that the benefit is real?**

## Establish the actual starting point

Read the latest available Universe paper, roadmap, critique, pillar documentation and relevant application evidence before proposing changes. Read the project context files: 00_READ_ME_FIRST, 01_ISUZU_AND_ISZE_BUSINESS, 02_NUMBERS_AND_HOW_THEY_ARE_BUILT, 03_OPERATIONS_OPEN_WORK_AND_CAVEATS and 04_GLOSSARY_AND_HOW_TO_WRITE.

Where available, inspect the corrected tonnage v2 evidence, the ITUK dealer-data/source-code pack, the PRISM overview and the relevant IDS documentation. Do not pretend to have read missing files, inspected a local Windows directory or tested an application you cannot access.

Create a compact evidence register identifying the source, version, period, population, what it establishes and its limitations. Separate implemented capabilities, documented but unverified capabilities, approved plans, new proposals and unresolved questions.

Apply explicit approved corrections to the versions they supersede. Do not assume that a later file modification date makes every definition superior. Preserve deliberately different measures and explain differences in basis.

The accepted scope remains the starting point: preserve the portfolio overview and Genuine/BVP comparisons; keep the sales cube removed; keep photographs deferred. Do not repeat feedback already resolved in tonnage v2 or reopen closed work.

Ask only questions that cannot be answered from the available evidence and materially change the design. Continue all unblocked work. A missing source is not permission to invent its content, but neither should it prevent useful work on unrelated sections.

## Define the commercial proposition before the features

Write a one-sentence definition of Universe that names its users, the decisions it improves and the practical result. Avoid definitions such as 'a connected ecosystem', 'a single pane of glass' or 'a platform bringing everything together'.

Then explain the present problem, the better decision Universe enables, the evidence needed, who acts, and how the result is reviewed. Distinguish value to ISZE, value to the distributor and value to the dealer. More purchases from ISZE are not automatically proof of greater dealer profit or better customer service.

ISZE sells to distributors, distributors sell to dealers, and dealers serve customers. Respect that commercial chain. Do not design a proposition that depends on ISZE becoming a direct-to-dealer ordering channel.

Identify the limited set of recurring decisions that justify the product. For each, specify the current method, what Universe changes, why the existing reports do not already suffice, and what unnecessary work disappears.

Reject any feature whose only explanation is 'better visibility'. Complete the sentence: 'This lets this user decide ___, using ___, and then do ___.'

Show a credible first useful experience using information already available. Do not make the entire proposition depend on every distributor contributing perfect data first.

Be ambitious about useful outcomes, not the number of features. For each important limitation, identify a supported alternative action or the precise prerequisite that would unlock the proposed benefit. The limitations should shape the design, not replace the proposition.

## Resolve the three pillars without inventing PRISM

Use the approved expansion **AIM: Aftermarket Intelligence Model**. Preserve its established classification and commercial-analysis role. Address its agreed benefits: sales explanation, pricing precision, market comparison and service-range opportunity. Rewrite the last benefit in concrete terms rather than leaving it as a slogan.

Use **IDS: Isuzu Dealer Standards**. It records dealer self-assessments and supports improvement discussions. It is not an ISZE distributor audit. A summary of dealer assessments must identify the assessed population and assessment period.

Recover PRISM's exact definition and actual responsibilities from its documentation. Do not invent an acronym expansion. Do not turn PRISM into a generic 'insight engine', 'action layer' or duplicate sales dashboard simply to complete a three-part story.

Produce a pillar responsibility table covering each pillar's unique question, authoritative inputs, transformations or analysis, outputs, primary users, decisions supported and explicit exclusions. Identify what each pillar consumes from the others and what it returns.

Give each calculation and authoritative business definition one accountable owner. A result may appear in multiple places, but it must not be independently recalculated under conflicting rules.

Apply the removal test: if AIM, IDS or PRISM disappeared, what specific capability would be lost? If removing a pillar changes only the branding, the boundary is not credible.

Where current responsibilities overlap, show the overlap and recommend a resolution. Label any reassignment as a proposed change, not an established fact. Preserve the three-pillar intention without hiding an unresolved definition behind a diagram.

Explain what Universe itself coordinates across the pillars: context, navigation, authorised access, evidence and follow-up, where supported. Shared functions need not become another pillar.

## Show the dynamics, not merely connections

Specify a relationship diagram whose connections carry meaning. Label what moves between components: a classification, an assessment finding, a commercial observation, a supported comparison or an agreed follow-up. An unlabeled arrow saying 'connected' is insufficient.

Show a complete decision sequence:

**Observation -> interpretation -> verification -> decision -> agreed action -> outcome review.**

For each stage, identify the responsible participant, source, population, period and status. Show how a user returns later to determine whether the action was useful.

Include a concrete situation where combining evidence supports a better discussion than the individual views alone. Also include a situation where a connection must not be made because identities, periods or populations do not match.

Do not force every task through all three pillars. Explain when AIM alone, IDS alone, PRISM alone or a combination is appropriate. A user should not have to understand the architecture to complete a task.

Do not imply that an IDS weakness causes lower parts purchases merely because both appear together. Show the distinction between an observation, a hypothesis worth discussing and a demonstrated relationship.

## Make the distributor proposition an explicit exchange

Answer the distributor's question: 'What do I receive that is useful enough to justify sharing data, reviewing the results and involving my dealers?'

Define the information requested, its minimum acceptable form and frequency, the work required to supply it, the output returned, and the decision that output supports. Separate requirements for the first useful release from optional information that unlocks additional analyses.

Develop a realistic monthly review that prioritises a small number of meaningful issues, explains the supporting evidence, allows the distributor to challenge the interpretation, and ends with agreed actions and a later review.

Evaluate candidate benefits such as relevant dealer conversations, validated Genuine/BVP range comparisons, purchasing-pattern explanations and better-supported commercial-support requests. Do not describe candidates as existing capabilities without evidence.

For every claimed benefit, name what data it requires and what cannot be concluded without it. Purchasing records alone do not establish stock availability, inventory efficiency, customer demand or sales lost to competitors.

Explain how Universe reduces duplicated preparation and gives something useful back to the distributor's team, rather than creating another reporting obligation for ISZE.

Address commercial confidentiality, dealer relationships, data quality, workload and the fear that incomplete information will become a misleading ranking. Do not invent discounts, incentives or guaranteed commercial support to make participation attractive.

## Make the dealer proposition stand on its own

Treat dealer value as a separate design problem, not a paragraph underneath distributor management.

Distinguish the dealer principal or aftersales manager from the parts manager. Identify a practical task for each and the benefit they personally receive. Consider service-management tasks only where the available data supports them.

Answer: 'Why would I use this when nobody is asking me to complete an assessment?' Then answer: 'What useful result do I leave with, and why would I return?'

Test possibilities such as a clearer review of the dealer's own purchasing pattern, a technically supported Genuine/BVP comparison, a better-evidenced request for distributor support, or an IDS improvement action with visible follow-through. These are options to evaluate, not compulsory features or promises.

For every proposed dealer benefit, explain the decision, output, evidence, limitations and distributor involvement. Show how the dealer can add context, dispute an incorrect interpretation and obtain a response.

Do not promise profitable stock recommendations without inventory and demand evidence, job-completion guidance without technical validation, or customer retention analysis without customer-level evidence and permission.

Compare direct dealer use, distributor-led review and a combination. Recommend the participation model that delivers real value with the least unnecessary burden. Do not manufacture a dealer portal simply to claim adoption, and do not confuse attendance at a mandatory review with voluntary product use.

Specify the first dealer experience, the useful result after the first review, and the reason to return in the following reporting cycle. Make the data required for each stage explicit.

A proposal that helps ISZE and distributors supervise dealers but gives dealers no meaningful benefit does not pass this dimension.

## Make trust and information access part of the proposition

Propose a clear information-access table for ISZE, distributors and dealers. State who can see which measures, at what level of detail, for which entities and purposes. Mark approval requirements rather than presenting proposed access rights as existing policy.

Include the minimum data needed, correction procedures, permitted exports, confidentiality boundaries, historical versions and the handling of small comparison groups. Peer comparisons must use genuinely comparable populations and must not reveal another dealer's commercially sensitive information.

Separate participation in IDS from permission to share transaction data. Do not silently repurpose self-assessment responses or invent contractual data-sharing obligations.

Propose how dealers and distributors can understand an observation's source and challenge it. A statement that can lead to a commercial discussion should not be an unexplained black box.

Treat hiding a screen as different from restricting access to the underlying information. Any shared implementation must demonstrate authorised access before sensitive data is distributed.

Keep raw line-level billing, complete price lists and other restricted material out of shared outputs. Use only approved derived information for examples and demonstrations.

## Demonstrate the proposition through worked situations

Develop five concise worked situations: a distributor-led monthly review; a dealer-first Genuine/BVP discussion; a dealer-first IDS improvement follow-up; a case with incomplete or unmatched data; and a case where the correct conclusion is 'do not take commercial action yet'.

For each situation, state the user and decision, trigger, available evidence, missing evidence, relevant pillar contributions, what appears to the user, action or deliberate non-action, responsible participant and later verification.

At least one situation must show what the existing reports cannot accomplish as readily. At least one must show how a dealer benefits without providing a large new dataset. At least one must show that combining pillars improves the interpretation without implying causation.

Use real examples only where the evidence supports them. Otherwise label the situation illustrative and use placeholders rather than invented turnover, margins, fleets or opportunity amounts. An illustrative walkthrough is not evidence of successful adoption.

Do not make a missing purchase an automatic sales opportunity. Check range availability, vehicle applicability, source completeness, relevant periods and any necessary stock or commercial context first. A shared classification or similar part name is not proof of interchangeability, an approved substitute or a technically complete repair kit.

## Make the experience distinctive because it explains more

Specify a focused experience that feels coherent and technically accomplished without using visual effects to hide weak evidence.

Explain the opening view for each role, how users find a relevant issue, how context follows them between views, how they inspect the evidence, and how an agreed follow-up remains linked to its source.

Give the geographic map a defined job. Distinguish geographical location, validated distributor responsibility, analytical grouping and dealer-assessment population. Do not infer business responsibility from country, proximity or submission-cohort labels.

Preserve the selected distributor, dealer, period, part family and measurement basis where applicable. When a destination cannot support the same selection, explain the change rather than silently broadening the data.

Propose no more than three distinctive interactions, each tied to a business question. For each, describe its behaviour, required data, practical advantage, simpler alternative and acceptance test. Include an honest unavailable-data state.

Keep the portfolio overview and Genuine/BVP comparisons. Do not restore the sales cube, use deferred photographs as a dependency or add a decorative three-dimensional scene without a demonstrated task advantage.

Specify readable labels, keyboard access, reduced-motion behaviour, useful loading/error states and sensible performance expectations. State proposed performance targets as targets, not achieved results.

Provide a textual diagram specification and a short screen-by-screen storyboard for the strongest scenario. Do not substitute a list of screens for the commercial explanation.

## Apply the non-negotiable evidence rules

Keep ISZE-to-distributor billing, distributor-to-dealer sales and dealer-to-customer sales separate. Establish the actual seller, buyer and transaction stage from source documentation, not a filename alone. Missing downstream data means unknown activity, not zero activity.

Keep recorded quantities, quantities valued at a reference dealer price, actual invoiced revenue, actual gross profit and modelled opportunity distinct. State currency, price basis, reporting period and population wherever a figure is used. A reference-price valuation is not actual billing or realised profit. Do not subtract amounts at different commercial stages or price bases and label the difference a loss.

The supplied billing context ends on 30 June 2026. January-June 2026 is not a full year. Do not compare it with a full year and call the difference growth or decline. Use one calendar or fiscal basis at a time. Claim a later cut-off only with a verified later source.

Keep ITUK and IUK separate, and UMI and UTI separate. Do not include UTI in a population tied to the 2026 dealer-standards round. Verify dealer-to-distributor identity links; do not manufacture them from geography or names.

Do not derive dealer-specific service-demand gaps from a national fleet without a validated dealer fleet or service population. Do not allocate national opportunities to dealers by purchase share and present the result as measured demand.

For tonnage analysis, keep exclusive-applicability purchasing, modelled service demand and market-level actual purchasing separate. Pool multi-band parts rather than allocating them. Never sum repeated market actuals across band rows or divide those actuals by a single band's expectation to claim band-level cover.

Warranty does not establish scheduled-maintenance activity, missed servicing or commercial loss. A weak assessment result, low purchase count or absence of warranty claims does not establish competitor sales.

Use the current definitions and corrections in the numbers register. Do not use the unusable fleet forecast beyond 2025. Do not externally total the euro service-demand gap. Preserve deliberately different service-demand bases instead of averaging them.

Every quantified gap must identify the euro basis, part family or service category and relevant fleet. When a required element is missing, explain the observation without inventing a quantified commercial opportunity.

Treat expected gains as hypotheses until measured. Do not claim that a before-and-after improvement was caused by Universe without considering price, volume, mix, timing, supply, fleet changes and purchasing behaviour.

Do not introduce a new composite business-performance score merely to make the application look more sophisticated. The requested 9/10 review evaluates this proposition, not distributors or dealers.

## Design a pilot that can disprove the proposition

Recommend a narrow pilot using the strongest actually available evidence. Treat ITUK as a candidate to inspect, not an automatic winner. State the proposed distributor, dealer population, reporting period, source coverage, validated identity links and reasons for selection.

Separate what can be tested with the current data from what requires new access, user research or approval. Do not assume that the existence of a dealer-sales pack proves an IDS connection is valid.

Define the first useful release, what it deliberately excludes, who participates, who supports it, and what preparation is necessary. Prefer a complete useful decision process over many partially connected features.

Specify how to test comprehension, task completion, repeat use, the value of returned information, reporting effort and follow-up quality. Where commercial outcomes are measurable, define the source, denominator, baseline, observation period and attribution limits.

Distinguish direct product use from a useful distributor-assisted review. Record who receives the benefit and who incurs the preparation cost.

Propose continuation, revision and stopping conditions before observing results. Label any numerical thresholds as proposed acceptance criteria requiring approval, not existing standards or achieved performance.

Include the possibility that users like the demonstration but do not find enough recurring value to continue. State what evidence would invalidate the current concept and what a smaller useful version would retain.

## Use a fixed review rubric

Set the rubric before rewriting. Score each dimension against the five criteria below. For each criterion, use 0 for absent or contradicted, 1 for partial or underspecified, and 2 for concrete, internally consistent and supported by the appropriate source or worked specification.

**Overall proposition:** a precise recurring problem; meaningful decisions and actions; a credible commercial benefit mechanism; clear advantage over the current method; and an executable pilot with outcome checks.

**Pillar boundaries:** unique responsibilities; clear ownership of definitions and calculations; explicit inputs and outputs; meaningful interactions and exclusions; and a documented, non-duplicative PRISM role.

**Distributor proposition:** a specific return for sharing data; a useful recurring decision process; proportionate effort; credible confidentiality and correction arrangements; and a testable reason to participate again.

**Dealer proposition:** benefits owned by dealer users; useful tasks beyond assessment compliance; a concrete first result; a credible reason to return; and a practical participation model with agency, data boundaries and distributor involvement.

A score of 9 requires at least four criteria fully met and the fifth substantially addressed. A critical unresolved dependency overrides arithmetic: an undefined PRISM role, invented evidence or absent dealer benefit cannot be hidden by stronger scores elsewhere.

For each score, identify the exact section or evidence supporting it and the strongest remaining weakness. Mark unavailable evidence as unavailable; do not convert it into a pass.

Report specification quality separately from implementation status and user validation. A well-specified concept can be ready to test without having demonstrated adoption or financial impact. Do not present your own assessment as independent customer validation.

## Run a bounded improvement cycle

Produce an initial revision, then challenge it from the perspectives of an ISZE commercial lead, a distributor parts director, a dealer manager, a data reviewer and a product designer. These are analytical perspectives, not real interviews or independent reviewers.

Use up to three revision passes within this assignment: commercial substance and pillar boundaries; evidence and feasibility; then stakeholder value and usability. Fix identified weaknesses before presenting the final output.

Keep the evidence rules and scoring criteria unchanged between passes. Preserve accepted decisions. Do not keep adding features to compensate for an unclear proposition.

Provide only the material changes, their rationale, the checks performed and remaining limitations. Do not produce a theatrical debate, unsupported self-praise or claims of work continuing after the response.

If a dimension remains below 9, give the honest score and specify the missing decision, evidence or design change needed. Do not stop at criticism when a practical revision can resolve it now.

## Deliver the paper and execution plan separately

Deliver a complete revised paper, not an outline or advice about how somebody else should write it. Aim for approximately 2,000-3,000 words for the main paper, with technical and evaluation detail in concise appendices rather than interrupting the argument.

The main paper must explain the proposition, all three pillars, their dynamics, separate distributor and dealer benefits, representative decisions, trust boundaries and the pilot. It must make sense without the implementation backlog.

Add a pillar responsibility table, the relationship-diagram specification, the worked situations and the experience storyboard. Keep examples consistent across these elements; do not use different definitions in each appendix.

Provide a separate prioritised roadmap showing current evidence, proposed changes, dependencies, responsible roles, required decisions and acceptance criteria. Separate essential pilot work, conditional extensions and deferred ideas. Do not attach unsupported delivery dates or effort estimates.

Prepare a first Cursor package of no more than five tightly scoped tasks. For each, state the purpose, verified starting point, permitted change, exclusions, inputs, dependencies, acceptance tests and evidence to return. Never invent repository paths. If the current application cannot be inspected, make inspection the first task and keep changes conditional on its findings. Do not order a general rebuild.

Provide a separate ChatGPT worklist for source reconciliation, commercial logic, narrative, scenario review and assessment of returned evidence. Identify decisions requiring ISZE or distributor approval. Do not imply direct control of Cursor, live systems or local folders.

Finish with the four-dimension scorecard, implementation and user-validation status, the unresolved-decision register, and a short record of changes made to address the original critique.

## Writing standard

Write as a colleague making a commercial case, not a consultant selling software. Use complete sentences, meaningful headings and tables only where comparison benefits. Do not number the main sections.

Explain acronyms at first use. Use 'parts billed', 'sales to dealer', 'reference dealer price', 'target spend per truck', 'trucks in service' and 'Genuine/BVP'. Put margins before factors. Use approved model names with plain explanations, not internal nicknames.

Keep file names and field names in the internal evidence appendix, not the distributor-facing argument. Distinguish facts, interpretations, proposals and unresolved questions throughout.

Do not end with a promise to start. Deliver the work now.

**The result should let an ISZE decision-maker understand why Universe deserves support, a distributor understand why sharing data is worthwhile, a dealer understand why participation helps their own business, and an implementer understand what must change and how to prove it works.**
