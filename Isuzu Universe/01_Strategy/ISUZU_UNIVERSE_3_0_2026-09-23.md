## Executive summary

**What the programme is.** Universe connects four things Isuzu already holds or already receives:
product knowledge (AIM), the composition of the parts business, the dealer self-assessment (IDS) and
the market and fleet context (PRISM). It turns them into five tools a distributor or dealer opens by
choice. The commercial argument is not that analysis is good. It is that the participant receives
something it wants, and therefore keeps supplying the context that makes the next answer better.

**What the programme is not.** Routine availability enquiries, ordinary part and BVP lookups, day-to-day
quotation handling and support administration stay in the existing business. They are not modules of
Universe, not rewards for participation and not bargaining instruments for more data. The full boundary
is in section 1.2.

**The commercial problem being solved.** ISZE has asked distributors for information for many years
with insufficient visible value returned. A reporting obligation can start an exchange. Only a useful
tool continues it. The 2025 arrangement tied a share of the normal distributor rebate to a major-KPI
submission; that is the floor, not the mechanism.

**The three decisions on the table this month.**

| Decision | Recommendation | What has to be true first |
|---|---|---|
| Fund campaign support | A named line of **€60,000 to €90,000** inside the existing €100,000 route, with a minimum campaign size of €150,000 of eligible billing | The campaign-selection rule is agreed and the first eligible campaign is named |
| Approach ten dealers | Yes, as designed in chapter 8: arrive with a finished own-business review and ask for correction, not for data | A prepared review exists from data already held |
| Build first | AIM Commercial Atlas and IDS Development Studio | The classification release is pinned to a named file and version |

**What the paper deliberately does not claim.** No realised return. No approved budget. No forecast of
incremental sales. No participant has accepted anything. The one place where the paper quantifies what
the investment must earn is chapter 4, and it states the hurdle rather than a predicted outcome.

## 1. The commercial promise and the scope boundary

[[FIG2]]

### 1.1 The promise

Universe should give distributors and dealers tools that change how they understand and develop their
parts business. The user discovers a range issue, understands a commercial movement, compares
alternatives or chooses an improvement directly in the tool. Opening a request for somebody else to
answer is not the defining interaction.

The participant return comes first. A distributor receives a clearer basis for national and network
priorities. A dealer receives a useful view of its own supported business and a set of development
choices it controls. ISZE receives better product and market understanding through an exchange the
participant can judge valuable in its own right. More purchases from ISZE alone do not establish that a
dealer gained anything.

### 1.2 The scope boundary — what Universe is not

A normal service is not a reward for participation, and participation is not the price of a normal
service. No participant should have to provide sales history, workshop records, margins or customer
details to receive the service it should already receive.

| Outside Universe — stays in the existing business | Inside Universe — a commercial capability beyond the service |
|---|---|
| When will this part arrive? | Recurring, evidenced constraints may inform a strategic range study. No arrival-enquiry workflow is built. |
| Is a BVP alternative available? | Combined Genuine and BVP positioning, product composition and range-development analysis. |
| Can you handle this quotation? | Competitive evidence and transparent economics for a selected pricing or range decision. |
| Can you follow up this training action? | An explorable IDS profile and a participant-chosen development plan. |
| Can you submit or administer this tender? | Relevant fleet and tender intelligence and a reasoned decision about which opportunities to pursue. |

The test is not whether a normal service can be made more sophisticated. It is whether the proposed tool
adds a commercial capability beyond that service, helping a user see something materially useful, compare
a choice or evaluate an initiative without creating a parallel operational process.

**Keep the evidence when it improves the analysis. Do not import the day-to-day service into the
product.**

### 1.3 Why the boundary matters commercially

Two reasons, and the second is the one that decides whether a distributor says yes.

First, scope discipline protects the pilot. A programme that also answers availability questions is
judged on availability response times, and it will lose that comparison against a system that already
does it.

Second, the boundary is the offer. The sentence that changes how a distributor's parts manager reads the
approach is: *we are not asking you to feed a support desk, and the support desk does not get better or
worse depending on what you send us.* That is what distinguishes this from the data requests of the past
several years.

## 2. The portfolio

[[FIG3]]

### 2.1 Five tools, five questions

The tools answer different questions, and a participant usually starts with one of them. A single
monolithic application would force every user through the same opening screen and would make the first
experience depend on evidence the participant may not have.

| Tool | The question it answers | Primary evidence | Designed for |
|---|---|---|---|
| AIM Commercial Atlas | What is really changing in my parts business, and where? | Purchasing history and product classification | Distributor parts manager, dealer principal |
| IDS Development Studio | What should I improve, and what has moved? | Dealer self-assessment and development records | Dealer principal, distributor network manager |
| Range & Campaign Planner | Which products belong in this initiative, and for whom? | Atlas selection plus account coverage | Distributor commercial lead |
| Pricing & Margin Lab | What does this commercial trade-off do to revenue and margin? | Comparable evidence and stated unit economics | Distributor commercial lead, ISZE commercial team |
| PRISM Market Intelligence | Where should we concentrate effort? | Historical fleet and source-dated opportunities | Distributor management, ISZE commercial team |

### 2.2 The three developed applications

Three applications show the portfolio doing commercial work. They are examples of what the programme
enables, not a closed catalogue.

**Coordinated tenders.** A distributor qualifies a local opportunity; the relevant Isuzu entities
assemble a combined vehicle and aftersales proposition; headquarters receives a decision brief that makes
the requested decision unmistakable. The value is a stronger joint proposition and less reconstruction
between functions.

**IDS development progress.** The dealer explores its actual assessment, chooses a development priority
with its distributor, records milestones with evidence, and sees progress separately from any score. The
value is that the assessment becomes a management tool rather than an archive.

**Deal of the Day.** ISZE helps a distributor prepare a specific, time-limited commercial offer: a
reasoned product selection, a relevant audience, transparent economics and a way to learn from what
follows. The value is in the preparation behind the offer, not in the discount.

### 2.3 What connects them

A common selection — entity, account, period, product range, source basis, permitted audience — lets a
user carry a meaningful selection from the Atlas into a range plan or a pricing scenario without
reconstructing it. An IDS plan need not use pricing data. A pricing study need not use an assessment.

**Connect the tools because the next decision becomes better, not because every component needs an arrow
to every other component.**

## 3. Why product detail, shown on real numbers

### 3.1 A total describes scale. Detail describes where to act.

A distributor can have a stable parts total while the composition changes considerably. Some ranges
expand, others are bought less often, and a change in mix conceals a change in quantity. A dealer may see
these movements locally without the classification or the wider context needed to interpret them.

Figure 1 is the whole argument on one chart. ISZE parts billed to Isuzu Truck UK in the brake-pad source
category fell from €347,389.58 in calendar 2024 to €302,366.25 in calendar 2025, a reduction of
€45,023.33 or 13.0%. Recorded quantity fell from 4,562 to 3,402, a reduction of 25.4%. *Measured* against
the supplied source-category aggregate, snapshot 19 September 2026; arithmetic rechecked 23 September
2026.

Value fell less than volume. A revenue-only view would suggest a demand problem, a quantity-only view
would suggest a pricing problem, and neither alone tells a distributor where to look. The contextual
fleet is 22,389 ITUK trucks on the stated calendar-2025 spend-per-truck basis. It is not the eligible
population for every pad reference, and it is not divided among dealers.

### 3.2 Where the change sits

[[FIG4]]

Across the three selected source categories — brake pads, rotors and shoes — the combined reduction is
€47,068.77, of which pads account for 95.7%. *Measured*, same source and period.

This changes the starting decision from "braking products are down" to "brake pads are down". It does not
support applying one remedy to every braking product, and it does not make the reduction recoverable
revenue.

### 3.3 Genuine and BVP are one commercial offer

[[FIG5]]

For I Motors (Portugal), combined clutch-kit billing rose from €29,959.87 in calendar 2024 to €31,702.47
in 2025, up €1,742.60 or 5.8%. Genuine billing fell from €2,901.92 to €2,057.86 while BVP rose from
€27,057.95 to €29,644.61. Recorded quantity was almost unchanged: 462 kits in 2024, 461 in 2025.
*Measured*, same source and period.

A Genuine-only view would have suggested a recovery campaign for a line whose total business grew. The
tool exists to prevent that mistake as much as to find opportunities. The 8,176-vehicle Portuguese context
is a market figure on the supplied basis; it is not the eligible fleet for these kits, and it is not
divided among dealers.

### 3.4 Where the concession actually sits in the network

[[FIG6]]

*Measured.* PMS billed €6.65M across 24 distributors in 2025. Of that, 8.4% (€558,851) sat at least 20%
under the reference price and 2.8% at least 30% under. In 2024 the same shares were 13.0% and 4.0% of
€7.11M. The reduction in deep-discount share from 13.0% to 8.4% is a real and favourable movement.

It is also why a campaign-support mechanism has to be designed carefully, and the chart shows the trap.
The distributors with the highest deep-discount share are not the largest ones: Ukraine 28.2% of €203k,
Portugal 20.2% of €338k, IUK 16.9% of €1.27M, Germany 16.6% of €416k. A programme that pays per campaign
without a size floor would spend most of its money in the smallest markets. Chapter 4 sets the floor.

[[FIG7]]

*Measured.* Two observations matter commercially. Brake-pad-front (36.0% of €423k) and blower filter
(25.2% of €374k) carry large deep-discount shares on modest billed bases, which is the shape of a handful
of large deals rather than a broad pricing position. Brake rotors (3.4% of €1.30M) and oil filters (1.5%
of €557k) carry almost none, which means their pricing is settled and their opportunity, if one exists,
is not price.

## 4. The economics, and what the investment must earn

This is the chapter the funding decision rests on. It states four things: the denominator, the hurdle, the
price of a dealer, and what campaign support should cost. It does not forecast a return.

### 4.1 The denominator

| Basis | Value | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Network parts billed to distributors, calendar 2025 | €32,381,311.55 | GPS parts billed across 22 distributors on the supplied basis |
| Of that, the 18 markets carrying a fleet figure | €30,756,771.61 | Same source, rows with a vehicle-in-service count |
| The €100,000 route as a share of one year's network billing | 0.31% | Arithmetic on the lines above |
| The €300,000 route on the same basis | 0.93% | Arithmetic on the lines above |

*Measured*, GPS distributor file, 2025 rows; arithmetic 23 September 2026.

The programme is therefore proposing to spend between three-tenths of one percent and nine-tenths of one
percent of a single year's parts billing. That is the correct size for a pilot and the wrong size for a
platform build. It also means the programme does not need to move the whole business to justify itself,
and it must not be presented as though it will.

### 4.2 The hurdle: how many successful actions the money implies

[[FIG8]]

If a beneficial action is worth €500, the €100,000 envelope requires 200 of them and the €300,000 envelope
requires 600. At €5,000 per action, the same envelopes require 20 and 60. *Proposed and hypothetical
arithmetic*; contribution per action is an assumption, not an estimate of dealer profitability.

Two consequences follow, and they are the reason this section exists:

- **A ten-campaign pilot is evidence, not a return.** Ten campaigns at €2,500 of contribution each is
  €25,000 against a €100,000 envelope. The pilot's job is to establish whether the mechanism produces
  qualifying actions at all.
- **The honest break-even needs forty qualifying actions** at €2,500 contribution per action. That number
  belongs in the funding discussion, not in a footnote after approval.

The hurdle is higher again when internal effort and continuing operation sit outside the envelope. Report
the cash-only comparison and the fully costed comparison separately.

### 4.3 The price of a dealer

| Dealer basis | Count | Consequence for the €15,000 enablement line |
|---|---|---|
| Dealers with a recorded IDS assessment in the ISZE network file | 519 | €28.90 per dealer if spread across the known estate |
| Portugal only | 16 | €937.50 per dealer if confined to the pilot market |
| ITUK and Portugal together | 60 | €250.00 per dealer |
| IDS networks represented in the file | 19 | France 84, Spain 45, UK CV 44, UK LCV 43, Italy 41, Norway 38, Belgium 34, Greece 34, Israel LCV 29, Poland 23, Switzerland 22, Denmark 20, Hungary 19, Portugal 16, Romania 15, Netherlands 9, plus one each in Malta, Cyprus and Luxembourg |

*Measured*, ISZE IDS dealer network file of 20 September 2026, 519 rows across 19 networks; arithmetic
23 September 2026.

This table decides whether dealer enablement is a programme or a gesture. €15,000 spread across 519
dealers buys nothing that survives contact with a dealer principal. €15,000 spent as twenty-five accepted
setup tasks at €600 in two pilot markets buys twenty-five completed onboarding experiences. The
recommendation in chapter 9 is the second.

### 4.4 What campaign support should cost

The mechanism matters more than the headline. Two structures are worth testing and three are not.

| Mechanism | Additive by construction | What it costs on a €120,000 campaign base | Recommended |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unit concession on additional-only volume against a documented baseline | Yes | 20 additional units at a 5% concession on a €30 gross profit is about €120 | Test first |
| Fixed campaign contribution paid on eligible campaign activity | No | Up to the fixed amount | Test |
| Unit concession on all campaign volume including the base | No | Five times the additional-only cost on the same volume | Do not use as standard |
| Payment per submitted record or per disclosed field | No, and it rewards disclosure | Scales with data, not with decisions | Do not use |
| Prize or award for the largest sales total | No | Cannot be compared across business sizes | Do not use |

[[FIG9]]

The arithmetic is the strongest single argument for an additional-only structure. On a 100-unit baseline at
€100 net with €70 parts cost, a 5% concession applied to all 120 units after the campaign produces €3,000
of parts gross profit. Applied only to the 20 additional units, with the baseline retaining its original
price, it produces €3,500. The €500 difference is scope alone and assumes no demand response.
*Hypothetical arithmetic* on stated inputs, not an Isuzu price, offer or forecast.

### 4.5 What the envelope needs to cover

| Line | Proposed | Basis | What has to be demonstrated |
|---|---|---|---|
| Campaign support | €35,000 to €50,000 | 8 to 12 campaigns at €2,500 to €5,000, against the minimum-size filter | Qualifying campaigns exist, and contribution is measured on the same scope before and after |
| Dealer enablement | €15,000 to €25,000 | 25 to 40 accepted setup tasks at €600 | A documented task, an accepted deliverable, and the later eligible-use test |
| Independent evaluation | €10,000 to €15,000 | Two waves at 90 and 180 days, including returns and displacement | The result is published whether it is favourable or not |
| Total additional campaign cash | **€60,000 to €90,000** | Inside the existing €100,000 route, without drawing on the engineering or assurance lines | — |
| Internal analytical preparation | Not costed here | Must be shown as internal time, not assumed free | Named owner and a stated allocation |

*Proposed* allocations against the mechanism in this chapter. They are a recommendation inside the
existing €0 / €100,000 / €300,000 planning envelopes, not a new budget request and not an approved
expenditure.

**What the investment must earn, stated once.** At the paper's own hurdle of €2,500 contribution per
beneficial action, the recommended €60,000 to €90,000 needs 24 to 36 qualifying actions before internal
costs. The pilot will not produce that. It will produce the first real measurement of the qualifying rate,
and that rate is the number on which any expansion should be decided.

## 5. AIM Commercial Atlas

[[FIG10]]

The Atlas turns original part references into a navigable commercial range. The user moves from
distributor or purchasing-account activity into families, product forms and exact references, keeping the
selected period and sales stage intact.

The depth comes from keeping distinctions visible: value against quantity where monetary evidence exists;
continuing against newly active references; Genuine against BVP; product form such as element, housing,
assembly or kit; and the contribution of individual references to a family movement. A validated
successor can be shown beside the original transaction without erasing the code that was actually
purchased.

A product is not an opportunity merely because the participant has not bought it. The Atlas distinguishes
no recorded purchase from a documented range gap, a coverage exclusion, a number change and an irrelevant
application.

[[FIG11]]

Element, housing, assembly and kit can sit near each other in a product tree while having very different
prices and use. Combining them into one average unit price describes a changing mix rather than a change
in price. A missing relationship stays a visible gap in the analysis; it is not filled with a plausible
match based on similar wording.

The result is a saved commercial selection: the source-defined range, the observed pattern, alternative
explanations, missing evidence and a proposed choice. The user may retain the range, investigate further,
prepare a campaign, compare economics or take no action.

## 6. IDS Development Studio

The studio turns the dealer self-assessment into a practical development tool rather than a questionnaire
archive or an action-chasing service. The user explores the actual assessment, sees the meaning behind
each section and selects a development priority it controls.

The first useful output is an understandable assessment profile: actual question wording, response,
section, questionnaire version and available assessment period. Missing, unanswered and inapplicable
items remain distinct. An import date must not replace an absent assessment date. Strengths are shown as
visibly as weaknesses.

A comparison may show a dealer's position against an appropriate, permitted group only where the
questionnaire and scoring basis are compatible. There is no distributor audit score and no new universal
league table.

[[FIG12]]

Progress has three separate meanings, and the tool never lets one update another: completing a development
initiative shows the agreed work occurred; a later comparable assessment shows a change in recorded
answers; a commercial improvement requires its own measurement. The tool therefore remains useful before
a second assessment exists.

## 7. Range, pricing and market tools

[[FIG13]]

### 7.1 Range & Campaign Planner

The planner takes an analytical selection and turns it into a proposed range or campaign decision: which
products belong in the initiative, which participant or vehicle segment is relevant, how the Genuine and
BVP offer should be positioned, and what would demonstrate that the initiative was useful. Its output is a
campaign brief the participant can use. Execution stays in the existing business; Universe does not become
an email-marketing system, an order platform or a quotation desk.

### 7.2 Pricing & Margin Lab

The lab combines supported competitive positioning with user-controlled scenarios. It is a tool for
evaluating a selected offer or range, not a service for answering routine quotations.

Parts gross profit is defined as revenue less the parts purchase cost of the quantities sold. Freight,
duty, overheads, distributor rebates and other operating costs are excluded from that particular measure,
and a separate initiative-economics view considers additional costs once without silently changing the
definition.

The quantity needed to preserve revenue or gross profit is an arithmetic threshold, not a forecast. A
participant may test its own quantities or supported commitments without claiming estimated elasticity.
Sensitive selling prices and margins further down the channel are not mandatory contributions to ISZE: a private
calculation can return only a permitted aggregate, or no shared financial result at all.

### 7.3 PRISM Market Intelligence

PRISM separates measured parts billed, historical vehicles in service and modelled expected demand. Spend
per truck is a measured purchasing rate on a stated fleet basis. Target spend and service expectations
depend on assumptions and technical applicability. A modelled gap is not automatically recoverable sales
and is not a programme business case.

A national vehicle total cannot be allocated to dealers by sales, visits or geography. A part that serves
several vehicle classes remains shared unless exclusive applicability establishes the attribution. A
workshop sample is not a dealer's complete fleet.

For tender intelligence, the useful output is a qualified shortlist: source and observation date, actual
scope, known deadline, relevance and a decision about pursuit. A tender's overall value is not expected
Isuzu parts revenue, and an award is not parts billed.

## 8. Participation: will they actually join?

This chapter states what dealers and distributors are being asked to do, what they receive, and how likely
each step is to happen. The score is a judgement on this design, not a measurement, and it is given so
that the design can be argued with.

### 8.1 The dealer's position

The dealer's reason to participate cannot be a reporting obligation. ISZE has no direct commercial
relationship with the dealer, the distributor already receives the dealer's purchasing information, and
the dealer is being asked to spend time at a parts counter where a customer is standing.

[[FIG14]]

#### What the dealer receives that it cannot get today

| Return | Why it is hard to get otherwise |
|---|---|
| Its actual IDS answers, in the real question wording, with section and version | The dealer has the questionnaire but not an explorable view of its own record |
| A development priority it chooses, with milestones and evidence it controls | Existing assessment rounds give a score, not a plan the dealer owns |
| A prepared own-business review of its recorded purchasing, with a correction route | Today this arrives, if at all, as a national or distributor-level summary |
| A visible record of who owes the next step, including ISZE and the distributor | Today the dealer cannot see when it is waiting on someone else |

The first two are the strongest dealer assets and they are under-used in the current design. Of the 519
dealers in the ISZE network file, 334 carry a recorded IDS score today. Figure 15 shows their
distribution: a minimum of 0, a maximum of 100 and an average of 77.5. That is roughly two-thirds of the
known estate with an individual result already on file, and it is the only place in this programme where
ISZE holds something a dealer will want to look at that it cannot easily assemble itself.

[[FIG15]]

#### How the dealer joins, step by step

1. **Approach through the distributor.** The distributor chooses the invitation. ISZE does not approach a dealer directly.
2. **Arrive prepared.** The first item opened is a review built from information already held. There is no form, no portal registration and no upload in the first session.
3. **Ask for correction, not for data.** The dealer confirms or corrects one interpretation. "Your reading is wrong" and "no action is needed" are both successful outcomes.
4. **Offer the IDS profile.** The dealer sees its own assessment in a usable form and chooses one development priority.
5. **Record the plan.** Milestones, owner and evidence, with the record staying with the dealer.
6. **Propose the next eligible task, not the next report.** The repeat invitation depends on an actual commercial decision occurring.

#### Honest assessment of the first ten invitations

| Step | Score /100 | Why that number |
|---|---:|---|
| Dealer accepts the first prepared review | 55 | Reasonable, because it costs ten minutes and arrives finished. It competes with a customer standing at the counter. |
| Dealer corrects the interpretation | 70 | Correction is low-cost and self-interested. This is the most reliable step in the design. |
| Dealer opens and uses its own IDS profile | 65 | The strongest hook in the programme: its own answers, in the real wording, with a priority it chooses. |
| Dealer returns for a second eligible task | 30 | No cash, no visibility gain, no recurring trigger. Habit does not form on goodwill alone. |
| Dealer shares anything beyond a correction | 20 | Requires a reason that has not yet been created. Nothing in the design currently supplies it. |
| **Dealer joins as designed today** | **45** | The design is credible in its first three steps and unproven after them. |

#### What raises the dealer score

Three changes, in order of effect. **Make the IDS profile the opening, not a later step**, so the first
thing a dealer sees is its own data rather than a supplier's reading of it. **Fund a bounded setup task**,
so the dealer's own time has a price and the distributor has something to offer rather than something to
ask for. **Make the return visible**, so the dealer can see the answer, the decision and who owes the next
step, because invisible follow-through is what ended the previous data requests.

### 8.2 The distributor's position

The distributor's position is stronger and simpler. It already has an obligation to report. It already
has a rebate linked to reporting. The change being proposed is what comes back.

[[FIG16]]

#### What the distributor receives

| Return | Frequency | Decision it supports |
|---|---|---|
| Prepared national or network review | Monthly, after a usable submission | Which accounts and ranges deserve attention |
| Product detail behind a movement | On the selected case | Which references and product forms explain a change |
| A range or campaign brief | On the selected initiative | Whether to develop, narrow or protect the offer |
| A tender proposal framework | On a qualified opportunity | Whether to pursue and what support to request |
| A reviewed development record per dealer | On IDS work | Where to direct its own network-development effort |

#### How the distributor joins

1. **A director-level conversation, not a data request.** The opening statement is that the recurring report is reused and nothing additional is required for the core review.
2. **Agree two or three definitions once.** Covered accounts, channels and products. These are reused rather than re-requested.
3. **Receive the first prepared review.** Built from data already supplied, with the question behind it stated.
4. **Choose one case.** One range, one account pattern or one campaign, with a named owner on the distributor side.
5. **Extend only where a case needs it.** Additional detail is requested for a defined purpose with a named recipient and a stated return.

#### Honest assessment

| Step | Score /100 | Why that number |
|---|---:|---|
| Distributor accepts the opening | 75 | Cheap, adds no reporting burden, and the rebate floor already exists. |
| Distributor uses the review internally | 45 | Depends entirely on whether one review answers a question the distributor already had. |
| Distributor selects a case with a named owner | 50 | Requires management attention, which is the scarcest resource in a distributor's year. |
| Distributor shares account-level detail beyond the current report | 35 | Genuinely useful and genuinely sensitive. Needs the boundary in chapter 10 to be real, not promised. |
| Distributor uses Universe to develop its own dealers | 40 | This is the highest-value outcome and the one the design supports least well today. |
| Distributor co-funds participation | 25 | No precedent. Should not be assumed and should not be requested in the first year. |
| **Distributor joins as designed today** | **55** | A credible, low-cost start with an unproven second step. |

#### What raises the distributor score

The gap between the 75 for accepting and the 45 for using is the whole problem. Two changes close it.
**Answer a question the distributor already has**, rather than presenting a general capability, because a
review that is merely available is a review that is not read. **Make the distributor's own dealer
development the visible output**, because that is the only framing in which the review serves the
distributor's commercial interest rather than ISZE's.

### 8.3 The base rate, and what it means

519 dealer self-assessments exist today across 19 networks. That is what this network produces when an
obligation is attached to a dealer-facing requirement. Anything in this programme that scores below that
base rate needs a reason the participant can feel, and the only two reasons available are money and a
visible improvement in its own business.

### 8.4 Do not do these

| Do not | Why |
|---|---|
| Pay per submitted record, per row or per disclosed field | Rewards disclosure volume rather than useful work |
| Make ordinary service conditional on contribution | Turns a normal service into a bargaining instrument and destroys the premise |
| Build a public dealer league table | Turnover favours large businesses and self-assessment scores have different coverage |
| Ask for dealer resale prices by default | Not needed for the core review and the most sensitive item on the list |
| Approach a dealer directly | The distributor owns the relationship and the audience decision |
| Count logins or uploads as participation | Neither is evidence that a decision improved |

## 9. What to build first, what to fund, and what to stop

### 9.1 The sequence

| Stage | Result | Evidence to move on |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Agree the scope and the portfolio | A clear five-tool portfolio and the written exclusion of routine services | This paper, the presentation and the showcase explain the same proposition |
| 2. Pin the classification release | One named file, version and build date | Reproduce a selected family and product-form view from the pinned release |
| 3. Open two prepared views | An Atlas view and an IDS profile, both prepared before the first invitation | A participant completes a real task and finds it clearer than the existing review |
| 4. Run the dealer sample | Ten dealers across two markets, including one that is reluctant to add reporting work | Correction, useful output, total effort and reasons for non-use, all recorded |

### 9.3 The go / no-go and the stop condition

**Go** if, at the pilot review, a real participant completed a real analytical task, the output was correct
on its source, and the participant can name the decision it changed. **No-go** if the only evidence is that
a screen rendered, a file arrived, or somebody said the demonstration looked good.

**It stops.** If, after two eligible cycles, no participant can name a decision the tool changed, or if a
confidentiality or identity weakness is found that cannot be closed, the programme stops. It does not
pause, it does not add a report, and it does not broaden the participant population to look for a better
sample. If the incentive that currently produces the file were removed tomorrow, the honest test is
whether anyone opens the tool anyway.

## 10. Delivery, ownership and risk

### 10.1 Ownership

The business owner can define the commercial question, design the information exchange, prepare analytical
examples and establish what a useful output should contain. Existing AIM, Atlas, PRISM and IDS work is
inspected and reused where fit for purpose rather than reproduced as parallel calculations. Specialist
work is bounded: access isolation, durable shared records, reliable imports, recovery, or an agreed
connection to company systems. Each is a task with an accepted deliverable and a handover.

### 10.2 What no amount of code closes

There is no backend on this machine. There is no shared case service, no production identity and no
attachment storage. The prototype deliberately refuses an authenticated identity rather than inventing
authority, and says so on every result. A browser-side allowlist is not access control and is not described
as one anywhere.

The access rules that matter — only the authorised distributor sees its selected accounts and terms, only
the dealer sees its own permitted record, and no distributor sees another's submissions — are enforced by
the operating application and by the contents of exports, not by hidden menus and filters. Until that is
implemented and independently reviewed, the first shared use stays distributor-led and document-based.

### 10.3 Copyright and competition boundaries

Universe is a commercial programme offered by ISZE to European distributors and through them to dealers.
Under the European motor-vehicle block exemption framework, agreements using market-share thresholds of
30% or 40% are valid until 31 May 2028, after which the motor-vehicle-specific regime lapses unless
replaced. Nothing in this paper is a legal determination, and none of it has been reviewed for
competition-law compliance.

Three design rules follow and are proposed as standing constraints:

- **No exclusivity in exchange for information.** Participation is not conditioned on purchasing, on range commitments or on refraining from a competing offer.
- **No price coordination between distributors.** Universe compares a distributor to its own history and to permitted aggregate context. It does not transmit one distributor's terms, plans or intentions to another.
- **No resale-price instruction.** ISZE sets its net price to the distributor. Universe does not prescribe, and does not retain, dealer resale prices.

This must be reviewed by the responsible function before any shared output is used with a participant.

### 10.4 Privacy and the data boundary

The recurring standard is price-free. Selling prices, resale margins, customer identities, vehicle
identifiers, full inventory and workshop job cards are not silently added to it. Quantity-only data can
support valuable analysis without becoming recorded resale revenue. Where a financial outcome is needed,
a locally calculated aggregate may be sufficient, and an aggregate is not automatically anonymous.

Corrections have their own rule: a corrected submission replaces its declared predecessor for the affected
scope, identical re-imports add no activity, annual controls are not added to monthly observations, and
national controls are not added to their dealer detail.

### 11.2 Inferred

Value fell less than volume in the ITUK pad line, which means mix, price and coverage deserve examination
before any concession is considered. Combined Genuine and BVP grew in Portugal while Genuine fell, which
means a Genuine-only view would have produced the wrong campaign. The distributors with the highest
deep-discount shares are not the largest ones, which means a campaign programme without a size floor would
concentrate in small markets.

### 11.3 Modelled, and labelled as such

The quantity thresholds, the contribution figures and the hurdle table are arithmetic on stated inputs.
They are not estimates of customer response. Where a cost is unknown it is printed as unknown; an empty
cost is not zero.

### 11.4 What elasticity evidence exists, and how weak it is

There is no reliable measured price response in the current evidence base, and the paper does not pretend
otherwise. It is worth stating what has been measured, because silence lets a reader assume whatever
responsiveness suits the conclusion being argued.

| Product family, 2024 to 2025 | Price effect on quantity | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Maintenance service | Steeply negative, but with a large standard error and almost no explanatory power | Direction only. Not a usable elasticity. |
| Brakes | Flat to slightly positive, with a large standard error | Inconclusive. |
| Lubrication | Not identified, because the list-price movement was nearly uniform across the pairs | Use the median quantity move instead. |

The measurement is of the stocking response from ISZE to the distributor, not dealer-market demand, and it
does not control for demand shocks. Approximately €233,000 of the brakes change sits in price-up with
volume-down, €172,000 in price-up with volume-up, and €335,000 in volume-down with price flat. *Measured*,
year-on-year pairs on 2024 and 2025, direction and rough magnitude only.

The practical rule is therefore: **no scenario in this programme forecasts a demand response.** A threshold
says what would have to happen. It does not say what will.

### 11.5 Not known, and printed as not known

The customer's reason for purchasing elsewhere. Whether a distributor's dealer network covers the vehicles
that buy the part. The gross-profit effect of a range change before the campaign is run. The
qualifying-campaign rate that chapter 4 needs. Whether a dealer will return after the first task.

## 13. Reference: periods, populations and identities

Keep calendar and fiscal periods distinct: Isuzu's fiscal year runs April to March, while the historical
examples here compare full calendar years. The billing reference for 2026 runs through 30 June 2026 and is
a half year, not a full-year observation. A missing month is not filled with zero.

ITUK is not IUK, and UMI is not UTI. Country aggregation must not silently combine their fleets or
purchasing behaviour. A purchasing account is not automatically a physical workshop, and a dealer company
can have several sites. The original transacted part reference remains available alongside its
classification and any separately governed successor relationship; a later relationship must not silently
rewrite a historical transaction.
