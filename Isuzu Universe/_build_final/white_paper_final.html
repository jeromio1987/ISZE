The paper on one page

What Universe is. A programme that gives distributors and dealers tools worth opening and, in return, earns the parts and aftersales information that makes the next answer sharper. The tools are the offer. The exchange is the point.

Why now. ISZE has asked its distributors for information for years without returning enough visible value to keep the request alive. A reporting obligation can start an exchange; only a useful tool keeps it going. The 2025 arrangement that tied a share of the normal distributor rebate to a performance submission is the floor, not the mechanism.

What it is not. Availability questions, part and Best Value lookups, day-to-day quotation handling and support administration stay in the existing business. They are not modules of Universe, not rewards for taking part, and not bargaining chips for more data.

1.1What a Deal of the Day is

ISZE helps a distributor prepare a specific, time-limited commercial offer: a reasoned product selection, the right audience, transparent economics, and a way to learn from what follows. The value is in the preparation behind the offer, not in the discount.

It is the most concrete thing Universe does, and the one a distributor's parts manager understands in thirty seconds. That is why it opens this paper. It is also the one that spends money, so it has to be designed so that the money buys evidence.

Not to be confused with Don't Lose the Deal (chapter 4), which is Isuzu Truck UK's existing support for competitive quotations. The initials are the same and the products are different.

1.2Which units get the discount

Illustrative A distributor sells 100 units a period at €100 net, with €70 of parts cost: €3,000 of gross profit. A Deal of the Day lifts volume to 120 units at a 5% concession. If every unit gets the discount, gross profit stays at €3,000, so the 20 extra units have bought nothing. If only the 20 extra units get the discount and the baseline keeps its normal price, gross profit is €3,500. The €500 difference comes from the scope of the concession alone and assumes no change in demand.

1.3The size floor

A Deal of the Day costs preparation and support whether it is large or small. With the same illustrative economics (a 30% gross margin and a 5% concession on the extra units, so 25 cents of gross profit on every extra euro sold), the chart shows how much extra business one Deal of the Day has to produce just to pay back its own support.

At €150,000 of eligible billing, €2,500 of support needs 6.7% extra sales and €5,000 needs 13.3%. At €50,000 the same support needs 20% and 40%, which no discount of this size should be expected to deliver. Proposed A Deal of the Day qualifies for support only above €150,000 of eligible billing.

1.4Where concession already sits

Measured Maintenance parts (PMS) billed €6.65M across 24 distributors in 2025 on lines carrying a reference price. Of that, 8.4% (€558,851) was billed at least 20% under the reference price and 2.8% at least 30% under. In 2024 the same shares were 13.0% and 4.0% of €7.11M. The fall is real and favourable.

Inferred Ukraine, Portugal and Germany carry high deep-discount shares on modest billed bases. A support programme without a size floor would spend most of its money in the smallest markets, because that is where the share is highest. The data argues for the floor; caution alone would not.

1.5A candidate for the pilot: Isuzu UK air filters

Measured Air filters billed to Isuzu UK rose from €82k in 2024 to €116k in 2025, while the share billed at least 20% under the reference price fell from 38.6% to 15.1%. This line already has a measured concession history, at a distributor where account-level detail exists. It is the right place to test whether a prepared Deal of the Day adds anything a concession alone does not.

Proposed On its own, at €116k, the line sits below the €150,000 floor. It qualifies as part of a wider filter selection for the same distributor (Isuzu UK filters together billed €406k in 2025), or if the floor is set lower. That choice belongs to the funding decision. Nothing has been agreed with the distributor.

1.6Why thresholds, not forecasts

Measured ISZE has measured how distributor purchasing responded to list-price moves between 2024 and 2025. For maintenance-service parts the response is negative, but the uncertainty is half the size of the estimate and it explains less than 1% of the movement. For brakes it is flat to slightly positive, with an uncertainty three times the size of the estimate. For lubrication it cannot be measured at all, because the price moved almost uniformly. Of the brakes change, roughly €233k sits where price rose and volume fell, €172k where both rose, and €335k where volume fell with price flat. This is ISZE-to-distributor purchasing, not dealer demand, and demand shocks are not controlled for. The evidence is too weak to forecast from, which is why every Deal of the Day in this paper is judged against a threshold: what would have to happen, not what will.

2.1The parts classification system

Every example in this chapter rests on a new parts classification system built by Jérôme Van der Pluym. It places every Isuzu part number in a family, a product group and a product form (element, housing, assembly or kit) and keeps Genuine and Best Value side by side.

It answers the question a total cannot: what is really changing in this parts business, and which references explain it? A distributor's parts business can be flat while its composition moves a great deal, and a change in mix hides a change in quantity. The classification is what lets a parts manager move from "our parts business moved" to "brake pads moved, and these are the references behind it".

2.2Isuzu Truck UK brake pads

Measured Parts billed to Isuzu Truck UK in brake pads fell from €347,389.58 in 2024 to €302,366.25 in 2025, a reduction of €45,023.33. Units fell from 4,562 to 3,402. Read the two halves together: a revenue view suggests a demand problem, a quantity view suggests a price problem, and neither is the right first question. The gap between the two percentages is the first thing a parts manager should look at. The national figure of 22,389 Isuzu trucks in the UK is context, not the population that buys each pad, and it is not divided among dealers.

2.3Where the decline sits

Inferred The starting sentence changes from "braking products are down" to "brake pads are down", which is something a distributor can act on. It does not support one remedy across all braking products, and it does not turn the reduction into recoverable revenue.

2.4Portugal clutch kits: a mistake prevented

Measured Combined clutch-kit billing rose from €29,959.87 to €31,702.47 (+5.8%). Genuine fell from €2,901.92 to €2,057.86, while Best Value rose from €27,057.95 to €29,644.61. Units were flat: 462 kits, then 461.

Inferred A Genuine-only view would have proposed a recovery action for a business that grew. Here the tool earns its place by preventing a mistake rather than finding an opportunity. Genuine and Best Value are one commercial offer, and only the classification shows both answers at once.

2.5The ranked family view

Measured Two families carry a large deep-discount share on a modest base: front brake pads at 36.0% of €423k and blower filters at 25.2% of €374k. That is the shape of a handful of large deals, not a broad pricing position. Two large families carry almost none: brake rotors at 3.4% of €1.30M and oil filters at 1.5% of €557k.

Inferred A family whose pricing is settled has no price opportunity. If it has an opportunity at all, it lies in range, application or coverage. Saying so stops a settled line being offered up as a Deal of the Day candidate.

2.6What the classification refuses to do

  1. A product is not an opportunity just because a participant has not bought it. No recorded purchase, a documented range gap, a coverage exclusion, a number change and an irrelevant application are five different findings.
  2. An element, a housing, an assembly and a kit are never averaged into one unit price. Doing so describes a changing mix and calls it a price change.
  3. A missing relationship stays a visible gap. It is never filled with a plausible match based on similar wording.
  4. Classification does not establish fitment. A successor relationship says one number replaces another. Whether the replacement fits a given truck is validated separately before any alternative is recommended.

One condition before any family label reaches a distributor. The classification must be fixed to one named release, with a version and a build date. Until then, family labels are for use inside ISZE only, because two people in the same meeting could open two versions and get two answers for the same part number.

3.1The scope boundary

Universe is not a better support desk. A normal service is not a reward for participation, and participation is not the price of a normal service.

Outside Universe: stays in the existing businessInside Universe: a commercial capability beyond that service
When will this part arrive?Recurring, evidenced constraints may inform a range study. No arrival-enquiry workflow is built.
Is a Best Value alternative available?Combined Genuine and Best Value positioning, product composition and range development.
Can you handle this quotation?Competitive evidence and transparent economics for a chosen pricing or range decision.
Can you follow up this training action?An explorable Dealer Standards profile and a development plan the dealer chooses.
Can you submit or administer this tender?Relevant fleet and tender intelligence, and a reasoned decision on which opportunities to pursue.

The boundary matters commercially for two reasons. First, a programme that also answers availability questions will be judged on availability response times, and it will lose that comparison to a system that already answers them. Second, the boundary is the offer. The sentence that changes how a distributor's parts manager reads the approach is: we are not asking you to feed a support desk, and the support desk does not get better or worse depending on what you send us.

A Dealer Standards plan needs no pricing data, and a pricing study needs no assessment. The tools are connected where the next decision gets better, and only there.

3.4Market and revenue analytics

The platform brings four views together that were previously held apart: the fleet (Isuzu vehicles in service by market and model), target spend per truck against parts actually billed, maintenance-parts revenue capture by vehicle and service interval, and competitor price monitoring from public online prices. It also carries an early-warning view on distributors and dealers and the public-tender feed described in chapter 4. Its job is to show where revenue is being left on the table, and to say when the evidence is not strong enough to act on.

Measured For 2025 the model puts the ceiling on target spend at €55,359,060 across the markets with a fleet figure, against €32,381,311.55 of parts actually billed. Of that ceiling, €22,278,000 (40.24%) comes from 8 markets where no measured purchasing rate exists and a flat €250 per truck is assumed.

The ceiling is not revenue. It is what a market could spend on parts on a modelled basis, not money available to ISZE, and two-fifths of it is an assumption. Multiplying it by a recovery percentage would invent a revenue case this paper refuses to make. Its correct use is comparative: this market sits at this share of its own ceiling, the next market at that share, and the order is the decision input.

Three rules keep the numbers honest. A national vehicle total is never allocated to dealers by sales, visits or geography. A part that serves several vehicle classes stays shared unless exclusive fitment proves which model it belongs to. And a modelled gap is not recoverable sales and not a business case.

3.5The Dealer Standards Studio

The Studio turns the dealer self-assessment into a development tool rather than a questionnaire archive. What a dealer would see, in order:

  1. Its own sections (premises, organisation, service, parts and accessories, vehicle sales), each with the number of questions answered, unanswered and not applicable.
  2. The actual questions as worded, with its own recorded answer against each. Strengths are shown as visibly as weaknesses.
  3. Why a chosen question is worth attention. A comparison against a group is offered only where the questionnaire and scoring are compatible.
  4. A development priority it chooses, with milestones, an owner and the evidence it decides to keep.

Three records, and none updates another. The assessment is what was recorded. The development record is what the dealer agreed to do. A later reassessment is a new record. Completing a milestone does not amend an answer, raise a score or prove a commercial outcome. That is what keeps the tool useful before a second assessment round exists. There is no distributor score derived from dealer assessments and no public league table.

3.6Pricing, range and offers

Pricing & Margin Lab. Combines competitive positioning with scenarios the user controls. Parts gross profit is revenue less the parts purchase cost of the quantities sold; freight, duty, overheads and distributor rebates are excluded from that measure, and any extra costs are shown once in a separate view. The quantity needed to preserve revenue or gross profit is an arithmetic threshold, not a forecast. Selling prices and margins further down the channel are never mandatory contributions.

Range & Offer Planner. Takes an Atlas selection and turns it into a proposed range decision or Deal of the Day brief: which products, for which accounts or vehicle segments, how Genuine and Best Value are positioned, and what would show that it worked. Execution stays in the existing business. Universe does not become an order platform or a quotation desk.

4.1Don't Lose the Deal at Isuzu Truck UK

Don't Lose the Deal is Isuzu Truck UK's existing programme for competitive quotations: support that helps a dealer keep a job when the customer has a cheaper alternative in front of them. It runs on a memorandum of understanding, a rebate credit note that moves the money, and Japan-side sign-off.

Measured Five credits carry the programme name: three in 2023 and early 2024 that together make €30,000 for 2023, then €30,000 in February 2025 and €30,000 in March 2026. This is a recurring arrangement, not a one-off. €30,000 is 0.54% of the €5.56M ISZE billed to Isuzu Truck UK in 2025, which is why the programme is affordable there. The first two 2023 credits carry a slightly different label. One question to Isuzu Truck UK confirms whether they are the same programme, and it should be asked before any baseline for a rollout is fixed.

4.2The label, and the missing case

The programme name survives in the reference typed on the original invoice records, but the working billing file that everyone searches drops that field. A search there finds nothing, and that is how the programme came to look as if it left no trace. The chain from programme name to billing line is broken in the middle, and a broken chain can be repaired: keep the field.

The deeper gap does not move. Five labelled payments, zero recorded cases: not one part reference, dealer, competitor quote, price gap or won-or-lost outcome. A credit note proves that support was paid. It does not prove that the customer was kept or that anything happened that would not have happened anyway. Anyone who reads rebate volume as a result is reading a payment as an outcome.

4.3Rolling it out to every dealer

The intent is to run Don't Lose the Deal for every ISZE dealer, with Isuzu Truck UK as the template. That makes the gap urgent: rolled out as it stands, every new market buys the payment and inherits the inability to judge it. The cheapest moment to fix the record is now, while there is one arrangement to amend rather than nineteen.

Proposed At €30,000 per market a year. The money is affordable. Judging it is the harder part, and three constraints decide the design:

  • The sign-off path, not the cash, is very likely the binding limit. Depending on case size, a network programme generates between roughly eight and 160 approval events a month. The design answer is to agree the envelope once and approve cases locally against a written rule. That is only safe together with the case record below.
  • Cheap cuts both ways. A cost small enough to approve without argument is also small enough to approve without evaluation. Three years of the full network at this level, unmeasured, is about 5% of one year's parts spent on a bet nobody can judge.
  • An uncapped rollout buys activity, not evidence. Record first, then scale.

4.5The public-tender feed

Measured 689 notices reduce to 619 distinct opportunities, of which 286 carry an aftersales signal (a parts, service or maintenance element a distributor could supply). 199 are in the action queue and 45 are due within fourteen days. Decisions recorded so far: 2 attack, 204 selective, 75 watch, 338 ignore. 588 of the 619 opportunities carry no value figure, and the paper does not invent one. No outcomes are recorded, so the feed cannot yet show a win rate.

Four rules apply. A tender's advertised value is not expected Isuzu parts revenue, and an award is not parts billed. A notice count is not a pipeline; the deliverable is a qualified shortlist. Every opportunity carries its source and the date it was seen, because a distributor who chases a closed tender stops reading the feed. And declining is a valid outcome, with the reason recorded.

The give and the ask. ISZE gives country-relevant opportunities with the aftersales content made visible early enough to act on, and, where it fits, a combined vehicle and aftersales proposal with a headquarters brief that makes the requested decision unmistakable. ISZE asks for one line when a tender is won, lost or ignored.

4.6One decision closes both

In both Don't Lose the Deal and the tender feed, the event that matters happens outside ISZE, and the only trace on ISZE's side is a part that eventually ships. ISZE can do everything up to the outcome and nothing after it. Agree to record outcomes, one line per closed case, in one shape both can read. It is not a system, a report or a permission. It is an agreement that when a case closes, one line gets written down.

The sentence a dealer principal hears. "A review prepared from your recorded purchases" can sound like "my supplier's supplier is looking at my book". The design answers it directly. The review reaches the dealer only through its distributor, it covers what the distributor already receives, and the dealer decides what, if anything, goes further.

5.2What ISZE already holds for dealers

Inferred This is the strongest dealer asset in the programme: the only place where ISZE holds something a dealer wants to look at and cannot easily assemble for itself. So the design opens with it. The first thing a dealer sees is its own assessment, not a supplier's reading of its business. The scores are self-assessments from one round with no per-dealer date. They are context, not a performance measure.

5.3How a dealer joins

  1. Through the distributor. The distributor chooses whom to invite. ISZE does not approach a dealer directly.
  2. Its own Dealer Standards profile first. The dealer sees its own answers and chooses one development priority.
  3. A prepared review, no form. Built from information already held. No registration and no upload in the first session.
  4. Ask for a correction, not for data. "Your reading is wrong" and "no action needed" are both successful outcomes.
  5. Record the plan. Milestones, owner and evidence, and the record stays with the dealer.
  6. Propose the next task, not the next report. A repeat invitation depends on an actual commercial decision.

A funded, bounded setup task (€600, section 1.7) gives the dealer's time a price and gives the distributor something to offer rather than something to ask for.

5.4How a distributor joins

  1. A director-level conversation, not a data request. The recurring report is reused; nothing extra is required for the core review.
  2. Agree two or three definitions once. Covered accounts, channels and products.
  3. Receive the first prepared review, with the question behind it stated.
  4. Choose one case: one range, one account pattern or one Deal of the Day, with a named owner on the distributor's side.
  5. Extend only where a case needs it, for a defined purpose, with a named recipient and a stated return.

Two things turn acceptance into use. Answer a question the distributor already has, because a review that is merely available is not read. And make the distributor's own dealer development the visible output, because that is the framing in which the review serves the distributor's interest rather than ISZE's.

5.5Open for decision: a participation score

Proposed, not decided Whether participation should carry a score, what it counts and what it unlocks is still open and is not settled in this paper. No scores are given here. If a score is introduced, four principles would hold:

  • It counts useful exchange and decisions, not data volume, logins or uploads.
  • Each participant sees its own score. There is no public league table.
  • Ordinary service never depends on it.
  • Candidate components, for discussion: the agreed report on time and complete; corrections made; maintenance and service data shared; a tool used for a named decision; outcomes recorded.

6.1The denominator

Measured ISZE billed €32,381,311.55 of parts to 22 distributors in 2025. The €100,000 route is 0.31% of that and the €300,000 route 0.93%. The programme does not need to move the whole business to justify itself, and it must not be presented as if it will.

6.2The hurdle

Illustrative At €500 per beneficial action the €100,000 route needs 200 of them and the €300,000 route 600. At €5,000, 20 and 60. The honest break-even for €100,000 is 40 qualifying actions at €2,500 each. That number belongs in the funding discussion, not in a footnote after approval. A pilot of ten Deals of the Day at €2,500 each is €25,000 against €100,000: it establishes whether the mechanism produces qualifying actions at all. The hurdle is higher again once internal time is included, so report the cash-only and the fully costed comparison separately.

6.3The price of a dealer

Inferred €15,000 spread across 519 dealers is €28.90 each and buys nothing that survives contact with a dealer principal. The same €15,000 spent as twenty-five accepted setup tasks at €600 in two pilot markets buys twenty-five completed onboarding experiences. Same money, completely different product. The recommendation is the second.

6.4The existing rebate floor

Measured In 2025 a share of the normal distributor rebate was tied to a major performance submission, and the submission arrived. That is the floor: the file exists because the rebate depends on it. Nothing in that arrangement gives a distributor a reason to open anything, correct anything or send anything the rebate does not name. The euro value of that share is not stated in this paper. It should be added before the funding discussion, so the programme is weighed against the incentive already being paid.

6.5Which route

Proposed Start at the €100,000 route and re-point its Deal of the Day element as set out in section 1.7. The €300,000 route adds dealer enablement and resilience before anyone knows how often a Deal of the Day qualifies, so it should wait for that rate. Nothing here is a new budget request or an approved expenditure. It is a recommendation inside the existing €0, €100,000 and €300,000 planning envelopes.

7.3Go, no-go and stop

Go if, at the pilot review, a real participant completed a real analytical task, the output was correct against its source, and the participant can name the decision it changed.

No-go if the only evidence is that a screen rendered, a file arrived, or somebody said the demonstration looked good.

Stop if, after two eligible cycles, no participant can name a decision the tool changed, or if a confidentiality or identity weakness cannot be closed. The programme stops. It does not pause, add a report, or widen the population to look for a better sample.

The honest test underneath all of it: if the incentive that produces the file today were removed tomorrow, would anyone open the tool anyway?

7.4Ownership, and the unowned answer

The commercial question, the design of the exchange, the analytical examples and the definition of a useful output are owned inside ISZE Parts. Specialist work is bounded, each piece a task with an accepted deliverable and a handover: access isolation, durable shared records, reliable imports, recovery, and any connection to company systems. Existing classification, analytics and Dealer Standards work is reused where fit for purpose, not rebuilt.

What no amount of software closes is the answer. The product pays off when ISZE answers a participant's question, and nobody has yet been named, given hours, or funded to do that. Software is cheap at the margin and a person's time is not. The risk is that the cheap half gets built, the expensive half gets deferred, and the product arrives looking complete while the thing it was for never happens.

7.5Delivery, competition and privacy

Delivery. There is no production system yet: no shared case service, no production identity, no attachment storage. The access rules that matter (only the authorised distributor sees its own accounts and terms, only the dealer sees its own record, no distributor sees another's submissions) must be enforced by the operating application and by what exports contain, not by hidden menus. Until that is built and independently reviewed, the first shared use stays distributor-led and document-based.

Competition law. Under the European motor-vehicle block exemption framework, the current regime runs until 31 May 2028. Nothing here is a legal determination, and the design has not yet been reviewed for competition-law compliance. Three standing rules are proposed: no exclusivity in exchange for information; no price coordination between distributors, so Universe compares a distributor with its own history and permitted aggregates and never passes one distributor's terms or plans to another; and no resale-price instruction, so ISZE sets its net price to the distributor and Universe neither prescribes nor keeps dealer resale prices. The responsible function reviews this before any shared output is used with a participant.

Privacy. The recurring exchange is price-free by default. Selling prices, resale margins, customer identities, vehicle identifiers, full inventory and workshop job cards are never added silently. Where a financial outcome is needed, a locally calculated aggregate may be enough, and an aggregate is not automatically anonymous.
