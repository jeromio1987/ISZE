The paper on one page

The argument builds in five steps. Each one is the reason for the next.

  1. We cannot see our own aftermarket. ISZE, part of Isuzu Motors, billed €32,381,311.55 of parts to 22 distributors in 2025. Only 6 of them sent any sales-to-dealer data for 2025, and only one sent it by named dealer (17.2% of billing). Some do not track it at all. We already tie 20% of the normal distributor rebate to KPI reporting, and it buys a file, not a habit.
  2. The next big thing is sales-to-dealer data, as an exchange. The distributor knows which dealer bought which parts and which dealer sold which trucks. ISZE knows the parts, the service intervals and the fleet. Together, and only together, that shows which dealer is losing maintenance business. We give the distributor that view of its own network, so it can develop its dealers, and it sends us the data that makes the view possible.
  3. Dealers bring the workshop. Maintenance records are the one source that turns modelled demand into observed demand. Dealers share them when they get something back: their own Dealer Standards profile, a service-due list, a workshop benchmark and better offers.
  4. Deal of the Day is the engine. A prepared, time-limited offer that pays only on extra volume. Measuring extra volume needs a baseline, and the baseline is sales-to-dealer data. The data ask is built into the offer rather than bolted on.
  5. It has to pay back. One point of maintenance-parts capture across the modelled network is worth about €249k of revenue a year. The €100,000 route is 0.31% of one year's billing. The money is released in stages, against gates that measure data, use and return.
Decision this monthRecommendation
1. Re-point the KPI rebateLink the existing 20% rebate share to a monthly sales-to-dealer standard (part × dealer), in tiers. No new money.
2. Fund Deal of the DayA named line of €60,000 to €90,000 inside the €100,000 route. Only Deals of the Day above €150,000 of eligible billing, and only with a sales-to-dealer baseline.

What this paper does not claim. No realised return, no approved budget, no forecast of extra sales, and no participant has agreed to anything. Figures marked illustrative are arithmetic on stated inputs, not forecasts.

Chapter 1

What an OEM cannot see

Isuzu Motors Europe sells parts to distributors, and distributors sell them to dealers. We know exactly what leaves our warehouse. After the distributor's invoice, our view goes dark.

1.1Where our sight stops

Measured In 2025 ISZE billed €32,381,311.55 of parts to 22 distributors. 6 of them sent any 2025 sales-to-dealer data: together €18.4M, or 57.0% of billing. Five of those six send national quantities per part number and nothing below it. Only Isuzu Truck UK sent data by named dealer: 64 dealers, for one year, covering 17.2% of billing. The other 15 distributors send nothing below the invoice. Nothing reaches ISZE from a dealer's sales to its customers, and ISZE holds one dealer's workshop records, obtained once.

Reported Some distributors do not track their sales to dealers at part level at all. For them the problem is not willingness to share: there is nothing to share yet.

1.2What we already pay to see

Measured Since 2025, 20% of the normal distributor rebate is linked to reporting a set of major KPIs. It works as a floor: the file arrives, because the rebate depends on it. It does not buy part-by-dealer detail, a monthly rhythm, or anything the distributor uses itself, so the file is as thin as the condition allows. For years ISZE has asked for information and returned too little visible value for the request to stay alive.

A reporting obligation can start an exchange. Only a useful tool keeps it going. The test for everything in this paper: if the rebate condition disappeared tomorrow, would the distributor still send the data, because it wants what comes back?

1.3What not seeing costs

Blind spotWhat it means today
Which dealer is losing maintenance businessImpossible to say outside one market. No distributor sends trucks sold or serviced per dealer, so no dealer can be measured against its own fleet.
How much of the maintenance market ISZE suppliesModelled ISZE supplied about 19% of the modelled need for the maintenance-parts families in 18 markets in 2025. Where the other 81% goes (aftermarket, other channels, or services not done) cannot be seen.
The size of the prize40% of the modelled target-spend ceiling (€22.28M of €55.36M) rests on a flat €250-per-truck assumption, because those markets report no purchasing rate.
Whether a price change workedPrice response can only be measured on ISZE-to-distributor stocking, not on dealer demand, so pricing decisions run on thresholds, not evidence.
Whether support paid off€90,000 of Don't Lose the Deal credits since 2023 and 199 tender opportunities in the queue, with zero recorded outcomes.

Inferred Every one of these gaps closes on the same two sources: sales to dealer, by part and dealer, and what the workshop actually fitted. That is where the rest of this paper goes.

Chapter 2

What detail shows when we have it

Before asking anyone for more data, this chapter shows what it does when it exists. Every example rests on a new parts classification system built by Jérôme Van der Pluym. It places every Isuzu part number in a family, a product group and a product form, and keeps Genuine and Best Value side by side.

2.1Isuzu Truck UK brake pads

Measured Brake pads billed to Isuzu Truck UK fell from €347,389.58 in 2024 to €302,366.25 in 2025, while units fell from 4,562 to 3,402. A revenue view suggests a demand problem, a volume view suggests a price problem, and neither is the right first question. Across the three braking categories, €47,068.77 was lost, and pads are 95.7% of it. The starting sentence changes from "braking is down" to "brake pads are down", which a distributor can act on.

2.2Portugal clutch kits: a mistake prevented

Measured Combined clutch-kit billing rose +5.8% while Genuine fell from €2,901.92 to €2,057.86; units were flat at 462 and 461 kits. A Genuine-only view would have launched a recovery action for a business that grew. Here the tool earns its place by preventing a mistake.

2.3The ranked family view

Measured Front brake pads (36.0% of €423k) and blower filters (25.2% of €374k) carry heavy concession on modest bases: a handful of large deals, not a pricing position. Brake rotors (3.4% of €1.30M) and oil filters (1.5% of €557k) carry almost none. Their pricing is settled, and if they hold an opportunity it lies in coverage, which is exactly what ISZE cannot see without dealer data.

The point of Part I. At distributor level, detail already changes decisions. Everything in the next part is about taking the same detail one level down, to the dealer, where the maintenance business is actually won or lost.

Chapter 3

The next big thing: sales to dealer

Give the distributor a view of its own dealer network that it cannot build alone, and it will send the data that makes the view possible. That is the whole value proposition.

3.1Two halves of one answer

The distributor holds
Which dealer bought which part, when, and how many. Which dealer sold which trucks, and where they are serviced.
ISZE holds
The parts classification, service intervals per model, the fleet in service, reference and competitor prices, and benchmarks across 22 networks.
Together
For every dealer: how many maintenance parts its trucks should need, how many it bought, and where the gap is.

Neither side can answer "which of my dealers is losing maintenance business?" alone. The distributor knows it sold many trucks through a dealer, but not what those trucks should consume. ISZE knows what a truck consumes, but not which dealer looks after it. The exchange is that simple, and it serves the distributor first: developing its dealers is its job, and this is the tool for it.

3.2Where we are weak, seen from the market

Modelled In Germany ISZE supplies about 15% of the oil filters the fleet should need, at Isuzu UK 38%, and in Greece 97%. This is as far down as ISZE can see today. Inside each market, some dealers will be near 100% and others near zero. The distributor average hides both, and the market view cannot tell them apart.

3.3What the one dealer-level market already shows

Measured Isuzu Truck UK's 64 dealers bought €5.51M of parts from their distributor in 2025. The ten largest took 43.2% of it, and the median dealer bought €57k. What this cannot tell us is whether a dealer buying €57k is doing well. That depends on how many trucks it looks after, and the distributor has that number in its own vehicle sales.

Chapter 4

Bringing the dealers in: maintenance data

Sales to dealer shows what a dealer bought. Maintenance data shows what the workshop actually did: which trucks came in, which parts went on, and which parts did not come from us. It is the one source that turns modelled demand into observed demand.

4.1Why the workshop matters

Every modelled figure in this paper assumes how often a truck is serviced and what is fitted. The workshop record replaces the assumption with the fact. It shows the service that happened, the mileage at which it happened, and the parts fitted, including parts that were not bought through the Isuzu channel. ISZE has done this once, with one dealer's workshop export: vehicles, jobs and fitted parts could be matched. It works; it just has never been asked for at scale.

4.2What we ask, and nothing more

FieldWhy
Job date and mileageTurns an assumed service interval into an observed one
Vehicle model and year (vehicle identifier pseudonymised)Ties the job to the fleet model without identifying a customer
Operation performedSeparates routine service from repairs
Parts fitted: part number, quantity, and whether it came through the Isuzu channelShows capture and leakage at the point of fitting

Monthly, as the dealer's own workshop system exports it, in any format. No customer names, no prices, no labour rates. The dealer decides what goes further than its distributor.

4.3What the dealer gets back

Its own Dealer Standards profile
Its answers in the real question wording, section by section, against its country and an anonymous network average, with an action coach that turns a weak answer into three concrete steps.
A service-due list
Which trucks in its own workshop history are due for which maintenance in the next 90 days: work it can book, not a report.
A workshop benchmark
Parts per job, service intervals achieved and share of jobs using Isuzu-channel parts, against anonymous peers in its own quartile.

Plus Deal of the Day offers matched to the trucks it will actually service, and a funded €600 setup task to connect its workshop export once. Proposed

4.4The door is already open: Isuzu Dealer Standards

Measured 519 dealers across 19 networks completed the Dealer Standards self-assessment, and 334 have an individual score. That is the base rate for what this network does when it is asked. Reported The per-country dealer comparison built in-house (dealer against its country and an anonymous network average, on a map) is already used in feedback meetings, and distributors and dealers respond well to it. Until 2026 the results arrived as Excel sheets that took about five people to process.

Inferred This is the only place where ISZE holds something every dealer wants to look at and cannot assemble for itself. So the dealer journey opens with it: the first thing a dealer sees is its own assessment, not a supplier's reading of its business. The maintenance-data ask comes second, attached to the tools that need it.

4.5The dealer's worries, answered

The worryThe answer built into the design
"My supplier's supplier is looking at my book."Everything reaches the dealer through its own distributor. The dealer sees its own data first and decides what goes further.
"This will be used against me."No public league table. Each dealer sees its own position and anonymous quartiles. Nothing is published by name.
"Customer data and privacy."No customer names; vehicle identifiers pseudonymised; aggregates where a financial figure is needed.
"I have no time at the counter."No forms. One export connection, funded once, then automatic. The first session is ten minutes with a finished profile.
"What if I don't take part?"Ordinary service does not change. Participation adds tools and offers; it never removes anything.

Chapter 5

Deal of the Day: the engagement engine

ISZE helps a distributor prepare a specific, time-limited offer: a reasoned product selection, the right dealers, transparent economics and a way to learn from what follows. It is the part of Universe people feel in thirty seconds, and it is the one that spends money, so it has to buy data and evidence as well as volume.

5.1Why it is the engine

It needs a baseline
Support is paid only on extra volume, so every Deal of the Day starts from what the chosen dealers bought before, which is sales-to-dealer data for the selected families.
It needs targeting
The right dealers are the ones with trucks due and low capture, which is the maintenance scorecard and the service-due list.
It returns a result
Contribution measured on the same scope before and after, which is the evidence the programme needs to justify the next euro.

The data ask is built into the offer rather than bolted on. A distributor that wants Deal of the Day support sends the baseline because the mechanism cannot work without it, not because a form requires it. Not to be confused with Don't Lose the Deal (chapter 6), Isuzu Truck UK's existing support for competitive quotations.

5.2Which units get the discount

Illustrative If all 120 units get 5% off, gross profit stays at €3,000, so the 20 extra units bought nothing. If only the 20 extra units get the discount, gross profit is €3,500. The €500 comes from the scope of the concession alone. Support is paid on extra volume against a documented baseline, never on all volume.

5.3The size floor

At €150,000 of eligible billing, €2,500 of support needs 6.7% extra sales and €5,000 needs 13.3%. At €50,000 it would take 20% and 40%. Proposed Support only above €150,000 of eligible billing. Several dealers or families can be pooled to reach it.

5.4Where concession already sits, and a pilot candidate

Measured 8.4% of €6.65M maintenance-parts billing in 2025 sat at least 20% under reference, down from 13.0% in 2024. The highest shares sit in small markets, so without a size floor the money would follow them.

Measured Isuzu UK air filters rose from €82k to €116k while the deep-discount share fell from 38.6% to 15.1%. Proposed A strong pilot candidate as part of a wider filter selection for Isuzu UK (filters together €406k in 2025), because on its own it sits below the floor.

5.5Why thresholds, not forecasts

Measured Distributor purchasing response to list-price moves between 2024 and 2025 is too weak to forecast from. For maintenance-service parts it is negative, but the uncertainty is half the size of the estimate and it explains less than 1% of the movement. For brakes it is flat to slightly positive, with an uncertainty three times the estimate. For lubrication the price moved too uniformly to measure. Of the brakes change, roughly €233k sits where price rose and volume fell, €172k where both rose, and €335k where volume fell with price flat. This is stocking, not dealer demand. Dealer-level data is exactly what would make a real measurement possible. Until then, every Deal of the Day is judged against a threshold.

6.3What we deliberately do not build

Availability enquiries, part and Best Value lookups, day-to-day quotation handling and support administration stay in the existing business. A programme that also answered availability questions would be judged on response times against a system that already does it. The boundary is also the offer: we are not asking you to feed a support desk, and the support desk does not get better or worse depending on what you send us.

6.4Don't Lose the Deal: from payment to case

Measured Isuzu Truck UK's programme for competitive quotations runs on a memorandum of understanding, a rebate credit note and Japan-side sign-off. It has paid €30,000 a year: €90,000 across 2023, 2025 and 2026. €30,000 is 0.54% of Isuzu Truck UK's 2025 billing. Zero cases are recorded: no part, dealer, competitor quote or outcome. The intent is to roll it out to every dealer. At €30,000 per market that is €90,000 for three markets or €570,000 for nineteen (1.76% of 2025 billing). Rolled out as it stands, every market would buy the payment and inherit the blindness. The quote defender fixes it: part, dealer, dated competitor quote, support requested and granted, outcome, and the follow-on order reference. That is about five minutes per case, attached to the credit note that is raised anyway.

6.5Tenders: from notice to outcome

Measured 689 notices reduce to 619 opportunities, 286 with an aftersales signal and 199 in the action queue; no outcome is recorded. A tender's advertised value is not Isuzu parts revenue, and a notice count is not a pipeline. ISZE asks for one line when a tender is won, lost or declined. One decision closes both gaps: record the outcome of every supported case, in one shape.

Chapter 7

Designing for maximum engagement

Engagement is not a communication problem. It is a design problem: what each party gets, how soon, how often, and what it costs them to give something back.

7.1Six principles

PrincipleWhat it means in practice
Give firstEvery participant receives something finished before anything is asked: a prepared network review, its own Dealer Standards profile.
Value in the first ten minutesNo registration, no form, no upload in the first session. The first screen is the participant's own data.
Make the distributor the heroThe distributor invites its dealers, owns the scorecard and decides the action. ISZE never goes round it.
Make giving data nearly freeAny format through the intake assistant; a funded setup task; one connection, then automatic.
A reason to come back every monthThe monthly brief, the refreshed service-due list, the Deal of the Day calendar. Each depends on this month's data.
Recognition without a league tableEach participant sees its own position and anonymous quartiles. No public ranking by name.

8.3The hurdle for Deal of the Day

A pilot of ten Deals of the Day proves the method, not the return. It produces the first real measurement of how often a Deal of the Day qualifies, and that rate decides the second stage.

Modelled ISZE supplies about 19% of the modelled maintenance-parts need today. At a 30% gross margin, the €100,000 route is earned back by 1.3 points of capture network-wide, and the €300,000 route by 4.0. A modelled gap is not recoverable revenue. The pilot measures capture in pilot dealers against comparable dealers, and only that measured difference counts.

9.3Go, no-go and stop

Go if a real participant completed a real task, the output was correct against its source, and the participant can name the decision it changed. No-go if the only evidence is that a screen rendered or a demonstration looked good. Stop if, after two eligible cycles, no participant can name a decision the tool changed, or a confidentiality weakness cannot be closed. It does not pause or widen the sample.

The honest test: if the rebate condition were removed tomorrow, would the distributor still send the data?

9.4The unowned answer, and the other risks

The answer needs an owner. Tools raise questions: why is this dealer at 23%? Nobody has yet been named, given hours or funded to answer them. Software is cheap and a person's time is not. Without a named owner, the product arrives looking complete and the point of it never happens.

Delivery. There is no production system yet: no shared identity, access control or storage. Until that is built and independently reviewed, sharing stays distributor-led and document-based. Only the authorised distributor sees its own accounts; only the dealer sees its own record; no distributor sees another's data.

Competition law and privacy. The European motor-vehicle block exemption regime runs until 31 May 2028. Nothing here is a legal determination, and the design must be reviewed before any shared output is used. Three standing rules: no exclusivity in exchange for information; no price coordination between distributors (each is compared with its own history and anonymous aggregates only); no resale-price instruction. Workshop data comes pseudonymised, with no customer names.
