## The two things that touch the dealer page directly

**Fleet-base targets are being set from the parc, not from history, starting this year.** The working
assumption in the meeting was ~€260 per vehicle per year on a parc of about 10,000 units across Belgium,
Poland and the Netherlands. Bavo pushed back hard on the parc size — he put Belgium nearer **4,000**
than the ~8,000 assumed, Poland at ~3,100, and the Netherlands and Luxembourg as negligible — and argued
that this made Astara's genuine-parts share look roughly half of what it really is. A stretch target of
**+1.8 percentage points of genuine-parts share, about +€43k, to roughly €2,340,000** was discussed, on
top of the existing business-plan target.

**Read that against Portugal.** Portugal's own parc rebuild is exactly this method done properly: real
registrations convolved with a survival curve. It is the one market in the pack where the fleet base has
been measured rather than assumed, and it is worth saying so.

**The dealer-standards tool was shown in the meeting, and Bavo's stated use for it is the point.**
He wanted it to target coaching across Astara's regional and field teams, "including dealers suspected
of inflating self-assessment scores". That is the same caveat this pack raises about the sixteen
Portugal scores, in the importer's own words.

## Also discussed, where it changes what can be asked for

- **Sell-out data.** Isuzu asked for monthly dealer-level sell-out: invoice date, part number, quantity,
  explicitly no pricing. Bavo had no objection in principle but had to confirm with his director, and
  said **history before the current system (about May 2025) is not available** — going further back would
  cost roughly four times the effort. The Portugal file that arrived on 3 September has no dealer
  column; this is the item that would add one.
- **Special pricing replaces campaigns.** Isuzu can no longer run blanket price campaigns. The process is
  now distributor-initiated: Astara compiles the specific pain-point parts with a target price,
  typically 20–30% below today's, and Isuzu takes that to the supplier. It has to be accompanied by a
  12-month forecast, and Isuzu's minimum expectation is that a requested cut is matched by at least as
  much extra volume. Bavo's objection is on the record: the brake-parts pricing problem is structural
  and he wants it solved at the root, not dealer by dealer.
- **Parts performance.** Astara is at or above the network average on most filter categories. The two
  flagged gaps are **fuel/oil filters and brake parts**, both below network average, and Jerome was to
  check whether that lines up with low Best Value Parts uptake.
- **Repair and maintenance contracts / tenders.** Poland is the significant tender market. In Belgium
  the pickup tender market is small and aftersales is rarely involved.
- **Stock.** Astara has moved toward holding more of its own stock and less urgent vehicle-off-road
  ordering, avoiding roughly a 7% urgent-order surcharge.
- **Data quality.** Bavo: the monthly feed to Isuzu is not fully clean yet and is improving slowly.

## What this means for the first page

Two of the fourteen items are the ones that would change the page most, and both are Astara's:

- **Item 8, the dealer-level sell-out file.** That single file turns the national 51,363-unit picture
  into a per-dealer one and makes the whole page comparable with what ITUK gets. It is already agreed in
  principle and needs a director's confirmation.
- **Item 7, the brake-parts list with target prices.** Brakes are €122,880 of Portugal's 2025
  sales-to-dealer value, the fourth-largest family and the one with the highest measured Best Value
  share inside it. Astara has already named brake pricing as the structural problem.

Until item 8 lands, a Portugal dealer page cannot show what a dealer bought. That is a design
constraint, not a reason to wait — the sixteen assessed dealers, their sections and the market's own
48.9% capture are enough for a first page that is honest about what it is.
