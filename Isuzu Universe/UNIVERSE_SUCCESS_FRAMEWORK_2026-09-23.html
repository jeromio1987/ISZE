My view, as judgement, not measurement. As the programme stands today I give it roughly a one-in-four chance of becoming something distributors and dealers use without being pushed. The idea is strong. The problem is real and measurable, the tools can be built quickly, and there is already an incentive to re-point. What holds it down is not the concept. It is four things that have nothing to do with software: nobody owns the answers the tools will raise, the programme depends on one person, no distributor has yet been asked whether it wants the dealer scorecard, and nothing is recorded that could prove a return.

With the ten moves in section 4, done in the next two quarters, I would put it at better than even odds. Most of those moves are cheap: a named owner, three distributor conversations, a mock scorecard on Isuzu Truck UK's 64 dealers, a written tier definition for the rebate, and an outcome line on every credit note. The expensive parts (a production system, the Deal of the Day money) only matter once those are done.
