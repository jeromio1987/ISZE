# Network Atlas 0.2 - review and handover

**20 September 2026 | ISZE internal/private | Isuzu Dealer Standards (IDS), 2026 round**

The map can be improved now, but distributors must remain commercial entities and dealer self-assessments must remain a separate layer. This revision does not establish responsibility for individual sites.

This is a bounded revision of the supplied Atlas 0.1 prototype, not a replacement of the existing IDS, sales, pricing or fleet dashboards. No live Cursor workspace, hosting account or deployment was accessed. The original inputs are retained unchanged. Publication is not authorised by this document.

**Scope completed in the prototype:** independent commercial and assessment-group selectors; removal of inferred ownership lines and distributor IDS scoring; detailed geographic background; three map styles; clusters with selectable underlying records; geographic precision labels; separate commercial and dealer comparisons; original checklist access; local draft follow-ups and review scenes.

**One disagreement with the inventory's design conclusion:** a missing responsibility table prevents us from unfolding a distributor's *verified* dealer network. It does not prevent an honest map of the supplied dealer records or a separate distributor commercial map. Geographic proximity and the assessment submission label are not substituted for that table.

I withdraw the earlier claim that selecting a distributor can currently unfold **its** dealer network. I also withdraw the earlier distributor comparison against an apparent IDS score. ISZE does not audit distributors; a summary of dealer records must never be presented as a distributor assessment.

### Time and fleet layers

There is only one IDS round in the supplied inventory, with no per-dealer assessment dates. The commercial selector can still compare complete calendar years, but it must not change the date of the IDS snapshot. A later IDS history would require dated assessments, standard versions and stable record/site identities supplied by the dealer-network team and participating distributors.

Fleet stops at market/distributor level. Dealer or territory reach needs a separately validated local fleet source and verified site locations. Neither a national fleet divided across dealers nor a detailed background map supplies that information.

### Dealer X-ray and fingerprints

The embedded original IDS HTML contains section and question results. Those support navigation and fingerprints. Evidence attachments and dealer-level operating or fleet measures are not supplied. UK truck records contain totals only; their section cells remain unavailable. UMI's unscored vehicle-sales section remains unscored, not a failure.

The two UK light-commercial records named Adam and PatKirk remain inspectable but are excluded from the new means and comparisons because the count review did not locate their source workbooks. No original score is rewritten. Repeated trading names remain separate source records, with warnings where present; this is not a certified premises register.

### Investigation workspace

The useful first version asks what is known and what would establish an explanation. The inventory describes Ireland's dated backorder snapshots, but those files are not attached to this map. Their integration needs the snapshot date, order/line identity, part number and quantity definitions. Adding weekly open balances would double-count persistent orders; disappearance from a snapshot alone does not prove fulfilment.

There are no lost-order transaction logs or measured stock balances in the pack. A reported stock-control practice cannot establish actual stock availability, and a modelled target-spend gap cannot establish a lost order. Pricing, supply and dealer behaviour remain hypotheses until the relevant evidence is supplied. No monetary effect is assigned to an IDS answer.

### Access and hosting

A self-contained internal demonstration remains suitable for this round. A separate recipient-specific file could later be distributed through an approved restricted folder, but the file must contain only that recipient's permitted data. A hosted application would require approved identity, authorisation and data separation before delivery.

The user's latest report supersedes the access note's instruction to clear a publishing folder: active public-upload instructions were removed without deleting the retained files. This revision does not reopen that cleanup. Whether any historical public deployment exists remains unverified; editing a local README cannot answer it. No blanket claim about a platform's current enterprise authentication capabilities is required to make this decision.

## Block 2 — Gaps

### Ranked blockers and who can close them

**First: verified site responsibility.** This blocks ownership lines, verified distributor dealer lists, dealer-to-account linkage and recipient-specific data entitlement. The dealer-network team and distributors must confirm a relationship table containing stable site and dealer identifiers, distributor code, vehicle scope, relationship type, validity dates and evidence. Cursor can prepare and validate the structure; software cannot approve the commercial relationship. This is data collection and business validation, not a hosting decision.

**Second: dealer identity and score-source reconciliation.** Cursor can reconcile source records, names, addresses and originating workbooks; the network team must resolve ambiguous sites and unsupported records. Use the embedded original IDS HTML for this revision. Do not fill gaps from the cleaned score CSV. The pack's score-table note reports that blanking invalid values did not make that CSV agree with the dashboard.

**Third: authoritative commercial and classification sources.** Use the main account register's explicitly eligible commercial codes for this bounded revision. Enrich legal names only when documented; do not promote secondary-register entries into billed customers. The corrected dated classification file is a candidate, not an authority silently selected here. The classification owner and Cursor need to confirm the approved file/version and coverage before a full AIM connection is made.

**Fourth: named sales-to-dealer identities.** ITUK has a named-dealer quantity source for calendar 2025, but its identities are not a validated IDS/site crosswalk. Obtain a narrow approved crosswalk and the relevant aggregate by named dealer and part family. The original quantities are measured; values calculated using ISZE billing prices must be labelled as valuations, not distributor invoice revenue. Do not request raw ISZE billing or the full price list.

**Fifth: permissions and delivery.** The user and ISZE's responsible IT/security role must approve an external audience, allowed fields, peer benchmarks and delivery method. This does not block the internal file, but it blocks sharing the all-network file with distributors.

**Later feature dependencies:** accurate premises coordinates and service scope for reach analysis; dated subsequent IDS rounds for history; inventory, lost-order records and dated interventions for causal or return estimates. These are new inputs, not cosmetic map changes.

### What cannot work from this pack

A verified distributor-to-site network, dealer spend per local truck, service-reach demand polygons, repeated IDS history, measured lost-order attribution, and an evidence-based investment-return simulator cannot be produced from these inputs. They are not represented by fake data, zero values, interpolated history or inferred allocations.

### Source authority and unresolved contradictions

**Scores:** retain the embedded HTML; the cleaned CSV and its derived spread statistics are not an alternative authority. The original HTML also has known defects, so retaining it does not certify every record.

**Counts:** this revision retains 521 non-Benelux source records from the prototype. The 43-row overlapping Benelux view is not displayed again. The official round summary's 481 submissions from 1,034 invitations is shown separately. Neither source-record counts nor repeated trading names establish a distinct-premises census. The pack's own use of “distinct dealers” should not be promoted into a site count without identifiers.

**Distributors:** the main table contains billing-account rows, including repeated distributor codes. The revision uses 22 distinct codes explicitly marked `network_db`; it does not treat account rows, country labels or secondary-register entries as additional customers. Map positions remain documented country-centre coordinates.

**Parts:** preserve the supplied limited family aggregates. Do not total the top-product-key sample or select a disputed complete classification without a documented authority decision.

**Commercial periods and fleet:** preserve full-year 2023-2025 figures and their original denominators. The spend-per-truck and target-spend extracts use different fleet bases in some markets; they are not averaged. ITUK uses the March 2026 registration fleet as a reference denominator for the displayed historical billing years, and that date is now stated explicitly. Billing through June 2026 does not justify a full-year 2026 comparison. No future fleet is used.

## Block 3 — First build

### A single screen

The screen has two independent selectors: a commercial distributor and a dealer assessment group. The map occupies the main area, with a persistent inspector beside it. Map controls select dealer/distributor layers, map style, labels, roads and placement precision. A commercial family panel and a recorded-practice panel sit below.

Distributor diamonds open full-year billing and market-level fleet/target context. Dealer points open recorded sections and questions. A numbered cluster opens its underlying records rather than hiding co-located sites or moving their coordinates. The same source positions are used in each style. The Earth backdrop is historical geographic imagery, not live site photography.

Missing data produces a reason: “not joined”, “not scored”, “totals only”, “source check” or “not held”. It does not produce a fabricated zero. Map detail is explicitly separated from location accuracy.

### How far a selection travels

A distributor selection travels to its supplied full-year commercial figures and part-family aggregates. It stops before named IDS dealer responsibility. A dealer-group selection travels to dealer records, sections, questions and a draft evidence request. It stops before an unverified distributor assignment, dealer revenue or dealer fleet.

Both selections can remain visible as separate discussion context. They do not constitute a database join. Selecting a family never changes a dealer's IDS score or allocates distributor purchases to that dealer.

### First demonstration market

**ITUK is the commercial integration pilot**, because the inventory identifies named dealer quantities for calendar 2025. Its limitation is equally useful to demonstrate: the IDS truck records provide totals only and the sales-name-to-site crosswalk is not approved. The first commercial demonstration should therefore stop at the supported boundary rather than simulate a complete connection.

**Norway is the visual map demonstration in this revision**, continuing the user's current Sarpsborg view. Its dealer records support section and question navigation, but no named-dealer commercial data is inferred. This is a presentation choice, not a ranking of commercial opportunity.

### Limits visible on screen

Distributors are not audited. IDS is the 2026 dealer self-assessment round, not “today”. Commercial periods are complete calendar years. Coordinates are approximate. Record counts are not a network census. Ownership is not joined. Dealer revenue, local fleet and missing evidence are not estimated. Modelled targets are not observed lost sales. The file contains internal data and is not an access-control mechanism.

### Deliberate omissions

No ownership curves, warehouse claims, delivery animation, travel-time polygons, dealer revenue colouring, repeated IDS timeline, full AIM hierarchy, natural-language calculation engine or monetary return simulation is introduced. The comparison now changes between two honest questions rather than making a distributor look like an audited dealership.

### Decisions versus execution

The user has authorised this isolated internal map revision, so no further host decision is required to inspect it. Official site responsibility, source authority disputes, external permissions and delivery still need their respective business approvals. The original prototype and all supplied inputs remain unchanged; the new version is a separate file.

## Block 4 — Decisions and order of work

1. **Decisions for the user.** Continue with an internal-only demonstration: yes/no. Before any external version, approve named billing euro for the recipient's own distributor: yes/no; approve only anonymous peer IDS benchmarks rather than named peer detail: yes/no; approve cross-distributor questionnaire detail: yes/no; approve fleet/target benchmarks externally: yes/no. Choose one approved delivery option with IT: restricted recipient-specific files or an authenticated application. Another distributor's named commercial data and internal pricing remain excluded by default. Historical public-host status needs the account owner's check, not a guess from this pack.

2. **Order and ownership of work.** **Work completed here:** the bounded map revision, entity-separation changes, source-preservation checks and packaged browser interaction checks. **Next work, Cursor as proposed integration owner:** compare the supplied revision with the currently active local branch, then selectively integrate the approved map and terminology changes. Do not replace Cursor's current files blindly. **Business validation required:** network team/distributor confirmation of the site relationship and sales-name crosswalk. **Work after validation:** connect the narrow approved data and repeat numerical/eligibility checks. **Blocked on a decision:** any external data cut, login, sharing or publication. This is a proposed division of work; it is not a claim that Cursor has received or accepted it.

3. **Do not do this yet.** Do not upload the all-network HTML to a public host, delete retained files, re-open completed local publishing cleanup, manufacture responsibility from a country, migrate guessed links into an official register, combine ITUK/IUK or UMI/UTI, use the cleaned CSV to overwrite scores, sum the sampled product table, divide by an invented dealer fleet, or merge this revision over concurrent edits without reviewing the differences.

4. **The three most dangerous contradictions.** First, the original design implied both distributor audits and a verified distributor-to-dealer network; neither is supported. Second, approximate coordinates and a market fleet cannot support premises reach or dealer-level spend even when the map looks precise. Third, the presence of a checklist question, re-exported score file or modelled gap does not establish an operational transaction, a second assessment or an observed lost sale.
