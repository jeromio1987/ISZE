The proposition
A decision environment, not another collection of dashboards
Universe should connect parts purchasing, dealer-network information and market context so that a 
commercial question can be followed through to a specific, reviewable action. Its purpose is not to make 
every available dataset appear on one screen. It is to make the relationship between a claim, its evidence 
and the next decision explicit.
The working proposition is simple: identify what changed, locate the parts and population involved, test 
plausible explanations, choose an intervention and review the result. A useful conclusion names its 
reporting period, transaction flow, affected parts, evidence limitations and action owner. An attractive 
chart without those elements is not a completed analysis.
Universe brings together AIM, IDS and PRISM. Tender support is a possible extension rather than an 
assumed fourth pillar. The names remain working names. [D1]
Four commercial outcomes
Outcome
What the user should receive
Sales explanation
A monthly change explained by actual part numbers, families, customers and 
transaction flows, with coverage changes separated from business changes.
Pricing precision
A support or price-review case tied to comparable offers, applicable parts and an 
explicit gross-profit scenario, not a blanket discount recommendation.
Market comparison
Competitor evidence with a dated source, matching basis and commercial terms, 
placed beside the relevant product and market.
Service-range 
opportunity
An applicable part, kit or documented BVP alternative worth discussing, with a reason 
it may improve the offer. Absence of a purchase alone is insufficient.
These outcomes preserve the commercial structure agreed for AIM while making the last outcome 
concrete: complete or improve an applicable offer, rather than merely count products that were not 
bought. [D9]
What success would look like
A distributor should be able to open a monthly review, understand a purchasing change, examine the 
actual parts, discuss a suitable response and record the outcome. ISZE should be able to support that 
conversation with the same evidence, without rebuilding the explanation in a separate spreadsheet or 
presentation.
The proposed value is better decisions and a more useful information exchange. Revenue uplift, time 
saved and improved capture are outcomes to test; they are not established benefits of the current 
prototype.
Universe should make a decision easier to defend, not make incomplete evidence 
look complete.
Scope boundary. Preserve the portfolio overview and Genuine/BVP comparison. Keep the sales cube 
removed and product photography deferred at this stage. Do not turn the product into an ERP 
replacement, an automated price-setting system or a distributor-audit tool. [D2, D5]

Three pillars, one decision
Different roles, shared context
AIM - Aftermarket Intelligence Model. AIM provides the parts-classification and commercial-analysis 
foundation. Its role is to give purchasing data a useful product structure and enable analysis from 
portfolio to family to part number. Classification is not, by itself, proof of interchangeability, vehicle 
fitment or the contents of a kit. Those relationships require their own evidence. [D9]
IDS - Isuzu Dealer Standards. IDS records dealer self-assessments. It contributes the dealer's reported 
practices, assessment context and agreed follow-up. ISZE does not conduct distributor audits through 
IDS. A distributor-level presentation of IDS must explicitly describe the assessed dealer population, 
response coverage and assessment dates. [D5]
PRISM. The proposed boundary in this paper is the vehicle and market-context pillar: fleet composition, 
applicability, relevant vehicle segments and modelled service-demand context. This functional boundary 
requires confirmation against the PRISM specification before implementation. It is not a new expansion 
of the acronym or a claim that every supporting dataset is already integrated.
The connections that matter
Connection
Decision it should improve
AIM + PRISM
Is the purchasing pattern plausible for the relevant fleet and applicable product 
range? What part of the comparison is observed, and what part is modelled?
AIM + IDS
Does a dealer's purchasing pattern suggest a useful discussion alongside its self-
reported practices? What can be followed up without claiming causation?
IDS + PRISM
What market or fleet context is relevant to an assessed dealer population, where a 
valid relationship exists? National context must not be presented as a measured 
dealer catchment.
All three
Which specific commercial or dealer-support action has enough evidence to be 
assigned, and what would demonstrate progress?
Competitor prices, warranty, supply availability, SPA history and tender information are supporting 
evidence sources. Their presence in a discussion does not establish that they are production-ready 
features of any one pillar.
A shared operating loop
Observe. Select one population, one reporting clock and one transaction flow. Identify the change and 
the records behind it.
Explain. Examine product mix, returns, coverage changes, timing, applicable vehicle context and available 
market evidence. Keep untested explanations as hypotheses.
Act. Create a commercial intervention or a data-quality follow-up. Give it an owner, review date, 
approval requirement and expected observable outcome.
Review. Revisit the same scope and measurement basis. Distinguish an observed change from an effect 
attributable to the intervention.
The connection is therefore not simply navigation between three dashboards. It is preservation of the 
same question and evidence as the user moves between them.

Participation should pay back
Start with the existing incentive
A portion of the distributor kickback is already tied to providing sales-to-dealer information. The 
mandatory requirement does not require distributor or dealer selling prices. Universe should use that 
existing mechanism to start participation, then make the return useful enough to justify continued 
participation independently of the incentive. [D4]
The proposal is an explicit exchange: a distributor provides a repeatable, declared-coverage quantity feed 
and receives a repeatable commercial review. Participation should not become an expanding demand for 
sensitive information with no visible return.
The monthly return to the distributor
Return
Tangible output
Purchasing-pattern 
explanation
Monthly family and part trends; regular, intermittent and interrupted purchasing; the 
parts contributing most to change; a visible coverage check.
Genuine/BVP review
Comparable, applicable products; purchasing mix on a stated basis; documented 
alternatives or an explicit unresolved-match status.
Market and fleet 
context
Dated competitor observations, relevant PRISM context and clearly separated 
modelled demand. No invented local market share.
Dealer-network 
discussion
A dealer's own development and carefully matched peer context, where dealer-
granular data and a valid population are available.
IDS follow-up
A connection from a specific self-assessment item to a purchasing question and an 
agreed support action, without converting a self-rating into a causal diagnosis.
Commercial-support 
case
An evidence packet for an SPA, range review or tender discussion: exact parts, 
demand basis, terms, assumptions, owner and review point.
The same monthly package should expose corrections and unresolved evidence. Receiving an accurate 
explanation of what cannot yet be concluded is more useful than receiving a confident but unsupported 
ranking.
Earn additional information
Optional stock exchange. A distributor provides selected stock and open-order information for an agreed 
scope; in return, it receives a replenishment and availability review that quantity sales alone cannot 
support.
Optional commercial exchange. A distributor provides a restricted price or offer sample; in return, it 
receives a comparable market-position or support analysis. Access and retention are agreed before 
transfer.
Optional service or fleet exchange. A distributor provides an authorised, appropriately limited dataset; in 
return, it receives a more relevant service-demand or fleet-support assessment. The added benefit must 
be demonstrated on the agreed scope.
Outside the mandatory feed: selling prices, distributor margins, inventory balances, customer identities, 
VINs, workshop records and end-customer sales. None should be silently inferred from the minimum 
feed. Optional sharing must not become a concealed condition for receiving the basic return.

The minimum data contract
Proposed submission standard
Use a monthly quantity feed with a declared population. The proposed deadline is within ten working 
days of month-end, subject to agreement with participating distributors. Distinguish a closed month from 
a provisional month. This is a proposed operating standard, not a statement of existing kickback terms.
The preferred aggregate grain is distributor x dealer x calendar month x part number x unit of measure x 
transaction type x channel. A more detailed feed may be accepted, but invoice prices and end-customer 
information are not required.
Required element
Meaning and control
Distributor identifier
Stable identifier in an agreed namespace; never resolved from a display name alone.
Dealer identifier
Stable source-system identifier. Map it to a legal entity or site explicitly; maintain 
aliases and effective dates.
Reporting period
Calendar month and source time basis. Preserve transaction dates separately when 
supplied.
Original part number
Text value preserving leading zeros and source formatting. Store a normalised join 
key separately.
Signed quantity and 
unit
State whether a unit is a piece, set, kit or other measure. Positive sales and negative 
returns must remain distinguishable.
Transaction type and 
channel
Identify sale, return or adjustment and direct/wholesale channel. Preserve an explicit 
unknown classification rather than guessing.
Submission identifier
Distributor-period batch ID, revision, extraction timestamp and schema version.
Coverage declaration
Included dealers, channels and product scope; excluded records; missing periods; 
provisional status and known limitations.
Dealer population. Provide a stable dealer roster, or an agreed controlled equivalent, including zero-
activity dealers. Absence from a transaction file alone does not establish zero activity or the end of a 
commercial relationship.
Wholesale. Declare whether wholesale is included and to which known purchasing entity the sale is 
attributed. Do not assign a wholesaler's purchases to downstream dealers without a supported 
relationship. The minimum contract must accommodate the agreed sales-to-dealer scope, including 
wholesale where applicable. [D4]
Correction and acceptance
Use full-replacement revisions for a distributor-month wherever possible. A corrected batch supersedes 
the earlier accepted batch; it is not added to it. Reprocessing the same submission must not duplicate 
quantities. Preserve earlier versions and the reason for revision.
Before acceptance, check identifiers, periods, quantities, units, duplicates, coverage and control totals. 
Record accepted and quarantined quantities, not merely rejected row counts. An incomplete dealer 
mapping need not prevent a valid national total, but it must prevent unsupported dealer attribution.
Return a receipt with the accepted period, coverage summary, exceptions and correction route. Rebuild 
dependent outputs when a revision changes them, and show that the historical result was restated.

Evidence that can be trusted
Never merge the transaction flows
ISZE to distributor records the distributor's purchasing from ISZE. Distributor to dealer records the 
dealer's purchasing from that distributor. Dealer to end customer is a third, separate flow. None is a 
substitute for another. Missing sales-to-dealer coverage means unknown downstream activity, not zero 
activity. [D7]
The same physical part may appear in more than one flow. These values must not be added together as a 
single market-sales total. Timing differences may reflect order cycles, returns, stock movements or other 
factors; without the relevant evidence, they do not prove lost demand.
Distinguish what the number represents
Evidence class
Permitted interpretation
Recorded quantity
Activity in the named source and covered population, on the source's declared unit 
basis.
Recorded billing
Monetary activity in the named billing flow, with its currency, credits and relevant 
accounting basis identified.
Reference-price 
valuation
Quantity multiplied by a stated reference price. Useful for a common comparison 
basis; not actual selling revenue or margin.
Modelled demand or 
potential
An estimate based on explicit fleet, interval, applicability and valuation assumptions; 
not observed servicing.
Hypothesis or 
candidate
A possible explanation, substitute or action requiring stated validation before a 
stronger claim is made.
A calculated total inherits the limitations of its inputs. A precise euro value does not make a modelled 
result measured. Missing price coverage must be visible; unpriced parts must not silently become zero-
valued parts.
Every comparison carries its scope
Persist the transaction flow, population, period, reporting clock, measure, currency, valuation basis, 
source version and coverage state. Compare a partial current month only with an equivalent cut-off or 
label it separately. Never add CY and FY views of the same activity.
Use the latest complete month and trailing complete-month views where supported; preserve a different 
explicitly selected reporting clock. If a dealer or channel enters the feed, show its effect separately from 
like-for-like development.
Coverage precedes ranking. Establish which distributors, dealers, months, channels and part populations 
each source covers before comparing performance. A declared population is not necessarily the whole 
market. Where a reliable denominator is unavailable, show what is covered rather than inventing a 
completeness percentage.
A result should become less certain when its evidence becomes weaker. It should not 
become more definite simply because it has been placed on a dashboard.

Fleet, GPS, PMS and warranty
Keep the business lenses distinct
GPS concerns the broad genuine-parts opportunity; PMS concerns the defined interval and competitive-
parts scope. Their approved definitions, part populations and denominators must be registered 
separately. A PMS-family result must not be relabelled as overall GPS performance. [D7]
A useful purchasing-intensity lens is billing or a common reference valuation per relevant vehicle. The 
numerator, vehicle population, period and valuation basis must be compatible. This is not automatically 
market share: a modelled potential is not a measured denominator of total market transactions.
For modelled service demand, expose the assumptions that drive the estimate: relevant UIO, age or 
usage profile where supported, interval, quantity per event, applicability, observation period and value 
basis. Present a range or sensitivity where uncertainty materially affects the decision. A single exact-
looking estimate must not hide a weak fleet or usage assumption.
Vehicle and tonnage attribution
ISZE billing has no VIN, chassis or model-level servicing evidence. Vehicle attribution is permitted only 
through validated exclusive part applicability. Parts that fit multiple bands or families remain pooled; they 
must not be allocated by fleet share merely to complete the picture. [D6]
Maintain three separate views: recorded exclusive-applicability purchasing, modelled expected service 
demand by band, and market-level actual purchasing. The recorded exclusive subset is not the whole 
demand of that band.
A market actual repeated beside several bands is non-additive. It must not be summed across bands or 
divided by each band's expectation to claim measured band service cover. A modelled allocation of a 
market gap is not measured leakage and must not become a distributor or dealer ranking.
Standing implementation rule. In 02_nseries_band_evolution.csv, filter 
sums_to_n_series_total == "yes" for the relevant additive view. Treat single_band_only as the 
exclusive numerator and also_fits_band as non-additive. Keep CY and FY separate. The corrected 
tonnage v2 rules supersede v1. [D6]
Warranty is a different source
Warranty records claims, defects and settlements; it does not provide a complete record of scheduled 
maintenance. Low warranty value is not evidence of a healthy aftermarket. Missing maintenance claims 
do not prove that dealers are failing to service vehicles. [D7]
Warranty may contextualise a product issue or claim-related event, within its coverage. It must not 
supply an invented service interval, maintenance-capture rate or leakage conclusion. Linking a claim 
pattern to a parts trend creates a question to investigate, not an automatic commercial explanation.
Default wording: use "modelled opportunity", "unexplained purchasing change" or "candidate follow-up" 
when those are what the evidence supports. Reserve "measured leakage" for a genuinely observable and 
appropriately matched loss.

Five decisions Universe should support
A braking decline that can be explained
Start with monthly purchasing for the defined braking population and transaction flow. Show the part 
numbers contributing to the decline, returns, active purchasing entities and any coverage break. Separate 
a quantity change from a price or mix effect. Then test available supply, fitment, supersession, promotion 
and competitor evidence. Possible action: a targeted part-level investigation or offer, not an unsupported 
statement that the market is losing brake business. [D8]
A BVP alternative that is actually applicable
A Genuine part is losing activity or faces a documented competitive offer. Retrieve a BVP candidate only 
where fitment, function, contents and commercial units can be checked. A shared AIM family is 
insufficient. Test whether the comparison is one-for-one or a kit/set relationship. Possible action: an 
applicable BVP discussion or range review, with technical approval where needed. No validated match 
means no substitute recommendation.
A gap between upstream and downstream purchasing
ISZE-to-distributor activity slows while covered distributor-to-dealer activity is stable, or the reverse. 
Compare like periods and separate returns, valuation differences and missing channels. Inventory and 
timing are possible explanations, not measured facts without stock information. Possible action: a 
replenishment or availability discussion. This is not proof of overstock, shortage or end-customer demand 
loss.
A dealer-support conversation informed by IDS
A dealer reports a weakness in a defined IDS practice while its purchasing pattern shows an issue worth 
discussing. First validate the dealer/site link, assessment date and commercial period. Consider 
incomplete purchasing coverage and other explanations. Possible action: agree a support activity, such as 
an applicable range discussion or process follow-up, and review the later purchasing pattern. The 
association does not prove that the self-assessed weakness caused the commercial result.
A stronger SPA or tender-support case
A distributor presents an identifiable competitive challenge for defined parts or a fleet requirement. 
Combine actual historical purchasing, applicable alternatives, dated market evidence and explicit volume 
assumptions. Build a gross-profit comparison on the correct cost and price basis. Possible action: 
approve, amend or reject a bounded support proposal, with an owner, conditions and review point. A 
lower gross-margin percentage can coexist with higher gross profit; the calculation must demonstrate the 
case.
These are decision patterns, not findings about a market or dealer. Each must be 
populated with accepted records before it becomes a commercial claim.
Selection principle. Prioritise an action when the signal is material, the evidence is sufficient and 
someone can realistically act on it. A data gap should create a specific data-quality task. It should not be 
disguised as a sales opportunity simply to fill a top-five list.

The standard for every finding
A finding is a small, reproducible evidence packet
The headline should state the affected scope and observed change, not just the family name. The packet 
should allow a reviewer to move from the summary to the exact supporting records without recreating 
the analysis. This addresses the agreed requirement for monthly trends, identifiable parts, supported BVP 
comparisons and a coherent explanation. [D8]
Element
Minimum content
Scope and coverage
Distributor or dealer population, transaction flow, reporting clock, period, included 
channels, source version and known gaps.
Monthly evidence
A dated series with units or a clearly named monetary basis, comparable periods, 
visible missing months and an explanation of coverage breaks.
Part-level contribution
Original and normalised part number, description, AIM family, tier, current and 
comparison measures, contribution to change and returns where available.
Product comparison
Documented Genuine/BVP or competitor relationship, applicability, pack contents, 
unit conversion and validation status.
Interpretation
What is observed, which explanations are supported, which remain plausible, and 
what evidence would change the conclusion.
Action and review
Named owner or accountable role, proposed intervention, required approval, review 
date, expected outcome and later result.
Graphs must answer the question
For a claimed decline, show a monthly time series rather than two isolated totals. Do not draw a 
continuous line through an unreported month as though it were measured. A reported zero is different 
from a missing observation. Separate unit trends from price effects when the required data exists.
Show which parts contribute to the change, with an "other parts" residual so the contribution view 
reconciles to the selected total. Use signed contributions for increases and decreases. State whether the 
comparison uses a constant part population or includes new and discontinued products.
Do not aggregate pieces, kits and sets into a supposedly comparable volume without a validated 
conversion. If a common reference valuation is used, disclose the pricebook date and the priced 
population. The chart title must not call that result sales revenue.
Confidence as a decision status
Use ready for review, needs validation, context only or withheld, with the reason shown. Avoid a single 
confidence score whose ingredients are opaque. Coverage, identity matching, applicability, price 
comparability and period alignment are separate checks.
A report with no validated BVP match should say so. A report with no dealer-granular feed should stop at 
its supported level. A data-quality action can still be useful, but its commercial benefit must remain 
unclaimed.
Reproducibility test. Another reviewer should obtain the same total from the same accepted data and 
metric version. Export the filters, source references and calculation definition alongside the presentation.

Genuine, BVP and commercial support
Separate classification from product equivalence
Maintain an explicit relationship between candidate parts. A family label provides context; it does not 
authorise substitution. Store whether the relationship is a supersession, documented alternative, 
kit/component relationship or unverified candidate. Record its source, applicable vehicles, effective dates 
and validation status.
For Genuine/BVP comparisons, check fitment, functional scope, included components, package quantity 
and commercial unit. Compare a kit with its documented equivalent contents, not simply with one 
component. A missing BVP purchase is not an opportunity when applicability, availability or the feed's 
BVP coverage is unresolved.
Market evidence should identify the observed product, market, source date, currency, tax treatment, 
pack quantity, freight and relevant commercial terms. A public retail offer and an ISZE distributor price 
are different commercial levels; a visible difference alone does not establish that ISZE is uncompetitive. 
Use a documented normalisation or keep the comparison qualified.
Gross profit, not just margin percentage
For a proposed SPA, compare a defined counterfactual with the supported scenario over the same period 
and scope. Keep actual historical purchasing separate from a future volume commitment or forecast. Do 
not call a commitment an achieved result.
Baseline GP = sum of baseline quantity x (baseline net price - baseline unit cost).
Scenario GP = sum of scenario quantity x (scenario net price - scenario unit cost).
GP change = scenario GP - baseline GP.
Use compatible units and a stated currency basis. Show rebates, HQ support, freight treatment and 
incremental programme costs separately where applicable; ensure they are not counted twice. The 
commercial owner must approve the cost definition used for the decision.
For a single comparable product with unchanged cost and positive supported unit margin, the volume 
needed to preserve baseline GP is baseline quantity multiplied by baseline unit margin, divided by 
supported unit margin. If the supported unit margin is zero or negative, more volume does not solve that 
simple gross-profit problem.
Review what actually happened
After implementation, show delivered quantities, realised net prices, applicable costs and the resulting 
GP on the approved basis. Compare with the declared baseline; explain timing, mix and coverage 
changes. A before/after increase is an observation, not automatic proof that the SPA caused it.
Tender support should apply the same discipline to a defined fleet requirement, applicable parts and any 
total-cost-of-ownership assumptions. Usage, service events, labour, downtime and replacement life must 
not be invented to make a proposal look attractive. Tender support remains proposed. [D1]

Two pilots, different proofs
The shared aim is not identical analytical depth
I Motors in Portugal and ITUK in UK trucks are separate pilots. Their available levels of detail must 
remain distinct. The project baseline describes the Portugal pack as national sales-to-dealer information 
plus dealer self-assessment summaries, not purchasing attributed to individual Portuguese dealers. ITUK 
is the candidate for a dealer-granular commercial pilot. [D3]
Pilot
Initial proof and boundary
I Motors - Portugal
Deliver a national commercial review and a separately labelled view of the defined 
dealer-assessment population. Do not allocate national purchasing to named dealers 
or infer dealer performance from the national total.
ITUK - UK trucks
Test dealer-level purchasing patterns and an IDS connection, subject to valid 
identifiers, period coverage and confirmed dealer/site relationships. The described 
pack includes CY2025 and history; its coverage must determine the available 
reporting periods.
Portugal: a useful national return
The first useful output is a monthly national family-and-part review with a clear transaction-flow label. 
Add validated product comparisons and appropriate market context. Present IDS results for the actual 
assessment population, with dates and response coverage.
Keep those panels adjacent without implying that a national sales pattern belongs to an assessed dealer. 
A later dealer-level commercial connection requires an actual dealer-attributed feed and a validated 
relationship. It cannot be produced by dividing a national total among sites.
Before showing an upstream/downstream bridge, accept and reconcile the relevant ISZE-to-I Motors 
billing slice separately from the I Motors sales-to-dealer feed. The presence of one source does not 
establish the availability or completeness of the other.
ITUK: a dealer-level proof
Demonstrate a path from a validated dealer to a monthly AIM-family pattern, the contributing parts and 
an evidence-backed action. Where a BVP candidate exists, show its relationship and applicability 
evidence; otherwise show the missing-match state. Join an IDS assessment only when the site 
relationship and relevant dates are supported.
Normalise neither recorded quantities nor reference-valued totals into "actual billing" without the 
necessary billing evidence. Establish whether the purchasing account represents a single site, multiple 
sites or another entity before assigning a site-level result.
Exit criteria for both
A pilot passes when one useful review can be reproduced, explained to its intended recipient, corrected 
through the submission process and followed into an owned action. It must also demonstrate the 
unavailable-data state. A pilot does not pass merely because a map opens or because every dealer has 
been given a score.
No forced symmetry. A credible national Portugal pilot and a credible dealer-level ITUK pilot are 
preferable to two visually identical pilots built on incompatible evidence.

The workspace experience
Keep the portfolio; connect the work
The portfolio overview remains the entry point for understanding the range. Genuine/BVP comparison 
remains a central product experience. The sales cube stays removed and photography stays deferred. 
These are established scope decisions, not items to reopen implicitly during implementation. [D2]
The proposed navigation should expose four connected destinations: portfolio, network, commercial 
review and actions. AIM, IDS and PRISM inform these destinations rather than requiring the user to 
rebuild the same selection in three separate products.
Distributor and dealer views are different
The distributor view, or DB view, is a commercial workspace: purchasing, covered sales-to-dealer 
activity, product mix, market context and agreed actions. An IDS summary in that view must be labelled 
as results from a defined dealer self-assessment population, never as a distributor-audit score. [D5]
The dealer view shows only the commercial records legitimately assigned to the dealer or site. It should 
display the source identity, relationship status and coverage. Where the account covers several sites, 
present the appropriate account-level result instead of inventing a site allocation.
A distributor workspace is a user-facing view. A database-backed deployment is a separate technical 
choice; the two should not share an ambiguous "DB mode" label.
Preserve the analytical context
Opening a dealer, family, part or IDS follow-up must preserve the selected period, clock, transaction flow 
and population where applicable. When the next view cannot support that context, explain the 
restriction before changing it. A switch from actual billing to reference valuation must be visible, not 
hidden in a tooltip.
Keep a compact context strip near the headline result: scope, period, flow, measure basis, freshness and 
coverage. Make source and metric details available through a direct evidence link. Unknown and withheld 
values need distinct states; they must not render as zeros.
Maps do not establish responsibility
The Network Atlas may help users navigate known sites. Country, submission cohort or a map location 
does not validate which distributor is responsible for a site. Responsibility needs an explicit, dated 
relationship. Unresolved sites should be shown as unresolved, not silently assigned. [D5]
A usable return, not a dead-end dashboard
A commercial review should open the relevant parts and comparison evidence, create an action without 
retyping the scope, and export a readable evidence packet. Actions should return to their source analysis. 
The product should make a correction visible wherever the affected result appears.
The first release should favour a small number of complete paths over many incomplete pages. A user 
should always know what the screen measures, how to investigate it and what can be done next.

Logical data architecture
Separate source records, relationships and meaning
The proposed architecture has four layers: retained source submissions, accepted normalised records, 
versioned business definitions, and published views/actions. These are logical responsibilities; the choice 
of storage platform or application framework is a separate implementation decision.
Record group
Required responsibility
Source batch and 
coverage
Retain file identity, checksum, source owner, period, revision, schema, declared 
coverage, acceptance state and control totals.
Commercial facts
Store each transaction flow separately with source keys, dates, part IDs, quantities, 
units and monetary basis when available.
Product and 
applicability
Version AIM assignments, tier, descriptions, supersessions, kits, alternatives and 
applicability. Keep each relationship's evidence and status.
Network and IDS
Separate organisations, purchasing accounts, physical sites, dated distributor 
relationships, assessments and assessment items.
Market and demand 
context
Retain fleet vintages, segment definitions, competitor observations, reference prices 
and versioned service-demand assumptions.
Metrics, findings and 
actions
Store definitions, input versions, scope, calculation lineage, evidence status, owner, 
approvals and outcome history.
Joins must not manufacture volume
State the expected relationship for each join and test for duplication. A multi-applicability part joined to 
several vehicle bands must not multiply the original purchasing total. An account linked to several sites 
must not produce several copies of its commercial result.
Preserve original part identifiers alongside normalised keys. Keep unresolved mappings in an explicit 
bucket; do not discard their quantities from the reconciliation. Test totals before and after classification, 
relationship mapping and valuation. Where a transformation deliberately changes the measure, 
document and reconcile that change.
One definition for each named metric
A shared metric register should define GPS, PMS, GP, recorded quantity, actual billing, reference 
valuation and modelled demand. Each definition needs an owner, formula, eligible scope, exclusions, 
reporting clock and version. Different measures must not reuse the same label merely because they 
occupy the same screen position.
Build in correction and reproducibility
Retain accepted revisions rather than overwriting history without trace. A published result should 
identify the batch versions, relationship versions and metric version used to produce it. A correction 
should trigger recomputation of dependent results and a visible restatement, while preserving the earlier 
published snapshot for review.
Store a finding as more than prose: include its scope, underlying query or calculation reference, evidence 
links and status. An AI-generated explanation may summarise these records; it must not become the 
source of its own quantities or silently redefine a business metric.

Governance, access and release discipline
Accountabilities should be explicit
The proposed product owner decides scope and acceptance. Data owners validate source coverage and 
corrections. Metric owners approve business definitions. Commercial owners approve interventions and 
support terms. Technical owners maintain the application, permissions, testing and release record. Assign 
people to these roles before expanding beyond the pilots.
IDS interpretation belongs with the accountable standards owner. Product equivalence and fitment 
require an appropriate technical authority. A classification suggestion or AI-generated match cannot 
substitute for that approval.
Protect the value exchange
Distributors should receive their authorised information and approved comparative context, not 
unrestricted access to other distributors' records. Dealer views should follow validated relationships and 
access permissions. Aggregated comparisons should account for the risk that a small population reveals 
an individual participant.
Optional prices, costs, stock and service information need explicit purpose, access and retention controls. 
Cross-distributor price sharing and competitively sensitive benchmark design should pass internal legal 
and competition review before release. These are proposed product safeguards, not a conclusion that 
any particular sharing arrangement has been approved.
Treat imported files and narrative notes as data, not executable instructions. Automated assistants 
should have only the access and actions required for the review, and commercial changes should remain 
subject to the designated approval process.
A release is a retrievable deliverable
Every accepted release should have one stable index naming the version, date, owner, entry point, 
included modules, data snapshot and known limitations. List the actual deliverable filenames and their 
integrity checks. Identify the portfolio, Atlas and distributor-view entry points explicitly.
A temporary browser preview is not the release location. A label such as R1 or a statement that a build is 
complete is not evidence that the package exists, opens correctly and contains the agreed scope. Retain 
earlier releases, but make the accepted release unambiguous.
Test the failures that would damage trust
Before release, test duplicate submissions, revised months, missing periods, returns, unresolved dealers, 
multi-site accounts, multi-band applicability and unpriced parts. Also test that actual billing cannot 
silently switch to a reference valuation and that repeated market actuals cannot be summed across 
bands.
Regression tests must preserve the corrected tonnage v2 behaviour, the removed sales cube and the 
agreed portfolio/Genuine-BVP scope. Verify the Portugal national-only boundary and the distinction 
between IDS self-assessments and distributor commercial views. [D2, D3, D5, D6]
Acceptance record. Keep the tested scenarios, results, outstanding issues and approval with the release. 
Saving a rule or discussing a fix is not proof that the running product has adopted it.

Delivery gates and success measures
Foundation gate: establish the baseline
Create the release index, source-coverage register, metric register and unresolved relationship list. 
Identify which capabilities are accepted, demonstrated only, proposed or blocked. Confirm PRISM's 
functional boundary. Preserve existing scope decisions before adding new features.
Pass condition: a reviewer can identify the accepted product, the sources behind it and the 
interpretation rules it applies. There is no ambiguous "latest" file and no silent redefinition of a metric.
Pilot gate: complete two honest paths
Deliver the Portugal national review and the ITUK dealer-level review at their supported levels. Include a 
real monthly series, part-level explanation, a validated comparison or an explicit limitation, and an owned 
follow-up. Exercise a correction and show its effect.
Pass condition: each pilot can explain its figures, reproduce its result and demonstrate what is 
unavailable. Passing Portugal does not certify ITUK's dealer mappings, and passing ITUK does not create 
Portuguese dealer data.
Participation gate: make the return repeatable
Run the agreed monthly submission and return process. Measure accepted coverage, exceptions, 
correction turnaround and the work needed to produce a usable review. Ask whether the distributor 
used the output in a commercial conversation, not merely whether someone logged in.
Pass condition: participants can maintain the feed, recognise its limitations and receive a useful review 
without a bespoke rebuild each month. Targets should be agreed after the pilot baseline is known.
Expansion gate: add only demonstrated value
Expand to additional markets or optional data exchanges after the relevant controls work. Consider 
tender support, richer availability analysis or photography only when their benefit and maintenance 
ownership are clear. Do not restore deferred features merely to create the appearance of progress.
What should be measured
Track data reliability, review usefulness and commercial outcomes separately. Useful measures include 
valid mapped coverage, restated periods, review preparation effort, actions reviewed or closed, and 
realised GP for bounded interventions where it can be calculated. Avoid treating logins, modelled 
opportunity euros or the number of generated findings as realised commercial value.
Decisions needed for the next edition
Confirm the product and metric owners; approve the minimum feed and return package; confirm PRISM's 
boundary; validate the pilot relationship rules; approve access and comparative-output rules; and name 
the accepted release location. Each unresolved decision should have an owner and a due date, rather 
than being resolved by assumption in the application.
The next milestone is a complete, trusted decision path - not the largest possible 
dashboard.

Definitions and project decision trail
Working definitions
AIM: Aftermarket Intelligence Model. IDS: Isuzu Dealer Standards; dealer self-assessments. PRISM: 
retained working name; functional boundary to be confirmed. BVP: the user-facing name for the relevant 
alternative parts tier; not a synonym for any aftermarket product.
DB: distributor when describing the business view. UIO: units in operation. SPA: Special Price 
Agreement. GP: gross profit in currency. GM: gross margin as a percentage. CY / FY: calendar year / 
fiscal year; separate reporting clocks.
GPS / PMS: distinct business lenses with separately approved scope and formulas. Actual billing: 
monetary records from the named transaction flow. Reference valuation: quantity valued with a stated 
pricebook, not actual billing. Modelled opportunity: an estimate subject to its assumptions, not an 
observed loss.
