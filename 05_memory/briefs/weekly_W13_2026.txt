[3.1] 2026 VAN ZEV MANDATE TARGET: 24% (CARS) / ~33% (VANS, CUMULATIVE TRAJECTORY)
The UK ZEV mandate requires approximately 24–33% of new van registrations in 2026
to be zero-emission. Penalties for non-compliance: £15,000 per allowance (post
April 2025 revision, previously £18,000).

Flexibility mechanisms available:
- Banking: excess credits banked up to 3 years
- Borrowing: up to 25% of annual requirement borrowed forward (down from 75% in 2024)
- Car-van credit transfer: bidirectional from 2025 (0.4 rate car→van, 2.0 van→car)
- Trading window: 1 November–31 December each assessment year

D-Max classification note: D-Max is classified as an LCV (light commercial vehicle)
for ZEV mandate purposes despite its pick-up truck form factor. Isuzu UK's launch
of the D-Max EV in March 2026 is therefore directly relevant to ITUK's compliance
position as a seller of new vehicles.

ISZE implication: ZEV mandate pressure on ITUK is accelerating D-Max EV uptake.
Every new EV unit registered reduces future ICE PMS parts demand in that vehicle's
lifecycle. Golden Window revenue modelling assumption (2025–2032 ICE parts peak)
remains valid but the transition timeline is compressing faster than the base model
assumed. EV D-Max deliveries beginning Q1 2026 means the UIO EV share will be
non-trivial by FY2028.

THREE THINGS THAT MATTER THIS WEEK:

1. EV PARTS READINESS IS NOW URGENT.
   EV D-Max units in the field from March 2026 means first EV warranty and service
   jobs will appear at ITUK dealers within weeks to months. If IE40 doesn't have
   EV-specific PNs properly coded, ISZE loses those jobs to direct IML supply or
   improvised alternatives. Escalate to IML: what is the EV D-Max PN introduction
   schedule and IE40 coverage date? This is more urgent than lubricants right now.

2. THE PMS CAPTURE RATE IS STILL very low. NOTHING HAS CHANGED STRUCTURALLY.
   Market trends confirm older fleets are being kept longer (high vehicle costs,
   finance rates). This is good for PMS demand. But a very low oil filter capture rate
   at ITUK means ISZE is not benefiting. The structural problem is dealer behaviour
   and ITUK's sourcing choices, not market demand. The PMS joint programme (pilot)
   remains the priority lever — but there is no evidence it has changed capture
   rates yet. Avoid claiming PMS progress until the numbers show it.

3. LUBRICANTS: IML SANCTION IS THE BLOCKER, MARKET TIMING IS SECONDARY.
   UK lubricants market is growing (4–6% CAGR) with pricing pressure from
   additives — not the worst environment for a genuine BESCO launch. But the
   business case and 137,520L demand model are ready; IML go-ahead is not.
   Do not over-invest in lubricants market preparation until IML commits. Risk:
   spending ISZE capacity on a project that may not be sanctioned in FY2027.

BACKGROUND: The D-Max winning Pick-Up of the Year for the 5th year running is
genuinely good news — brand strength supports registrations, which supports UIO
growth, which supports the Golden Window thesis. But awards don't flow through
to parts revenue automatically. The gap between UIO potential and parts realisation
is still the core problem, and the EV transition is accelerating the urgency
to maximise ICE parts revenue in the 2025–2030 window before structural decline.
