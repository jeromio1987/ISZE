[1.1] UK PICKUP REGISTRATIONS COLLAPSE >50% YoY IN MARCH 2026 — D-MAX DIRECTLY HIT
In March 2026, UK pickup registrations fell more than 50% year-on-year. The
comparison is stark: March 2025 saw >8,000 pickups registered in the rush before
the April 2025 double-cab BIK tax change deadline. March 2026 recorded just ~3,700.

The double-cab BIK tax change — treating most double-cab pickups as company cars
(CO₂-based BIK, 3%–37%) rather than the flat commercial rate (£4,020/yr) — came
into force April 6 2025. The pull-forward in Q1 2025 is now a structural hangover:
fleet buyers have largely paused, and the D-Max is squarely in this segment.

Impact for ISZE:
- Weaker new vehicle sales in 2025–2026 means fewer new D-Max units entering the
  UK parc. UIO growth will slow materially vs. Hasegawa projections.
- Lower parc inflow delays the PMS parts opportunity by 1–2 years — the vehicles
  that drive high-value PMS demand (2–5 year old units) won't materialise as fast.
- Counterintuitively: existing ICE D-Max owners holding vehicles longer (due to
  BIK uncertainty around EV D-Max) strengthens near-term parts demand on the
  existing parc. Repair-over-replace dynamic is POSITIVE for ISZE volumes now.
- D-Max V-Cross Commercial (ICE, no double-cab BIK hit as van classification)
  deliveries from Nov 2025 — this variant is exempt from the tax change and may
  partially offset the registration decline.

Sources: EV Powered, Motester.co.uk, Fleet News

[1.2] UK AFTERMARKET ENTERING "REPAIR CULTURE" CYCLE — STRUCTURAL TAILWIND
With vehicle prices elevated and finance costs high, UK operators are holding
vehicles longer rather than replacing them. The 2026 aftermarket is characterised
by four main themes: digitalization, predictive thinking, repair culture, and
workshop modernisation. Turbochargers is the fastest-growing replacement segment.

The UK CV aftermarket CAGR is estimated at ~1.5% through 2033, with the commercial
vehicles segment projected to roughly double from $4.0B (2024) to $8.0B by 2035.

Impact for ISZE:
- "Make it last" behaviour directly benefits genuine parts penetration. Operators
  keeping older D-Max and N-Series units need maintenance parts: exactly where
  ISZE's PMS programme plays.
- The risk: older vehicles increasingly source from aftermarket (price-sensitive),
  not OEM. ISZE's competitiveness on high-frequency PMS parts is the critical lever.

Sources: CurrentCare UK, SPER Market Research

[3.1] ZEV MANDATE LCV — MARKET RUNNING AT 9% VS 24% TARGET: STRUCTURAL NON-COMPLIANCE
Electric van registrations fell 15.9% in March 2026 (3,543 units), leaving the
UK LCV market running at ~9% ZEV mix — barely one percentage point ahead of last
year — against a mandatory 2026 target of 24%. This is a severe gap.

Manufacturers face fines of £18,000 per van if the 24% threshold is not met
collectively. Flexibility mechanisms (credit banking, borrowing, manufacturer
trading, and conversion to CO₂ scheme credits) allow some mitigation.

Government has confirmed diesel and hybrid vans can be sold until 2035, bringing
LCVs in line with passenger car PHEV policy and buying manufacturers runway.

Impact for ISZE:
- The underperformance of EV LCV adoption is good news for ICE D-Max parts demand.
  The "Golden Window" (elevated ICE parc demand before ZEV structural decline)
  is extending. The parc of ICE D-Max vehicles won't shrink as fast as feared.
- However: ITUK's D-Max EV (Feb 2026, £59,995) won't contribute meaningfully to
  parts revenue at ISZE. EV drivetrain = no EGR, no oil filter, no SCR/DPF/NOx
  sensor. Parts revenue per EV unit materially lower. Any EV growth cannibalises
  the high-margin ICE parts base.
- The 9% vs. 24% compliance gap creates manufacturer pressure. Isuzu UK may face
  ZEV compliance credit pressure or pricing incentives on D-Max EV — watch for
  below-cost EV promotions that distort ICE D-Max residuals (residual erosion
  makes dealers accelerate part-exchange, reducing average vehicle age in parc).

ZEV 2026 targets for reference:
  Cars: 33% ZEV required (2026) → 80% (2030) → 100% (2035)
  Vans: 24% ZEV required (2026) → 70% (2030) → 100% (2035)

Sources: EV Powered, Professional Van, EVA England, GOV.UK

[3.2] DOUBLE-CAB BIK CLASSIFICATION — CONFIRMED PERMANENT FROM APR 2025
HMRC has confirmed double-cab pickups (including D-Max double cab) are treated as
cars for BIK and Capital Allowances from 6 April 2025 onwards. Prior registrations
(ordered/leased before 6 April 2025) retain commercial vehicle flat-rate BIK
(£4,020/yr) until disposal, lease expiry, or 5 April 2029.

VED rate remains at commercial vehicle rate (£345/yr) — no BIK parity here.

Impact for ISZE: Confirmed structural headwind on new D-Max fleet volumes. The
pre-April 2025 pull-forward was a one-time event; the ongoing fleet market for
D-Max double-cab is structurally smaller. Single-cab and commercial variants
(V-Cross Commercial) remain unaffected.

EXECUTIVE SYNTHESIS (≤400 words)

This week's signals combine to paint a consistent picture: ICE parts demand is
holding stronger than feared, but the structural context is deteriorating for
new vehicle sales — and the two effects will diverge over 2–3 years.

KEY CALL #1 — PICKUP REGISTRATION COLLAPSE IS A PARTS TIMING ISSUE, NOT A VOLUME
LOSS (YET). The >50% drop in UK D-Max registrations in March 2026 matters for the
2028–2030 PMS wave, not for today. The existing parc (22,389 DVLA units) continues
to age into the high-demand PMS window. The danger is IML's Kosha model was built
on UIO growth assumptions that are now clearly optimistic given the BIK change.
If ISZE hasn't re-baselined UIO projections post-April 2025, that is a gap to close
before any FY2027 target-setting conversation.

KEY CALL #2 — ZEV MANDATE NON-COMPLIANCE EXTENDS THE GOLDEN WINDOW, BUT CREATES
EV CANNIBALISATION RISK. The UK LCV market is running at 9% ZEV vs. 24% mandate.
This is positive for ICE parts longevity. But the D-Max EV is already in market
(Feb 2026, £59,995). Every EV D-Max registered is a vehicle that will never buy
an EGR, oil filter, or NOx sensor from ISZE. The EV fleet is small now — but the
trajectory matters for the 2028+ revenue model. ISZE should begin tracking D-Max EV
registrations separately from ICE, so the parts revenue model can be decoupled.

KEY CALL #3 — EGR AFTERMARKET PRICING CONFIRMS EXPOSURE ON OLDER PARC. The UK
aftermarket offers D-Max EGR valves from £45 (generic) to £482. ISZE genuine EGR
(€235.55 ≈ £198 at dealer level) sits in the mid-upper range. On vehicles >5 years
old, price-sensitive operators will shift to generic. This is not new — but the
"repair culture" trend (operators holding vehicles longer) amplifies the exposure.
The PMS programme needs to make the case for genuine parts on older vehicles — the
competitiveness data from the Dashboard is the tool for this.

KEY CALL #4 — BESCO TIMING: RISING CRUDE PRICES ARE AN ARGUMENT TO ACCELERATE.
Diesel and crude are elevated (diesel ~181p/litre, fuel duty rising Sep 2026). Oil-
linked aftermarket products are inflating. Texaco Delo pricing pressure provides
a window for BESCO to enter competitively. If IML sanction is still pending after
Feb 2026 MTG, this week is a reasonable moment to escalate for a decision —
the market window is tightening.

  □ Begin tracking D-Max EV vs. ICE registrations separately (DVLA or ITUK data)
