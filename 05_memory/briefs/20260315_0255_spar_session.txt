The correct conclusion is not “ITUK is definitely at fault,” but rather: **the UK market opportunity appears materially under-monetised relative to parc size, and the causes must now be evidenced.** Those causes may include dealer execution weakness, aftermarket substitution, fleet contract margin absorption, or route-to-market leakage outside the dealer network.

**The PMS gap is real and urgent, but it does not by itself absolve pricing.**  
The N-Series oil filter capture rate of roughly **very low** is structurally abnormal and indicates that ISZE/ITUK are missing a large share of routine maintenance demand. That gap is too large to be explained by seasonality or minor forecasting error, even allowing for future scrap-rate refinement. However, the gap should be framed as a **root-cause investigation trigger**, not as proof that price is irrelevant. If genuine filters are materially uncompetitive versus aftermarket alternatives, then targeted pricing action on high-substitution parts may be required. If fleet maintenance is occurring through contracts or independents, then route-to-market and channel design may matter more than list price alone.

**The Golden Window remains strategically valid, but only if capture improves quickly.**  
The UK ZEV mandate and long commercial replacement cycles support the view that the current ICE parc will continue generating maintenance demand into the early 2030s. This creates a finite opportunity to maximise parts revenue before structural EV transition reduces the addressable ICE base. But the window benefits ISZE only if genuine capture improves. An ageing fleet serviced through aftermarket channels is not an ISZE asset. Therefore, the Golden Window should be used not as a rhetorical justification for holding price, but as the commercial reason to accelerate corrective action now.

**Lubricants should be positioned as a channel activation and visibility tool, not assumed stickiness.**  
BESCO has strategic value because it can:
- create servicing visibility by dealer/account
- open workshop conversations
- displace competitor lubricant share
- potentially improve attachment of filters and routine service parts

But ISZE should avoid claiming that lubricant adoption will automatically drive genuine parts loyalty. That is a testable hypothesis, not a proven fact. The business case should therefore be framed around a pilot with clear KPIs: dealer adoption, litres sold, attachment rates to filters, and visibility gained on active service points.

**The case to IML must combine pricing discipline with a UK recovery plan.**  
ISZE should not present this as a binary choice between “hold methodology” and “concede to ITUK.” The stronger position is:  
1. the 2026 increase is methodologically and economically defensible;  
2. ISZE recognises that UK genuine capture is materially below potential;  
3. ISZE is prepared to address this through targeted commercial actions rather than blanket price retreat.

That is more credible to IML than a purely defensive pricing argument.

What is weak is the leap from those facts to the conclusion that **pricing is largely exonerated and ITUK execution is the main problem**. That may prove true, but it is not yet proven.

The strongest brief is therefore:
