[1.3] UK AFTERMARKET: "REPAIR CULTURE" STRENGTHENS — FAVOURABLE FOR ISZE PARTS PIPELINE
Industry research (Grand View, MarketResearch Future, CurrentCare) confirms UK
automotive aftermarket expected to grow from ~$17.5bn (2025) to ~$20bn by 2033
(CAGR ~1.5%). Drivers: aging vehicle park, high vehicle replacement costs, rising
finance costs pushing fleets to hold assets longer. Key 2026 trend: workshops
demanding faster part data accuracy, application listings, e-commerce integration.

Impact for ISZE:
- Ageing UIO (ITUK DVLA: 22,389 vehicles, mean age rising) directly feeds this
  tailwind. Older N-series trucks requiring more frequent PMS and repair. ISZE's
  PMS opportunity is not theoretical — it is riding a structural market current.
- EQ-HIT gap remains the blocker: if workshops cannot identify the correct ISZE
  part number quickly, they source aftermarket. The repair-culture tailwind benefits
  IAMs as much as OEM parts unless ISZE improves catalogue accessibility.
- Commercial vehicle spare parts global market forecast to reach $130bn by 2030
  (CAGR 4.9%), with electrification and connected fleet platforms as growth vectors.
  ISZE must position now for EV-compatible service portfolio (12V ancillaries,
  cabin parts, brakes) before ICE decline compresses the mix.

[3.1] 2026 VAN ZEV MANDATE: 24% TARGET NOW ACTIVE — TIGHTER REGIME THAN 2025
From 1 January 2026, 24% of new van registrations must be zero-emission. Non-
compliance fine: originally £18,000/van; updated (April 2025 consultation response)
to £15,000/van. Borrowing flexibility reduced to 25% in 2026 (was 75% in 2024).
Flexibility mechanisms remain: CO2 credits, pooling/trading, car-to-van credit
transfer (1 car credit = 0.4 van credits).

Year 1 (2025) outcome: UK van market met target, but via CO2 credits (6.8% ZEV
registrations + 5.3% CO2 conversion) rather than pure ZEV volume. Year 2 is
harder — borrowing ability severely curtailed.

Sources: BVRLA, ICCT, Autovista24, FleetNews

Impact for ISZE:
- ITUK faces more compliance pressure in 2026 than 2025. D-Max EV (launched
  Feb 2026) is a critical compliance asset for Isuzu UK. Commercial priority to
  push EV D-Max volumes will likely mean aggressive retail pricing/incentives on
  EV variants.
- Risk: ITUK EV focus diverts commercial energy from ICE D-Max accessory/parts
  campaigns. ISZE should be alert to ITUK channel resources being re-allocated.
- Opportunity: ITUK needs to maximise EV D-Max retail performance. ISZE can offer
  to support EV-compatible accessories pipeline (tow bars, bed liners, cabin parts)
  to capture accessory revenue on new EV units even if ICE parts revenue per unit
  is lower.

[3.2] ZEV MANDATE FLEXIBILITY — GOVERNMENT KEEPING CREDITS ARCHITECTURE INTACT
Government has not withdrawn ZEV flexibility mechanisms despite pressure from
vehicle manufacturers for more headroom. The ICCT (May 2025) flagged risks in
proposed changes — flexibility creates loopholes that could allow non-EV volumes
to persist under the credit shell. Government position: maintain strictness but
offer some fine reduction (£18K → £15K).

Impact for ISZE:
- Regulatory architecture is stable — no surprise relaxation that would massively
  accelerate EV transition beyond current trajectory. ICE D-Max will remain
  legally saleable through the mandate period with appropriate credits. This de-
  risks the Golden Window forecast.

W15 2026 — FY2026 CLOSE WEEK. The external landscape has not shifted adversely for
ISZE's core thesis, but two internal clocks are running out.

The D-Max EV is live and performing as expected: award recognition, strong brand
PR, but commercially niche at £59,995. The ICE D-Max retains full commercial
viability — the 24% ZEV mandate for vans uses a credit architecture that keeps ICE
D-Max legally saleable, and the 163-mile EV range limits real-world adoption to
sustainability-driven corporate fleets. The Golden Window is open. This week's
intelligence reinforces it.

The structural market current is also in ISZE's favour. The UK "repair culture"
trend — fleets holding vehicles longer, rising independent workshop demand for
accurate parts data — maps directly onto ISZE's UIO (22,389 DVLA-verified) and
PMS thesis. The risk, as always, is that IAM alternatives are live and accessible.
The Tranzparts NOx sensor listing (backordered) is a small but illustrative signal:
even emissions-critical parts are being contested by IAM suppliers. ISZE's defence
is speed-to-part and catalogue accuracy, not price.

Two immediate actions stand out from this week's scan:

First, IML lubricants sanction. FY2026 is closing this week. The BC v2 is filed,
the volume model is corrected (137,520L), but IML has not given written go-ahead
since February. This is not a delay that can be carried into FY2027 without reset
cost. A clear IML position — yes or no — is needed now so ISZE can either launch
a FY2027 pilot or formally close the file and redirect the commercial resource.

Second, Hillhead 2026. ITUK will be at Hillhead quarrying exhibition in June with
full sales and aftersales presence. This is a direct channel to the N-series
operators who generate ISZE's highest PMS revenue potential. The window to arrange
ISZE parts material inclusion in ITUK's stand is now — 6–8 weeks before preparation
locks in. A brief conversation with the ITUK parts team (copy James Marsh) before
end of April costs nothing and could land genuine parts messaging in front of fleet
operators at zero ISZE media spend.

Internally, W15 is FY-close week. The deliverables that constitute ISZE's credible
execution record — PMS pilot, lubricants BC, AIM classification, pricing
documentation — should be confirmed as filed and retrievable before the FY review.
Not because Kosha is reachable, but because the paper trail is the protection.
