[3.1] ZEV MANDATE — 2026 TARGETS AND FLEXIBILITY LANDSCAPE
2026 confirmed targets:
  - Cars:  33% ZEV (up from 22% in Year 1)
  - Vans:  24% ZEV (up from first-year target)
  - Fine:  £18,000 per non-compliant van unit

Flexibility mechanisms in 2026:
  - CO2 flexibility extended to 2029 (was expiring 2026). Manufacturers can create
    credits by reducing emissions from non-ZEV fleet vs. 2021 baseline.
  - Car-to-van and van-to-car credit transfer now available for multi-segment OEMs.
  - Non-compliance cap: 25% (tightened from 90% in 2024, 50% in 2025).

Isuzu-specific read:
  - Isuzu is essentially a mono-segment LCV OEM in UK ZEV terms. No car portfolio
    for credit transfer. EV D-Max must carry compliance load.
  - At £18K/van fine, ITUK has structural incentive to push EV D-Max volume hard.
  - Risk: EV sales pressure → ICE D-Max deprioritised → ICE parts pipeline narrows.
    For ISZE: manage the Golden Window, don't let ITUK's ZEV pressure pull resources
    away from ICE parts programme prematurely.

★ W14 EXECUTIVE SYNTHESIS (max 400 words)

Three themes this week — one time-sensitive, two strategic:

1. EV ACCESSORIES: FIRST-MOVER WINDOW IS OPEN NOW

D-Max EV is in customer hands. This is faster than most anticipated. The aftermarket
is not yet organised around EV-specific fitment — Truckman, 4x4AT, and Pegasus4x4
are still listing ICE D-Max accessories without confirmed EV applicability. Isuzu's
own accessories page on isuzu.co.uk makes no distinction. This window will close
fast once aftermarket suppliers catch up. DW and HT need to confirm IAS (Thailand)
accessory catalogue compatibility with EV D-Max body dimensions this month. Hard
tops and load-area protection are the priority — these are high-margin items with
early adopter willingness to pay. If ISZE confirms compatibility and publishes a
clear fitment list before third-party suppliers do, genuine accessory revenue on
EV D-Max is protected. If not, ISZE will be playing catch-up by Q3 FY2027.

2. EGR VALVE 2x PRICE GAP — THIS BELONGS IN THE ITUK POSITION PAPER

The ITUK pricing discussion is still unresolved. The EGR valve is an ABC:A, high-
frequency D-Max failure item (PN 8983320160, €235.55 IE40). At dealer sell-out
it's ~£300. Aftermarket remanufactured is ~£150. The gap is 2x. That is the
real competitiveness pressure facing ISZE — not ITUK's complaint about IE40
formula methodology. Any further price concession on this part accelerates
aftermarket substitution on the highest-revenue items in the basket. The current
position paper defends the FX/BP methodology. That's correct, but it's a technical
argument. Adding a "pricing vs. substitution risk" section — with the EGR example
as anchor data — transforms the paper from defensive to strategic. It frames ISZE's
pricing as a floor, not a ceiling, and puts the burden on ITUK to demonstrate that
lower prices would actually recover lost capture rate (which they cannot).

threat (£309K Texaco at dealer level) was already in the BC. The broader addressable
