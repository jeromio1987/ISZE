This week's dominant signal is the D-Max EV entering the market — first deliveries in
May 2026, priced at £60–62K ex-VAT. The product is technically credible (1-tonne payload,
3.5-tonne towing, 4WD — class firsts for electric), and the fleet win with National Grid

good news in the short term: the EV parts channel is undefined, and if IML routes EV
components direct to IMIE or ITUK bypassing ISZE, that is a revenue exclusion risk.

The 2.2L diesel launch (July) is the more immediate operational item. New engine = new
parts. IE40 catalogue must be updated before fleet operators start asking for 1,000km
service kits for a powertrain that isn't in the system. The PMS programme built around
the 1.9L needs a variant check.

On the competitive front, aftermarket pricing pressure from digital platforms continues
to intensify, but the ISZE/ITUK route-to-market (programme-based, fleet-attached) is
relatively protected if PMS and lubricants programmes actually land. The structural
problem — very low oil filter capture rate — is not a market problem, it's an execution
problem. DPF Parts Direct and Tranzparts are picking up the slack. That gap is
quantifiable and should be in the FY2027 action plan as a recoverable revenue line.

ZEV mandate tightening (24% van target, borrow flexibility cut to 25%) will squeeze
fleet operators through 2026. It does not threaten near-term N-series parts revenue,
but it confirms the Golden Window logic: peak ICE parc is NOW. The next 18 months are
the highest-yield period for PMS and lubricants campaigns. Delay costs compound.

Kosha read: nothing in this week's market news changes the structural gap. D-Max #2 in
