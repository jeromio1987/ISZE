# Proposal: validate the parts classification before broader commercial use

**For management discussion | 20 September 2026 | Draft**

## Decision requested

Approve a bounded technical validation of our existing parts classification and a limited distributor pilot. Do not yet approve unrestricted use of the current assignments for detailed category decisions or automated price changes.

## Why the approach matters

We should retain two separate three-level classifications: the catalogue product tree and the AIM parts family, meaning our aftersales grouping. Each serves a different question. Keeping them separate lets us compare category performance without treating a catalogue group and a vehicle system as equivalent labels.

Used with verified product identity, relevant fleet information and dated market evidence, the classification can support clearer sales analysis, more specific distributor discussions, service-range reviews and better-founded decisions on pricing, value parts and availability. These are intended uses, not a measured financial return already achieved.

## What the review established

The 20 September classification review found current assignments that do not preserve the recorded product identity, including a clutch slave cylinder labelled as a pivot and a heater core labelled as a water pipe. It also identified malformed part-type fields. A recent update or a completed catalogue lookup is therefore not sufficient evidence that an assignment is ready for general use.

The review also established that catalogue coverage and aftersales-family coverage are different measures. Neither is a technical accuracy rate. Open categories, missing detailed types and verified-correct assignments need to remain distinguishable.

Source: internal full-file classification and catalogue-evidence review, 20 September 2026. The technical findings are traceable in the accompanying internal evidence register. No uplift in turnover or profit is claimed.

## Work to approve

Assign a technical/category owner to approve the affected product definitions and reviewed corrections. Preserve component, assembly and kit distinctions; do not replace an unresolved item with the nearest available label. Require traceability from catalogue evidence to the accepted classification, and agree which classification each analysis should use.

Run a limited distributor pilot using reviewed parts only. Establish the relevant market, sales stage, period and fleet basis before interpreting a service opportunity. Keep distributor purchasing separate from sales to dealer and actual end-customer evidence. Evaluate the action on repeat business and achieved contribution, not the initial order alone.

## Conditions for wider adoption

The accepted classification must have valid identifiers, valid category paths, visible unresolved items and technical evidence for the reviewed products. The analyses used in the pilot must reproduce the agreed totals without double-counting, and must preserve product identity at the level required by the commercial decision.

Broader adoption should follow demonstrated reliability and a useful commercial result. Comprehensive product-attribute enrichment remains a later decision. No catalogue-wide price change, guaranteed financial uplift or new platform is included in this request.
