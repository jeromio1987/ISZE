# IDS question master

Internal until this wording is accepted. This is not a dealer file and it does not change this year's scores.

The continental sheet has 196 questions and 280 points. The sections are not equal: Premises 46, Organisation 80, Service 80, Parts 47, Vehicle sales 27. This master keeps the five sections and the 280-point total, and it cuts the list to 70 questions by joining lines that ask the same thing.

Twelve questions at 4 points plus two at 8 points is 64 points, not 56. That would move the total to 320 and break a like-for-like percentage. Each section is therefore two questions at 8, eight at 4, and four at 2. That is 56. Five sections are 280.

UK trucks stay on their own list. They are not on this scale. UTI and UMI stay separate. A section that was not filled is not scored. It is not 0%.

## How a question is scored

Every question has the same answers: No, Partly, Yes. Some also have Not applicable.

- No is 0.
- Partly is half the question, so 1, 2, or 4.
- Yes is the full 2, 4, or 8.
- Not applicable is left out of that dealer's section total. It is not a full mark and it is not a zero.

The section result is points earned divided by points that applied. The dealer total is the same across the sections that were sat, not an average of the five percentages. A whole section with no answers is marked not scored and stays out of the total. That is the rule already used where premises, organisation, or vehicle sales were not filled.

Not applicable is allowed only where this master says so: the main brand forbids the Isuzu mark, the distributor confirms that no electric Isuzu is sold in the market, or the distributor does not offer the maintenance plan or warranty extension. The dealer does not tick Not applicable on its own.

The Isuzu sign manual means the brand rules for the Isuzu mark. On the old sheet those rules are named I.VIS and I.BIS. Those codes stay in the crosswalk. They are not in the question the auditor reads.

## Five Forms on the iPad

One Form of 196 lines will not be finished on an iPad. Use the SharePoint site you already use for IDS. Add one page called Start a dealer visit, then five Forms, one per section.

The page asks for country, dealer, date, and who is answering. Who is answering is either the dealer, in draft, or the distributor, confirming. Five buttons open the five Forms. Each Form is the 14 questions above, in order, plus one last question: photos of anything scored No. One upload for the section, not a photo on every line. The upload is shown every time. If a photo will not send, the Form can still be submitted. A No without a photo stays a No.

Answers land in one SharePoint list. That list is the score. Nobody retypes a result workbook.

Suggested columns on the answer list:

- Country, dealer, visit date, person, role (draft or confirmed), status
- For each question: the answer, the points earned, and whether it was not applicable
- For each section: points earned, points that applied, percent, and a flag if the whole section was not scored
- Dealer total: points earned, points that applied, percent
- One photo link per section

Do not trust the total that Forms shows on the iPad. Not applicable breaks a quiz total. Power Automate writes the official score: No is 0, Partly is half, Yes is full, and Not applicable is dropped from the points that applied. A confirmed row replaces that dealer's draft. Dealer Compare and the visit page read confirmed rows only.

The dealer may fill the same five Forms before the visit. That row is a draft. Forms will not open the distributor's Form with the dealer's ticks already filled in. The distributor reads the draft on the SharePoint page and submits its own answers. Where the two differ, the distributor's answer stands. A sheet of Yes with nothing to show does not become the score.

Dealer Compare stays the picture you send. It should read this list once the list exists. Until then, do not wait. The visit page below can be filled from this year's Dealer Compare by hand.

## The visit

Dealer Compare is how the distributor chooses who to visit. It is the wrong tool to walk around the workshop with. The visit agrees three actions. It does not create a new score.

Before the day, open Dealer Compare and pick dealers below the country average. Start with the weakest.

On site, if the Form is already confirmed, do not ask all 70 again. Confirm the two 8-point questions in each section, photograph what is No, and stop. If the Form is not in yet, fill it on the iPad during the visit. The distributor's answers are the ones that count.

The visit page shows five lines, one per section: the question in that section with the most points left on the table. Points left are the question's full points minus the points earned. If two questions leave the same points, show the 8-point question. If a section was not scored, skip it. A dealer who sat only service and parts gets two lines, not five zeros.

Under those lines, three empty actions. Three is the cap. The list should refuse a fourth open action for that dealer. A new action can be added when one closes, and there are still never more than three open.

Each action names the dealer, the failed question in the words of this master, the owner at the dealer, the date, and the proof. Proof is a photo or a document back on the same list. Closed without proof stays open.

Choose the three in this order. An 8-point question scored No. Then any other No on safety and waste (B09) or genuine parts (D01). Then the rest. Parts and Service come before a new outdoor sign when both are open.

The next month is a filter on that list, not a new pack: dealers visited, actions still open, actions closed, dealers not yet visited. The score does not move because an action closed. The score moves at the next IDS only. The visit is not a forecast of parts sales.

What you send the distributor is Dealer Compare, plus this visit page for the dealers they will walk. Do not build a separate study by hand for each country.
