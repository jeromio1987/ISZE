Diagnostic 2 — parts billed that never reached a dealer

The money splits into two problems that need opposite responses. Under-ordering means the parts are selling through to dealers and the distributor is simply not reordering enough. Genuine overstock means the parts are sitting still. Merging the two produces the wrong instruction.

Value of exception lines at the distributor, calendar 2025. Five distributors have a sales-to-dealer file; the other thirteen cannot be measured this way.

Under-ordering is the bigger number: €678k across 470 part numbers is selling through to dealers faster than the distributor reorders. €1.57M is genuinely sitting as overstock. A further 377 part numbers show dealer demand with no billing at all — that is not stock, it is demand we never invoiced.

Diagnostic 3 — bought, but not from us

The aftermarket shows up as mix, not as lost revenue on exposed part numbers. Direct exposure is low everywhere — the worst market is NORWAY at 2.1% of net sales. What actually moves is the share of the service-part euro that goes to Competitive parts rather than genuine.

The response follows the mix reading, not the exposure reading. Because exposed revenue is 0.2% to 2.1% of net sales, the aftermarket is not currently taking revenue on part numbers where we list an alternative. It is shifting what the distributor chooses to stock — which is a pricing, availability and counter-positioning problem, not a lost-sale recovery problem.

Diagnostic 4 — parts we bill that have no usable price

31 part numbers worth €592k in CY2025 are either absent from the price list or present with no usable price. This is mainly a lubricants gap, not a parts one: one lubricant oil alone is 73.3% of the whole gap. The fix is a price-list entry, not a project.

Three distributors that carry the story

One is the largest Diagnostic 1 gap and partly a measurement artefact. One is the worst rate on a large book. One is above 100% and is not a gap at all. ITUK uses a separate external standard below — do not blend with IUK or UMI.

IUK — the largest Diagnostic 1 gap, and part of it is our own measurement

€5.80M · 82.7% unbought
  • 47,783 light commercial vehicles in service, no trucks
  • €5.80M across the filter, brake and clutch families in CY2025
  • This number is inflated by our own assumption. The model applies a flat 20,000 km/yr while IUK's measured N35 Grafter fleet averages 14,494 km/yr. At measured distance the gap is €3.87M — a 33.3% smaller gap, with capture of 23.9%. The 17.3% capture reading at flat 20,000 km/yr is outdated for IUK and should not headline a visit.

Germany — the worst rate on a large book

€1.87M · 86.1% unbought
  • 13,780 vehicles in service, mixed light and truck class
  • Second largest Diagnostic 1 gap and the worst rate of any large book
  • Competitive-parts share 26.4%, so this is not a substitution story — the parts are simply not being bought from us or from anyone else we can see
  • The market's own measured distance would settle it the way Israel's 51,680 was settled

Ireland — above 100%, and not a gap

-0.0% unbought — i.e. more bought than expected
  • The only distributor above 100% of modelled expected demand
  • Either a genuinely exceptional market or a denominator set too low
  • Both readings are possible and they need opposite responses, so Ireland is unresolved and should not be quoted as either a strength or a weakness
  • Its dealer-side reading is — of expected demand reached dealers, which is the highest capture in the network
ITUK external standard (IML schedule basis): 22,389 trucks in service; 35.1% oil-filter cover; €2.04M modelled gap across five service categories. Use these figures when ITUK appears externally — not the IUK N35 fleet base or Israel UMI numbers.

What to do first

Five items, each tied to a distributor and to a number on this page.

  1. IUK — re-baseline the distance before the visit: at the measured 14,494 km/yr the gap is €3.87M, not €5.80M. Acting on the uncorrected number buys the wrong conversation.
  2. Germany — commission the market's own annual distance the way Israel's 51,680 was confirmed, then re-read 86.1% unbought on 13,780 vehicles.
  3. IUK and UTI — split €1.42M: the under-ordering portion needs a reorder conversation, the overstock portion a stop-order instruction.
  4. UMI — one lubricant price-list entry closes €434k of the €592k no-usable-price gap; lubricants together are 88.7% of it.
  5. Ireland — resolve the above-100% reading before it is quoted either way.

What this page does not claim

  • The demand side is modelled. The service-demand model applies one flat 20,000 km/yr annual distance and is being corrected per weight class using measured values (IUK 14,494, Israel 51,680, N35/N75/F-series provisional). Per-distributor direction is solid; absolute levels move when that lands, and IUK's moves most.
  • The fiscal-year leg is derived, not published. The capture file on disk carries calendar years only. Fiscal 2025 (April 2025 – March 2026) is produced by applying the fiscal-to-calendar ratio of PMS-tagged billing lines, per distributor and per family, to the published calendar actuals. The expected-demand model itself has no period dimension, so expected demand is the same annual figure in both periods. Treat the fiscal leg as a directional recut.
  • The replenishment figure is a five-distributor number, not a network one. Only five distributors have a sales-to-dealer file. Under fiscal 2025 it drops to four, because the bridge file has no dealer-sales evidence for Greece before August 2025.
  • Every modelled figure is marked modelled. Diagnostic 1 gaps are per-distributor ceilings from service intervals and fleet counts, not observed lost orders and not one additive network total.
  • Warranty parts cannot be split by service family on this page. The warranty claim extract matches the service-family part-number registry on only seven part numbers (EUR 1,174 of 2025 claims), so the warranty-excluded view corrects the billing leg exactly per distributor but cannot re-cut the demand gap by family. The effect on Diagnostic 1 is small.
  • Competitive-parts share is measured on the service-part euro and excludes part numbers where no alternative is listed, which is why it reads far higher than the exposure column.
  • No forward fleet figure appears. The 2026–2030 vehicles-in-service projection is overstated pending correction, so nothing on this page depends on it.
