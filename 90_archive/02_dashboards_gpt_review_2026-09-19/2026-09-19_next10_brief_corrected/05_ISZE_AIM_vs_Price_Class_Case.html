ISZE internal · classification case

AIM classification is how we will run the parts book

Price-class letters build a list price. AIM tells us which product we are managing, where the euro sits, and how to talk to the network. This pack asks ISZE to adopt AIM as the internal commercial map. This document is internal / private. A customer-facing version would be a separate document, written after internal approval, with its own wording review.

One sentence
Price classes construct the list; functional families organise the business.

We keep the letter factors for list construction. We use the functional families to describe what a part is, how a family moves, and what we bill. Exposure to the independent aftermarket is a separate commercial attribute evidenced per market, part and date — never read off a family name.

Why this is important

  1. One language from ISZE packs to, later, IML and distributors.
  2. Decisions in euro and family — not in letter soup.
  3. One spine for target spend per truck, capture, and sales to dealer.

Three levels — the sweet spot

What three levels unlock: L1 groups the book by an assigned commercial attribute — a modelling choice about how exposed a line is to the independent aftermarket, not a measurement of it. L2 ranks functional families by euro and by price move. L3 separates types that behave differently inside one family — without dumping thousands of part numbers on the table. Where exposure is claimed, it must carry its market, its date and the quality of the price match; the family name alone proves nothing.
Basis note. Family euro figures in this pack are on the in-scope pricing base: parts billed 1 Jul 2025 – 30 Jun 2026, customers in scope, priced on both sides of the revision. 90.6% of billed euro sits on a governed catalogue node; the balance is a named residual and is not presented as a family. The audit window (1 Jan 2025 – 30 Jun 2026) gives different euro for the same families — see the basis annex.
Page 4 · Why letters fail as a map

The letter trap — same code, different products

Competitive parts are mostly letter C (€6.79M) and V (€3.80M). That already mixes brakes, filters, exhaust, and steering. One letter cannot carry one commercial story.

Same product, split letters — Brake parts

C · €1.76M
V · €1.45M

Ask “how did Class V move?” and you mix brake content with oil filters and more.

Letter D is not a filter strategy

Inside Filters, oil and fuel are essentially all V. The entire competitive D block (~€516k) is Other filter — DEF / hydraulic residue — not “filters” as a parts manager means them.

Page 8 · Method

How we built it — so the numbers can be audited

Catalogue family → group → type from PR00, joined to every billed part. AIM workbook categories sit alongside. We quote the full catalogue join — not a thin source-flag slice that would drop to 13.5% of euro.

Collect billed part numbers + PR00 classification extract

2

Attach family / group / type by 10-character part key

3

Attach Parts Master categories by clean description match

4

Fill gaps with high-confidence suggestions or catalogue

5

Page 9 · Rollout path

ISZE first — then IML — then distributors

Today this pack is for ISZE. The same map is designed so that, once we approve it internally, we can send one language to customers.

Now

ISZE internal

  • Default axis for management packs
  • Price-revision readouts by family
  • Capture and competitive ranking
  • Letters retained as attribute only
Next

IML alignment

  • Show the map does not fight list maths
  • Same L1/L2/L3 in joint reviews
  • Clarify IML owns class factors
Then

All distributors

  • One workshop language across the network
  • Sales-to-dealer mix in family terms
  • Euro per truck where fleet exists
Limits we keep visible (trust): Other filter residual; thin air filter; four markets without fleet counts (~€1.54M euro still in totals); ~8.3% still unattributed to a real node; no elasticity claimed here. Classification works — the old scoring layer is not this case.
Page 10 · Decision

Closing ask

Adopt AIM catalogue classification (Level 1 → Level 2 → Level 3) as ISZE’s default commercial axis.

  • Management packs and price-revision readouts
  • Competitive-family ranking and capture discussions
  • Distributor reviews and sales-to-dealer mix — when we go external

Retain the old price-class letter only as an attribute next to the node, never as the storyboard. That keeps IML’s pricing maths intact and gives ISZE — and later every customer — a product tree the business can act on.
