Two clocks, and they disagree Is the business growing or shrinking? It depends which year you use.

Read this before quoting either number. The company plans on a financial year that runs April to March. The billing extracts arrive as calendar-year files. On the calendar the last year was down 2.9%. On the financial year it was up 1.9%. Both are correct. The calendar loss comes almost entirely from the run-in to March, which the fiscal year carries into the next period.

What this model does not tell you

  • How much volume responds to a price change has not been measured. There is one complete year of dealer sales at part level, which is not enough to fit it, so no claim is made here that a given price move causes a given volume change.
  • The target spend is known per market only, not per parts family. It is shown on every family row for context and must not be added down a family column.
  • The target spend is what the fleet could support at full list price, not a forecast. The actionable figure restates it at the price actually realised, and is still not an amount that can simply be collected.
  • The current year is part of a year. Every comparison involving it uses the same months on both sides, and the affected rows are marked.
  • The forward forecast of vehicles in service is overstated, by 9% in the first year rising to 59% by 2030 and worse in some markets. Nothing forward-looking is published here; the model stops at what actually happened.
  • A competing price is held on only a small part of the parts billed. Where there is none, that is an absence of evidence, not evidence of competitiveness. The two sources are different dates and different levels of the market and are never averaged together.
  • Credit notes and returns for the second quarter of 2026 are netted on the sign of the value alone, because the billing extract for that quarter does not carry the credit-note marker. That quarter's figures are therefore slightly less certain than the rest.
  • There is no online demand data: no web traffic and no search volume. Online presence enters only as a competing price and as a listing we can see.
  • The verdict on the value line is a rule applied to the value share and total units, not a measured outcome. Read it as a prompt to look, not as proof.
  • Part-number pricing depth (cost, margin, competitor price and the resulting lever) exists on a minority of the book. Everywhere else the answer stays at family or market level and the lever column is blank rather than guessed.
  • Two clocks are shown on purpose. The company plans on a financial year running April to March, while the billing extracts arrive as calendar-year files. The two can give different answers, so both are published side by side.
