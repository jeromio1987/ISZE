1Where total capture is still below 100%

Seventeen of eighteen markets sit below their wallet ceiling; only Ireland is above it (124%). The gap is €29.69M a year and it is not evenly spread — ITUK, IUK and UTI carry €22.37M of it, 75% of the total. ITUK is both the largest wallet and the worst rate in the network.1

Captured vs. uncaptured wallet by market · CY2025 · € million

The reason capture stalls below 100% is priced in, not mysterious. We sit upstream: our list price grosses up through the channel (distributor +40%, then dealer +27.5% = ×1.785) before it reaches the counter. Measured at list, 25 of 44 benchmarked part numbers are undercut by the aftermarket. Measured at the counter — the only comparison a workshop actually makes — 40 of 44 are undercut, covering 90% of the €12.91M benchmarked revenue, at a median of -47%. A genuine blower filter reaches the counter at roughly four times the aftermarket price. Capture below 100% is the arithmetic result.2

2Mix risk vs. true leakage

Conditional substitution sensitivity

The original-to-value unit-profit comparison is a scenario: it asks what the margin difference would be if the observed value-part volume had otherwise been original-part volume at the assumed price. It does not establish displacement. The same sale could replace an original sale, retain a customer who would otherwise leave, or win volume that would not have occurred. No displacement percentage is assumed.

These are not the same threat and they must never be added together. True leakage is wallet we never touch: €29.69M. Mix is a genuine sale that became a BVP sale — the customer stayed inside the tent, we earned a thinner margin. Across the whole parts book BVP is 3.5% of captured revenue (€1.06M), and the unit-profit difference if observed BVP volume had otherwise been genuine is €110,391 a year under that substitution scenario — not a proven realised loss.3

Leakage outweighs mix by roughly 269 to 1

€29.69M of revenue never touched, against €110,391 of gross profit lost to down-trade. Any FY28 programme that spends its energy on stopping BVP is optimising the smaller number by two orders of magnitude — and on oil filters it would actively destroy profit.

Unit-profit difference if a genuine sale is substituted by BVP · CY2025 · € per year (scenario)

The oil filter bar points the other way on purpose. A BVP oil filter earns €2.58 of gross profit per unit against €1.22 for genuine (36% vs 23% margin) — on our highest-volume line, down-trade is accretive, worth €44,819 a year. Mix is also concentrated rather than systemic: it is near-total in a handful of cells (UTI air filter 98% of units, UTI clutch kit 97%) while the network book stays at 3.5%. Treat mix as a margin-quality question per cell, never as a leakage number.4

3Top 5 commercial moves for the FY27–FY28 BP

FY27 needs no rescue: the plan lands at €35.32M against a €35.28M target, a €33,440 surplus (0.1%). The real ask is the step into FY28: €2.21M of incremental revenue, which the plan solves as a +3.81pp network uplift. That is the bar every move below is measured against. For orientation only: FY29 needs +8.73pp and FY30 +14.21pp on the same basis — the curve steepens, and Kosha's €52.1M FY2031 is not reachable on this trajectory and is not modelled here.5

Gate — half the PMS opportunity is a modelling artifact, and the one real IML factor isn't landing

€8.13M of the €16.05M "defend-zone" gap (51%) is clutch and urea demand that the model invents. Truck wear factors are applied flat to a service base that is 87% D-Max pickup — the model expects 30,428 clutch discs and 152,142 urea filters a year from a parc that largely has neither. The €2.45M IUK clutch line is the single biggest "opportunity" on the map and it is not real. Separately, the ITUK brake override — the only factor in the model sourced from IML's own data (2026-07-14, 100,000 km interval) — is silently dropped by the demand builder: ITUK brakes still run at the global 0.5 guess, so ITUK brake capture reads 58% where the IML basis gives 73%, and an unresolved per-axle question puts the true figure anywhere in a 36–73% band. Fix both before any PMS euro goes to IML. Note the network capture rate already moved 23.8% → 32.0% today when a related double-count was removed — no euro changed, but the headline did.6

Nothing above is blocked on revenue evidence — the wallet gap, the price position and the €328K/pt rate are all measured. What is blocked is the PMS demand model, and it is blocked in our favour: the honest pools are smaller than the map claims, and the two markets where we can defend a number line by line (UTI, and ITUK once its factor lands) are the two where the money actually is.
