ZEV Impact on Parts Demand
🔺
2025–2028: Parts demand RISES. Operators holding ICE vehicles longer means more service intervals, more repair events, more genuine parts consumption. This is the golden window.
🔻
2030–2035: Structural decline. ICE parc shrinks as ZEV mandate approaches 70–100%. Parts revenue follows parc with 1–2 year lag. The decline is non-recoverable for ICE parts.
⚡
EV parts profile unknown. D-Max EV will have very different parts profile — lower PMS, different failure modes, no EGR/clutch. Revenue bridge to EV parts requires D-Max EV launch data.

🏆 The Golden Window is the single most important strategic frame for ISZE's UK business. ZEV mandate will not kill parts revenue immediately — it will elevate it first. Fleet operators facing £18K penalties are holding their ICE vehicles longer, delaying replacement, and driving more km on existing vehicles. Every extra year an ICE D-Max stays on the road is 1–2 more service intervals, potential warranty-out repairs, and genuine parts revenue. ISZE must act NOW while the window is open — not in 2030 when it starts to close.

One-Page Brief for IML / IGS
ISZE UK Market Status — March 2026

The UK is ISZE's largest market by volume (€5.62M ISZE→ITUK) and faces the most acute ZEV mandate pressure in Europe. The Van ZEV mandate (16% in 2025 → 100% by 2035) directly targets the D-Max — our highest-volume model — with no EV variant available. Despite this, the ICE fleet ageing dynamic creates a Golden Window of elevated parts demand through 2028–2029 that ISZE is positioned to capture.

Two actions still require IML support: (1) D-Max EV launch timeline for post-2030 revenue planning; (2) EQ-HIT PMS service mapping to enable the ITUK PMS pilot. ✅ BESCO lubricants programme sanctioned by IML — May 2026. FY2027 launch confirmed (€442K revenue, 13% GP). Without the remaining two, ISZE cannot model EV revenue or scale the PMS pilot beyond manual curation.
