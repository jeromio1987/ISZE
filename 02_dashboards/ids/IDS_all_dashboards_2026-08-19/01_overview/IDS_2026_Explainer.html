2. Short timeline

The compare tool exists because the result workbooks were not a network you could sit in front of a distributor with.

  1. Until 2026IDS evaluations arrive as Excel result sheets. About five colleagues process the files — ranking lists, country packs, follow-up. Readable as a pile of workbooks, not as one network.
  2. Jerome builds HTMLThe dealer-compare sheets are built in-house: one country, a map, the dealer versus the national average, and a quiet anonymous network benchmark. Same reading layer on every market. No second scoring system — a reading tool for feedback meetings.
  3. Dealers respondDistributors and dealers are happy with the compare tool. They can see themselves against their own country and against the network without opening twenty Excel files.
  4. 31 Jul 2026Tomoe Baumann (IMG): red-marked dealers in France (20) and Spain (5) stay on the list and map but are left out of averages. Italy and Switzerland keep everyone in.
  5. 15 Aug 2026v1.4: one network average on all 19 country sheets. Then Section E for UMI is recorded as not scored and taken out of that average — so 0/27 cannot be read as a vehicle-sales fail.

3. Roadmap — what to put on it

Keep the compare tool. Use IDS as leverage on dealer scores. Do not turn a missing section into an awards penalty.

1

Keep the HTML compare tool as the reading layer. Do not go back to Excel-only for DB feedback. Country pack + map + national average + anonymous network average is what dealers already accept.

2

Section E policy, then stop arguing 62.6%. Either UMI HQ completes E as a second wave (Shelly’s line), or IMG formally scopes E out of the 280-point awards scale. Until then: A–D only, fill not_scored, no ranking on a section they did not sit.

3

Use IDS as leverage to improve dealer scores — not as a sales forecast. Diagnose (hit list), contract (3–5 actions with owner and date), pull (monthly return). Checklist gaps drive Kaizen. Do not claim “IDS up → parts sales up”.

4

Israel operating work is Section D (Parts). 61.6% vs 78.7%. Stock, counter, genuine display, fill tracking, monthly targets — the D questions. Do not chase 29 workshops to back-fill a vehicle-sales checklist they were never asked to run.

5

Incomplete / not-scored lane stays visible. France and Spain red-marks, UK LCV blanks, UMI Section E. Record them. Never paint them as a fail in the average.

6

Do not invent live stats. No occupancy %, no GPS capture on these sheets. Portugal 63.3% stays on the Portugal pack only. Hover every derived figure for the formula and the snapshot date.

4. Discussions we want with the DB

The distributor is the lever. IDS opens the door; the OS keeps it open until actions close.

Talking line

“We are not here to argue a total score. We are here to pick the dealers who are below the country average, agree three actions, and come back next month to see what closed. On UMI, the aftersales gap is Parts — not vehicle sales. Section E was not assessed; it is out of the average.”
