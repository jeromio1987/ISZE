What we need to measure back orders and fill rate properly

A back order (BO) is a customer order line we could not deliver yet. Fill rate is the share of ordered lines we deliver complete without that wait. Internal working note, 15 Oct 2026, written after Saskia's email.

Verdict. We cannot measure a true fill rate today. We can show how old the open back orders are, and we can show the fill rate each distributor reports against its own target. A true fill rate needs the history of every order line: what was ordered, when, and when it arrived. We hold only a list of what is still open, and that list shows only the orders that failed.

What we can and cannot say today

We can say

  • How old the open lines are, per distributor and per part type.
  • Which parts keep coming back on the weekly list (UTI has 39 weeks of lists).
  • Reported fill rate against target, with the month it refers to.

We can say with a stated margin of error

  • A fill rate rebuilt from several weekly lists: a line that leaves the list was delivered or cancelled.
  • Whether delay sits at IML (the Isuzu parts warehouse), in supply from Japan, or in our own picking.

We cannot say

  • A fill rate by lines or by pieces for any distributor that does not report.
  • Which customer a delayed warehouse line belongs to: no file links the IML order to the customer order.
  • How long a part really took to arrive: arrival is not in SAP.

Several weekly lists can rebuild the flow

A weekly list is a stock of open lines. Comparing two lists turns it into a flow. UTI already has 39 weeks, so the method is proven there.

What changes between two weekly BO lists tells us what happened

The catch: a line that leaves the list is not proof of delivery, because it can also be cancelled. Tiers 1 and 4 confirm which.
