Design proposal - not the dashboard

Fifteen graph forms for the aftersales growth KPIs, and the one that stops a projection being read as a fact

Every chart below is drawn by hand in this file with inline SVG: no chart library, no external file, no network request. The numbers are representative. Where a figure matches a verified source the card says so in its tooltip, and where a figure is rounded or invented to show the shape of the form, the tooltip says that instead. Nothing here is wired to live data. The board is illustrative throughout: a tooltip label reading "real figures, with limits" marks a shape taken from a verified source with its own caveats attached, and every dotted label on this page carries the same standing caveat, that this is a design mock-up and not a report a decision can be taken from.

The single idea worth taking first

The layout rules, the house palette and the tooltip convention are ordinary craft questions. The one that changes what the reporting set is for is this: a chart has to show the range, not only the value it is usually asked for.

The method-range fan: a projection is drawn as a range between the two documented methods, with the method break on the axis, never as a single line presented as fact.

Our fleet projection leaves the history on the same axis, in the same colour. The history is built on a survival curve and the projection is not, so the two rules disagree more the further out you go. The chart has to widen rather than pick one of them.

The form that does this - a widening band between the two methods, the break marked on the axis, and the as-published figure shown as a dashed reference line that deliberately stops before it leaves the band - is Form 13 below, placed next to the current form.

The charts

Each card carries the business question the form is built to answer, the form itself, and a tooltip on every derived figure. Read the cards in order: the first pair shows the improvement on spend per unit, and the fleet projection closes the set.

Form 1 · ranked bar with a reference

Which markets spend most per truck per year, and how far is the spread?

A ranking, not a distribution. Rank order replaces the axis, and the network average becomes a rule on the chart rather than a footnote.

The value is printed at the bar, so the number is quotable without opening a tooltip. The spread runs from 88 to 389 euro. real figures, with limitsSpend per unit equals parts billed divided by vehicles in service, 18 markets, full-year 2025. Denominator quality differs: the UK commercial fleet is registration-based; several other markets are modelled. The 2026 rows are excluded because they carry partial-year sales against a full-year ceiling. Form 10 · ranked gap bar

Where is the unmade parts revenue concentrated?

Ranked in euro, not in percent. One market can be a hundred percent short of a tiny ceiling, so the ranking exists to allocate attention.

Form 11 · dormancy against the normal gap

Which distributor-family combinations have gone quiet for longer than their own reorder rhythm?

Each row is measured against itself, so a fast-moving filter family and a slow-moving clutch family are comparable.

A fixed threshold in months would flag every slow-moving family and miss every fast one. real figures, with limitsThe multiple is months silent divided by that distributor-family's own median gap between orders, so two times means twice as long as normal for that specific line. Revenue is the trailing twelve months, so a dark bar with a euro figure is recoverable and a dark bar with none has already gone. Data horizon March 2026. Source: the defection alert file.Illustrative mock-up: this is a proposed form drawn with representative numbers to show the shape, not a published figure and not a report to act on.

Form 12 · market error ladder

How far off is the published fleet projection in each market, and in which direction?

A deviation anchored on zero, with the fleet size printed beside each bar, so a large percentage on a small row is not mistaken for the largest problem.

Six markets are overstated by 49% to 102%; one large market is understated by 25%. real figures, open integrity itemThe published 2026 to 2030 fleet projection removes about 3% of the fleet a year, while the 1990 to 2025 history uses a survival curve implying about 10.2% a year for trucks and 12.3% for pickups. The two rules do not agree, so every projected figure is overstated except UK commercial, which already uses the survival model, and Israel commercial, which is understated by about a quarter. Form 13 · uncertain projection, fan with the method break

How many vehicles will be in service in 2030, within the two methods we have actually documented?

A range, not a line. The band widens with distance because the two published methods disagree more the further out you go.

The published projection is drawn dashed only to 2029 and deliberately not extended into 2030. real figures, open integrity itemThe band comes from the two defensible endpoints and nothing else. The lower edge applies the truck survival curve, about 10.2% removed a year, with registrations held at the 2029 rate. The upper edge applies the truck curve with registrations continuing to grow at the 2025 rate. The as-published figure is roughly 9% too high in 2026 rising to about 59% by 2030, or about 45% if 2030 registrations carry forward at the 2029 rate. It is not a confidence interval: it is the distance between two arithmetic rules.Illustrative mock-up: this is a proposed form drawn with representative numbers to show the shape, not a published figure and not a report to act on.

The next level ladder

Five rungs above where we are. Each rung stands on its own, so stopping at any level leaves a usable report rather than a half-built one. Levels 1 and 2 need no new data and no new engine.

LevelWhat it isWhat changesCost to buildWhat it unlocks
Level 0Status quoStatic tables and single-series bars, coloured by hand. Words do the work.already therenothing new; a number is findable if you look for it
Level 1Form disciplineOne relationship per chart, the right form for that relationship, titles as business questions, values printed at the bar.one pass per dashboard, no data worka reader gets the story from the titles. The highest return on the ladder
Level 2Reference and deviationNetwork median, family norm, reference dealer price and the normal reorder gap become visible reference lines.small, because the reference values already exist in the derived filesevery number answers “against what?”. This is the line between a report and a management tool
Level 3Range instead of pointEvery projected or modelled figure is a range or a band; the method break is marked; measured and modelled look different everywhere.moderate, and it needs a decision rather than a buildthe reporting set becomes safe to put in front of a board
Level 4Residual and euro made visibleEvery composition names its residual, and every percentage carries the absolute behind it.small in the charts, real in the definitions, because some residuals are not computed yetthe argument moves from “our share is low” to “this is the euro on this line”, which is what gets a resource decision
Level 5Interactive drill with a decision attachedToggles and drill-downs, plus a permanent coverage strip and a scenario slider restricted to the two documented endpoints.highest, and it needs stable data contracts firstdaily use instead of monthly reading, and one place where a number and its definition cannot drift apart

Three forms specified but deliberately not drawn

They are in the written proposal with their tooltips and their titles. Drawing them now would give a false impression of readiness.

FormWhy it is not on the board
ScatterSpend per unit against fleet size, with bubble area as uncaptured value. Held back until the fleet denominators in the projection years are corrected, because a bubble sized on a wrong denominator is worse than no chart.
Box plotSpend per unit by weight class. The weight decode in the vehicle register reads a chassis generation code as tonnage, so a 3.5-tonne truck is charged the parts ceiling of a 7.5-tonne truck. Held back until that is fixed.
Age overlayActual consumption against vehicle age, where the replacement interval is known. It needs part-level intervals assembled into a series: real data work, lowest priority.
