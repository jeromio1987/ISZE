Getting closer to real demand — the data improvement roadmap
Current limitation: Every AIM score, customer ranking, and PMS capture rate in this dashboard is built on ISZE→DB sell-in (L2). This is a supply signal, not a demand signal — distorted by distributor MOS cycles, campaign loading, year-end stocking, and promotional buys. The two actions below are the highest-leverage fixes. Both are requests to BVDB. Neither requires IT integration.
The Demand Signal Pyramid
L4
Dealer → End Customer
True demand. VIN-level service records.
Needs dealer DMS access or workshop MOU. Years away — don't start here.
L3
DB → Dealer sell-out  ⭐ Near-demand — Target
What dealers actually order from the distributor. No bulk-stocking distortion.
ITUK: already partial (quarterly pivot file). Others: distributor MOU needed.
L2
ISZE → DB sell-in  ← what AIM uses today
⚠ Distorted by: MOS policy cycles · promotional campaign loading · year-end stocking · bulk orders
Shortcut: L3 ≈ L2 − ΔDB inventory. Two stock snapshots (Jan + Dec) = sell-out proxy.
L1
IML → ISZE shipments
Supply-side only. No demand signal. Not used in AIM.
Key insight: The inventory snapshot ask is the single highest-leverage action. It converts your existing L2 data into an L3 sell-out proxy — without any new collection infrastructure, without a dealer MOU, and without asking BVDB for anything they don't already have in Power BI.

Honest framing. Neither Claude (via Cowork) nor GPT Enterprise is an officially sanctioned ISZE tool. Neither has direct access to SAP, Power BI, or the ISZE shared network drive. Both are fed curated MD files. This page documents the actual architecture, the routing rules between the two tools, and the governance boundaries that keep this compliant.
TL;DR — Five Rules
1 · Access reality
Claude/Cowork reads C:\Users\jerom\Desktop\claude\ live. GPT Enterprise sees only what you manually upload as Knowledge. The ISZE shared drive, Power BI, and SAP are out of reach for both.
2 · Use each for its strength
Claude: multi-file local work, dashboards, xlsx/docx, Python, long memos.
GPT Enterprise: portable sparring, quick calcs on uploaded files, on-the-go email, offline-from-laptop.
3 · Don't integrate them
Claude and GPT cannot talk to each other from your position. You are the bridge. That's also the only compliant posture given neither is sanctioned.
4 · Single sync point: Context Pack
The 6 MD files in GPT_Enterprise/2_KNOWLEDGE_FILES/ are canonical. Claude edits them. You upload to GPT monthly. That's the whole sync protocol.
5 · Power BI integration = don't
Live PBI ↔ GPT needs Azure admin + IML sign-off you don't have. Ask BVDB for a scheduled CSV export into your folder instead. 5% of effort, 80% of benefit.
Not sanctioned — know the boundary
No SAP/Power BI trade actions. No upload of restricted content (Kosha internals, personnel, active disputes) to GPT. Paper trail: CHANGELOG.md, AI_Briefs/, git commits via deploy.sh.
