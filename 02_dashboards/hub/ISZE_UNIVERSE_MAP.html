The roadmap, in the order it has to happen

The strategy paper's own six stages. Each stage names the evidence that lets you move on.

  1. 1

    Agree the scope and the tools

    A clear five-tool portfolio, with routine services written out

    Move on when: This map, the strategy paper, the presentation and the showcase all say the same thing

  2. 2

    Pin the classification release

    One named file, version and build date

    Move on when: Reproduce a family and product-form view from the frozen release

  3. 3

    Open two prepared views

    One Atlas view and one dealer-standard profile, ready before anyone is invited

    Move on when: A participant completes a real task and finds it clearer than today's review

  4. 4

    Run the dealer sample

    Ten dealers in two markets, including one that does not want more reporting

    Move on when: What they corrected, what was useful, what it cost them, and why some did not use it

  5. 5

    Run the commercial line

    At least two offers that clear the size filter

    Move on when: Contribution measured on the same scope before, during and after, including returns

  6. 6

    Evaluate, then widen or stop

    A stated result, good or bad

    Move on when: Real evidence and effort, not a screen that rendered

What it costs, and when it stops. The paper recommends starting from the existing £100,000 route, with an offer-support line of £60,000 to £90,000 inside it, a minimum offer size of £150,000 of eligible billing, and £15,000 to £25,000 of dealer enablement spent as accepted tasks rather than as an entitlement. It stops if, after two eligible rounds, no participant can name a decision the tool changed — and it does not pause, add a report, or widen the group to find a better answer.
