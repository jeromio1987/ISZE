01Where the invoiced euro lands

The ladder from everything billed down to parts net. This part is clean — it uses only identified line types and needs no price list.

Billed → parts net, CY2025

Realization vs the 2026 list, by year

Net ÷ list value. Read the slope, not the level — see below.

The concession number cannot be separated from the price increase — and this is the finding, not a caveat. There is one price list on disk, dated 13 March 2026. Measured against it, realization climbs steadily the closer billing gets to that date: 0.8865 in 2023, 0.8956 in 2024, 0.9185 in 2025, 0.9416 in Q1 2026. That is the shape of a price list that has risen, applied backwards to older invoices. The apparent “€2.47M concession” in CY2025 is mostly the 2026 increase, not money given away. I am not presenting a list-to-pocket waterfall on this basis, because it would read as leakage and it is not.

All families share the same vintage bias, so the ranking is meaningful even though the level is not.

Since every family carries the same list-vintage bias, the spread between them is real: the parts most exposed to the aftermarket are also the ones realizing least against list. That is the expected direction, and it is the first thing worth testing once dated lists exist.

02Is the price list itself sound?

Unglamorous and the highest-yield view here. None of it depends on the list vintage — these are structural defects that are true regardless of when the list was cut.

€ at stake by defect type, CY2025

Hover for the part-number counts.

€4.96M of parts revenue — 15.1% — was billed on 522 part numbers that have no entry in the price list at all. Not a mismatch of format: these numbers are absent. Whatever price they were invoiced at did not come from the list, so for a sixth of the revenue there is no list to compare against, no governance, and no way to see a concession.
800 part numbers worth €1.82M are still being billed under a number the list itself marks as superseded. The list carries a Newest P/N for each, so the replacement is known. Every one of those lines is priced off a record that should no longer be in use.
15,699 lines worth €2.20M were invoiced more than 5% above list — and that holds at 11–12% of lines in every year including 2023, against a list dated 2026. Billing above a list that is itself the newest and highest version is the anomaly worth explaining first: either surcharges are landing in net value, or those parts were repriced downward, or the list is wrong for them.

Realized €/unit for the identical part number across distributors, same period. Vintage-free by construction — every point is the same list, same year. Limited to parts sold to five or more distributors with at least €20k of revenue.

Widest spreads

p90 ÷ p10 of realized unit price.

Median spread across 220 parts is 1.122× — tight, and that is good news. Pricing across the network is broadly consistent. The exception list is short and therefore actionable: 9 parts exceed 1.5× and one exceeds 2×.
Front brake pads are the standout, twice. 8982447940 runs €71.02 to €127.80 across 12 distributors (1.80×) and 8975056801 runs €62.68 to €83.37 across 15 (1.33×) — the same component family appearing twice at the top of the list. Together they carry roughly €127k of the €163,445 that would move if every distributor below the median were brought to it.

04What this needs next

Dated price-list snapshots are the single unlock. One list means no view of price movement, which is what blocks a real list-to-pocket waterfall — exactly the same gap that makes elasticity impossible at ISZE level. If prior-year IE40 lists exist in SAP or an archive, view 01 becomes a genuine realization measure instead of a diagnostic.
The billing extract has no gross or discount column. Only net_sales_value, so discount, SPA and mix cannot be separated even with dated lists. Adding gross and condition values to the extract would turn view 01 into the decomposed waterfall it should be.
Explain the 522 unpriced part numbers before anything else. Fifteen percent of parts revenue sits outside the price list entirely. Until that is closed, every realization figure covers only 85% of the business.
Sales to distributor, CY2025 unless stated. Excluded: SaTy CR/RE credit notes and returns, non-part lines (transport, rebate, late-payment interest, service fee), and Turkey, which is not an ISZE distributor. No margin figure appears anywhere in this dashboard — the billing extract carries no cost, so any margin would be invented; realization index against list price is used instead.
