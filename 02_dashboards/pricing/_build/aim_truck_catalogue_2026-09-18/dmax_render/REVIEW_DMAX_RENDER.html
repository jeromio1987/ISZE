D-Max — real 3D model, rendered in Blender

The model is a genuine 2021 Isuzu D-Max built by a third-party modeller (David_Holiday, CC-BY 4.0), obtained free and key-free from the Objaverse open dataset — no account, no card. I loaded it into Blender, wrote the paint, glass and chrome shaders and lit it, and rendered it. This is not my hand-drawn geometry. Reload this page to pick up new renders.

The tail was not a model defect — it was my measuring error

The previous version reported the load box and rear overhang as 15–19% short of Isuzu's specification, and that was wrong. It found the two axles by taking the largest gap in the model's rim geometry and splitting there. This model also carries extra wheel material behind the rear axle, which drags that split rearward, so the wheelbase read as 130.1 model units instead of 122.1. Because the wheelbase sets the scale, every dimension was then 6.6% too small — and the tail, being farthest from the front axle, was hit hardest. Reading the axles from the brake-rotor discs instead (which exist only on road wheels) fixes it. The tail is now within about 2–6% of spec, and the load box is 1.520 m long against a published 1.495 m.

How real does it actually look

Measured against a real D-Max silhouette, the outline overlaps by IoU 0.837 — about five sixths — at the hero camera angle of azimuth 80°, elevation 18°. The bottom edge, so the wheels, arches, sill and bumper line, agrees to 1.6% of vehicle height, which is why it sits on the ground like a truck instead of floating. The top edge is off by 4.3% (p95 11.7%), and that is where the drift lives: the roofline and the cab-to-bed transition. It reads clearly as a D-Max. What it is not is photographic, and the reason is specific — the model file carries no textures, so the paint is a shader I wrote rather than a photographed surface. No orange peel from a real paint map, and the badge is extruded geometry lit by the studio rather than a decal with its own artwork. Put crudely: a good 3D render, roughly three quarters to five sixths of the way to a press photograph, and the missing part is surface, not shape. The one dimension that is still genuinely off is bed depth, at 0.548 m against a published 0.490 m: on this model the rail sits about 3 cm higher and the floor about 5 cm lower than Isuzu's numbers imply, and I could not find a change that closed that gap without visibly distorting the top of the load box, so it is left alone and stated here.

What else changed in this pass

Beyond the scale correction.
ItemWhat was done
CameraSwept 28 azimuth/elevation combinations including rear three-quarter views and elevations up to 22°, scored each against the real silhouette, then picked 80°/18° — the best-scoring angle that also keeps the nose, cab and load box legible. 0.837 vs 0.837 for the best elevation and clearly ahead of my first pick at 72°/22°.
BadgeThe ISUZU wordmark is now real extruded letterform geometry on its own chrome material, sitting on a dark backing plate, placed from the measured grille band. The model's own badge is a material split on a smooth face and had no edge to catch light.
Number platesA read plate surface in a recessed dark frame at both ends, sized 470×104 mm. Deliberately blank — no registration number and no distributor branding is invented.
Small detail addedFour door handles on the beltline, two windscreen wipers on the cowl, two fuel filler doors, brake discs and calipers already present in the model and now given their own metal and rubber responses, and a darker wheel well and arch liner.
Paint and surfaceLayered basecoat plus clearcoat with a procedural metal flake on the coat normal and a very shallow panel wave — the two things that make paint read as paint rather than plastic. Faint large-scale base-colour variation so panels are not perfectly uniform. Glass got a higher IOR and a coat; the tyres a much rougher rubber with a mould texture; the chrome a faint directional roughness so the highlight smears.

What I could not do

I did not lengthen the load box, because it turned out not to need lengthening — the fix was to the scale, not the geometry. I did not close the bed-depth gap, for the reason above. I did not add grille slats: the grille already has a moulded two-bar structure and the front-on check showed extra slats would have read as clutter rather than detail. And there is no texture on the paint, so up close it will never pass for a photograph no matter how many samples I give it. Everything above is a measurement off the model or off the render, not an impression — and if you want the evidence for the scale correction, the `probe_*.py` scripts in this folder are what found it.
