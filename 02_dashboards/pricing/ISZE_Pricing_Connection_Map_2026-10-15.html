How pricing, AIM, PRISM and tenders connect

Internal read, 15 October 2026. Your markets only; the MIDI countries (France, Spain, Italy, Switzerland, Poland, Serbia) are left out.

They share one key already, the AIM parts category, and most of the pipes exist. What does not exist is evidence flowing the right way: the price revision does not see rival prices or tenders, and the tenders are not recalculated when the list price moves. The first limit is data, not wiring: rival prices are known for only a few hundred part numbers, and no tender has a recorded outcome.

What is in place and what is not

LinkState today
Revision → AIM categoryBuilt. The simulator sorts every part by AIM category and checks that its base matches monthly billing (148,293 rows, EUR 32.60M, difference 0.00). Per market: PR00 +5.92%, UTI +5.58%, UMI +5.64%, CIS +4.85%.
Revision → price intelligenceOne way only: a link from the simulator opens the intelligence page on a part number. The simulator's "dealers buy less" response is an assumption you set; it is not driven by how far rivals sit from us.
Tenders → distributor and service demandPartly built. 480 of 734 tenders are matched to one of your 16 distributors, and 310 are tagged as aftersales. But only 10 carry a euro value for parts demand, 29 a fleet size and 88 a contract value.
Tenders → rival pricesA script exists, but the file was last built on 2 Oct and covers 7 of 664 opportunities. Of its 98 category rows, 70 have some rival price. Do not read a blank as "no competitor"; it means we have not looked.
Tenders → revisionNothing. Tender parts demand is valued at today's price, so a +5.96% move changes tender value and nothing recalculates it. The simulator mentions tenders zero times.
Tenders → what price winsNo tender has a recorded outcome (0 of 734), so tenders cannot tell pricing what a winning level looks like.
Intelligence → PRISMSeparate. The rival price work lives in its own pages and scrapers; PRISM's GPS and PMS pages do not use it.

The options, best first

  1. Put rival-price coverage beside the move, per AIM category, in the simulator. Low to medium effort, reads data that exists. It tells you for each category whether a move is backed by evidence or is a guess. Risk: most categories will show "no evidence" at first, which is true and useful, but it will look thin.
  2. Re-price tender demand at the revised list and rebuild the tender price file. Low effort. It shows how a move changes tender value. Risk: only about 10 tenders carry a euro value today, so the picture is small until more are filled in.
  3. Record tender outcomes for your distributors' markets. Little code, but it needs people to report wins, losses and prices. It is the only route to learning what price wins.
  4. One hub page that links everything. Cheap, but it would only be a menu. I would not do it.
The one decision: should the first link be option 1, rival-price evidence next to each AIM category's price move, or option 2, tender demand re-priced at the new list? I recommend option 1, because the move is the decision in front of you and it is the one place the evidence is missing.
