SPA approval — turnover vs profit

Internal exercise, 15 October 2026. Brake pad price/volume check, Duncan’s ITUK braking SPA, and why the dual approval base (turnover and profit) is blocking the GPS SPA route.

Verdict

A −20% price does not produce +80% brake-pad sales on our numbers. That claim needs a price sensitivity of about −4. The pad reading is about −1.6; all brakes about −2.4. Even those are rough. Duncan’s own SPA asks for about +10% volume as the floor, not +80%. Yoshimoto san rejected the SPA unless there is growth in units and profit — that dual base is the hard KPI.

Why SPA applications stay few — and hard to approve

During GPS / sales reviews we asked distributors for SPAs. Few formal applications came back. The ones that did (UTI spa2 rounds, IUK wipers, ITUK brakes) struggle on the approval math.

  • Turnover base — more billed euros / more pieces than the reference period.
  • Profit base — more euro profit than before, even after the discount. That is the hard one when the distributor needs a real price cut.
  • On pads, a −20% price already cuts unit margin from 53% to ~41% at ISZE (illustration). You need about +61% volume just to hold the same profit. The honest pad reading does not give you that.
  • August ITUK MoM already left open: SPA approval criteria — turnover-only vs profit — ISZE management decision.

Ask for management

Finance has been saying our margin is already high. On the commercial book, profit is the easier side of plan; turnover is the one slightly behind. If Yoshimoto san’s rule stays “units and profit”, every meaningful SPA (Duncan’s brakes first) dies on the profit base.

Ask: approve SPA cases where we clearly grow turnover (and units), even if euro profit on those lines is flat or slightly down — provided the company margin/profit position overall stays healthy. That is the only approval KPI that is realistically hard for GPS SPA work. Without it, distributors stop applying, and ITUK’s brake PMS stays on hold.
