Which price moves have evidence behind them

Internal read, 15 October 2026. The 22 markets in the price revision simulator v6 (no MIDI). Base: what we billed, July 2025 to June 2026, EUR 32.60M.

Most of the price move has no rival evidence behind it, and our own history cannot fill the gap. Rival prices exist for 17% of billed euros. For 71% we know of no alternative at all, and that does not prove those parts are captive; it may only mean nobody has looked.

Genuine parts sold outside our network show no sign of broad undercutting. The same genuine part is priced outside our network on 1,092 of our parts (EUR 14.61M, 45% of billing). On 985 of them (EUR 13.24M) it costs more than the price a dealer would charge. Only 23 parts (EUR 442,742) sell below our own list.

Volume reaction cannot be measured from our own price changes. Our list moved by more than 5% on only 3% of parts in 2024 and 2025, and the estimate changes sign depending on how it is cut. The simulator's assumption of -0.16 stays an assumption.

Brakes is still the place to check first: largest step of the big categories (+7.7%), rival prices on 65% of billing.

2. What do we know about alternatives?

Per category: how much of the billing has a rival price, a rival number without a price, only our own value line, or nothing.

The dark segment is what the evidence covers. The pale segment is the unknown. Where it dominates (body, electrical, engine, steering) we cannot tell a captive part, where a move is safe, from a contested part nobody has searched. Searching those categories is what separates the two. A rival number with no price still tells us where to look next; there are 103 such parts, EUR 3.40M billed.

Inconclusive. Every cut has a range that includes zero except one, and that one has the opposite sign of the main cut. The reason is the data, not the method: only 3% of part-years saw a list move over 5% (6% of the euros), so there is almost no variation to learn from, and volumes are noisy. Distributors also buy ahead of announced increases, which would push the estimate downward. The cleaner test is the sales-to-dealer files for IUK, ITUK, Ireland, UTI and Greece around the April 2026 increase. Per category the ranges are far wider still (for example brakes -3.2, range -7.5 to +1.1), so no category figure should be quoted.

What this says for the price decision

1. Check brakes first: biggest step among the big categories, and the only large one with real rival coverage. Brake pads alone step +10.1%.
2. Body, electrical, intake and exhaust, steering and lighting carry EUR 16.50M of billing, and rival prices cover 7% of it. Searching them for a rival, or confirming none exists, is the data job that pays most.
3. The genuine price outside our network does not argue for holding back on the EUR 13.24M of parts where it sits above the dealer price, with the caveats above. It says nothing about the aftermarket.
4. Volume reaction stays an assumption until the sales-to-dealer files are used. Tender results are now readable, but the public result notices cover vehicle supply and carry no parts prices, so they tell us who wins fleets, not what a winning parts price is.
