How the price proposal is priced

Six short lessons on the +5% workbook sent on 11 October 2026: where each price comes from, what a margin does to it, what the switches do, and why the 5.0%, 5.3% and 6.0% are three different numbers. Written after Masumi's questions of 12 October.

1What is being priced, in euros

The workbook sets a new list price for 15,273 part numbers. The base is what the distributors actually bought from us from July 2025 to June 2026: EUR 32,595,853.

The increase is measured on that base, not on a simple average of the part numbers. A part that sold EUR 800,000 counts far more than a part that sold EUR 300. So "+5.00%" means: new price × same volume, minus old price × same volume = EUR 1,629,793, and EUR 1,629,793 ÷ EUR 32,595,853 = 5.00%.

2From factory price to list price: one part, step by step

Every list price is built the same way: the factory base price in yen, turned into our cost, then divided by (1 minus the class margin). Each part number belongs to one of seven price classes issued by IML. The class sets the margin.

Factory base price JPY 30,004 -> JPY 32,105 = +7.00% (our cost moves +7.00%) Our share of the move the whole book is scaled to 90.54% of each part's own move Proposed list move 7.00% x 0.9054 = +6.34% List price (PR00) EUR 241.79 x 1.0634 = EUR 257.12 Margin before 53.00% (cost is 47.00 of every 100 of price) Margin after 1 - (47.00 x 1.0700) / (100 x 1.0634) = 1 - 50.29 / 106.34 = 52.71% Change -0.29 points (the sheet shows -0.29%)

The part moves less than its own factory move because the book as a whole has to land on +5.00%, and other parts are held (lesson 4). The margin falls by 0.29 points, not by 1 point, because the price still moves most of the way with the cost.

3Why not simply +5% on every part

A flat +5% is easy to explain but it treats a part whose cost rose 15% the same as a part whose cost did not move. The first one loses margin badly, the second one gets fatter. The proposal follows each part's own factory move instead, then scales all moves so the total still lands on +5.00%.

An honest reading of this table The proposal is not the best of the three on margin: it loses slightly more margin overall (-0.50 points) than a flat +5% (-0.45), because it holds parts at today's price on purpose (pad kits, discs, service parts in eight markets) and asks other parts to carry the difference. What it buys is a better spread: EUR 2.4M of billed value loses more than one margin point, against EUR 5.6M under a flat +5%.

4The holds and the recovery extra: who pays for what

A hold means the distributor keeps paying today's price on that group of parts. Every euro held has to be earned back somewhere else, or the total falls below +5.00%. That somewhere else is the recovery extra: a few extra points on parts where the market allows it. The workbook solves the size of that extra by itself, so the total always lands on target.

5The UK shop test (Summary rows 7 and 8)

Masumi asked what these two lines mean. Both compare us with an independent UK shop (called KS in the workbook) that sells the same part number. To compare like with like we first need our reference dealer price: the price a dealer pays us, grossed up to what the dealer would charge.

How the reference dealer price is built List price × 2.19. Read as a margin: 37% at the distributor step and about 27.5% at the dealer step. (1 ÷ 0.63 = 1.587, and 1 ÷ 0.725 = 1.379; 1.587 × 1.379 = 2.19.)
Before 2 October 2026 the workbook used × 1.785 (1.40 to the dealer, 1.275 to the customer). The paper of 2 October replaced it with × 2.19 and marks it provisional. The 'Read me' sheet still describes the old × 1.785 in its main text and corrects it only in a note at the bottom. That mismatch is probably part of the confusion.
Row 7 - "Extra where the UK shop sells above our reference dealer price" (ON) If the UK shop charges more than our reference dealer price for the same part number, there is room to raise our price. These parts get an extra on top of their own move, capped at the part's own factory move plus 5 points.

Today it touches 5 part numbers, EUR 244,594 billed, average move 14.23%, EUR 34,794 extra.
Row 8 - "Hold at 0% where our reference dealer price is more than 1.6 times the UK shop price" (OFF) The opposite case: our dealer price is far above the shop, so we may be pricing the part out. The test holds those parts at today's price.

It is switched off, so it holds nothing today (0 part numbers). Reason: in the April 2026 dealer data, parts that got a bigger increase did not lose measurably more volume.

Example of row 8 if it were on: rotor 8983767550 (EUR 703,041 billed). Our dealer price is 4.03 times the UK shop price, far above 1.6, so the whole of its UK share would be held. The 'Proposal' sheet marks it "100% held: far above UK shop price" but, with the switch off, gives it +6.34% before the market holds. In the eight service markets it is then held at today's price anyway, because brake discs were decided held on 11 October.

Source: workbook 'Summary' rows 7-8, 65, 68; 'Proposal' row 4; 'Read me' rows 17 and 28. The 1.6 limit and the 2.19 basis are the workbook's own settings; I have not tested other values.

65.0%, 5.3% and 6.0%: the gap and what +5.3% does

Three numbers sit in the same conversation, and they do not measure the same thing.

The gap Masumi names is 6.0% minus 5.3% = about 0.7 points: our cost goes up by more than the price we would charge.

The gap in euros: two ways to read it, and what is still open As margin: at +5.3% the workbook still loses 0.37 margin points. On the new billed value (EUR 32,595,853 + 1,727,580 = EUR 34,323,433) that is about EUR 127,000 of margin a year that we would not earn at today's margins.

As price: passing on the full 0.7 points would be 0.7% x EUR 32,595,853, about EUR 228,000 more on the list prices.

The two differ because one counts only what the cost increase does to margin, the other counts the whole price move. Which one Masumi means by "gap amount" is a question for him. A second open point: I cannot see which purchase value the 5.3% is weighted on. If IML's 5.3% uses IML's weights and our 6.0% uses distributor volume, part of the 0.7 points is only a difference in weights, not a real cost gap. That needs one check with IML before a single number is quoted.

How the gap could be recovered, as Masumi asks (inside the price revision or through the GPS increase):

  • Inside the revision: only a higher target adds euros. The holds do not: because the total is pinned to the target, releasing a hold only changes which parts pay. Each 0.1 point of target is worth about EUR 32,600 a year.
  • Through the GPS increase: the gap is then recovered on the markets' spend target instead of on the list prices, and the price list stays as proposed.
