How to read this paper

"+5%"
The average rise of our price list — the price at which we bill our distributors — weighted by parts billed from July 2025 to June 2026. The same parts at the same volumes would bill 5% more: EUR 1.63M on EUR 32.6M. Individual parts move more or less than 5%. Whether distributors pass it on in their sales to dealer is their decision; we do not see their resale prices.
Parts billed
Our net sales of parts to distributors, in euro. It is the weight behind every average in this paper.
Sales to dealer
What our distributors sell on to their dealers, from the distributors' own monthly files. Used to see whether dealers bought less after a price rise.
Genuine
Isuzu-branded parts, supplied through IML (Japan) or IMIT (Thailand) and sold by us.
Aftermarket
Parts made by other brands to fit Isuzu vehicles, sold by independent shops and garages, usually cheaper than genuine.
Reference dealer price
What a customer pays at a dealer counter for a genuine part: our list price times 2.19, covering distributor and dealer margins. A construction used to compare outside prices with ours; provisional.
Euro effects
Every euro figure is the effect of a price change on the volume billed in the last twelve months, before any change in what dealers buy.
Status labels
Decided Suggested, not decided Open Negotiation topic Next price list
Decided = management decision taken. Suggested, not decided = recommended, awaiting a decision. Open = cannot be decided yet; what is missing is named. Negotiation topic = a commercial term per distributor, not a price list change. Next price list = for the following revision, not this one.

1Summary and decisions

In short.IML and IMIT raise their prices to us; this paper sets how our price list follows. The list rises by 5% on average, worth EUR 1.63M a year, with exceptions where a price rise would cost us business; every exception is paid for elsewhere on the list, so the total stays at +5%.

IML and IMIT raise their prices to us by 6.0% on average in their September 2026 price revision, weighted by parts billed (service parts 5.5%, repair parts 6.6%, body and collision parts 7.0%).1 Our price list rises by +5.0% on average: EUR 1,629,793 on EUR 32,595,853 of parts billed. Each part follows its own IML/IMIT increase, so parts IML and IMIT raised more go up more. Where a rise would cost us business — brake pads, service parts in eight markets under pressure, body and lighting parts in Israel — prices stay where they are today; the EUR 388,914 this gives up is recovered on repair and body parts for which we found no aftermarket alternative.

NewPrices can now be set per parts category. Every part number sits in a parts category (filters, brake pads, body panels, lighting and so on), so a decision can apply to a whole category in one market instead of part number by part number or to the whole list. Every decision below was made this way. Section 3.

2How prices are set, and what IML and IMIT changed

In short.Our list price follows what IML and IMIT charge us, through a fixed formula. In their September 2026 revision IML and IMIT raise their prices to us by 6.0% on average; each part passes on 90.5% of its own increase, which lands our list at +5.0% overall (EUR 1.63M) after a few exceptions.
Average IML/IMIT price increase to ISZE, September 2026 against September 2025, weighted by parts billed

Parts billed: service EUR 19.2M, repair EUR 8.8M, body and collision EUR 4.6M; from Japan EUR 15.9M, from Thailand EUR 15.1M. 82 part numbers (EUR 1.6M billed) carry no source of supply; they average 1.0%, mostly no change. Weighted on IML's own basis (quantity times yen base price) the same revision is 5.96%, against 6.01% weighted by parts billed. The previous revision, of September 2025, was about 3.2% (weighted by quantity billed) and fed our April 2026 list.

From the IML/IMIT increase to +5% on our list

Passing on the full IML/IMIT increase would raise our list by 6.0% (EUR 1.96M). Instead every part passes on the same 90.5% of its own increase. The pad kits and the parts in categories a distributor already loses stay at today's price, and a small extra applies where UK web shops sell the same part dearer than us (section 4); together these land the list at exactly +5.00%. Our margin falls by about 0.5 points, because our list rises less than our cost.

The formula behind every list price

Our selling price is built from IML's or IMIT's yen base price, converted at the corporate exchange rate, reduced by our purchase discount, and divided by one minus a class margin. IML assigns each part to a margin class, and the class alone sets the margin; nothing about the part's competition, market or demand enters the formula. There are eight margin levels: 56%, 53%, 52%, 50%, 47.5%, 45%, 44.5%, 20%. Three in four part numbers (74.9%) carry 53%. On the priced lines our margin is 51.09% (EUR 15.15M gross profit on EUR 29.65M billed), and 84.3% of parts billed earns between 45% and 56%.

Purchase terms and exchange rate

From 5 January 2026 the purchase discount on Japan-sourced parts fell from 38.0% to 32.7% (we now pay 67.3% of the yen base instead of 62.0%); Thailand is unchanged at 38.9%. The weaker yen largely offsets it. For an unchanged base of JPY 16,370, our cost is EUR 62.00 on the October 2025 list basis (163.7 JPY/EUR), EUR 63.83 in April–September 2026 (172.6) and EUR 61.72 at the rate announced for October 2026 to March 2027 (178.5): −0.45% against the list basis. The exchange rate moves the euro price and the euro gross profit; it never moves the margin percentage. Whether the announced rate also applies to Thailand-sourced parts is to be confirmed.

Principles agreed in September, still standing

  • Replace the automatic class margin with decisions part by part and category by category: reduce selectively where a credible competing offer leaves the distributor too little margin, keep the price where the position is right, increase selectively where a genuine advantage supports it.
  • No universal 8% ceiling on a part's increase. Larger increases need evidence and approval. This revision caps every part at its own IML/IMIT increase plus 5 points (section 6).
  • Parts in the 20% margin class keep their price.
  • How a price is approved: compare the alternative offer (product, quality, date, availability, price level); allow distributor and dealer margin; check our net economics (cost, exchange rate, selling support); record the evidence, the commercial purpose and the expected result.
  • Every priced line carries a reason, and separates our decision from a change passed through from IML or IMIT. The revision should also state its own effect on parts billed per truck in service, split into price, volume, mix and fleet, so that next year's increase is not read as an improvement.

Two reminders on the arithmetic of a cut: a 5% lower net price needs about 11% more volume to keep gross profit at a 50% margin (an illustration, not a forecast); and a reported internal precedent — a battery-electric vehicle battery priced at about 1% margin to answer an insurance-cost concern — shows that an exception can serve the wider vehicle offer. The battery case is management-reported and not verified here.

3Prices per parts category

In short.Every part number now sits in a parts category, so a price decision can apply to a whole category instead of to one part number at a time or to the whole list. That is how the decisions in this paper were made: a category facing competition stays at today's price in the markets where it matters, while the rest of the list moves. The categories carry no euro effect of their own; they are the means by which each decision's euro effect is applied.

The parts categories were approved on 2 October 2026. They arrange the list into 22 main families, 115 categories and 236 part types, anchored on the Isuzu parts catalogue. All 15,273 part numbers in this revision are found in it; 1,306 of them (EUR 0.21M, 0.6% of parts billed) still have no category assigned.

Three rules on top of each part's own increase

  1. Brake pad kits stay at today's price. 29 kits, EUR 1,178,671 billed. Two kits carry large IML/IMIT increases: 8982447940 (+24.9%) and 8982169220 (+21.9%). At an unchanged price their class margin falls from 45% to about 31–33%. Kit 8982447940 is the front pad kit bought mainly by Isuzu Truck UK and the Israel trucks distributor; the first draft of 29 September had it at +17.0%. Keeping it at today's price answers the leading dealer complaint in the UK truck network.
  2. Parts at today's price where the distributor already loses the category: on lines where a distributor sells under 45% of what its vehicles need in that category (69 part numbers affected).
  3. A small extra on 5 parts where a UK trade web shop sells the same part number dearer than our reference dealer price, limited to what that evidence supports.

What dealers did after the April 2026 rise

The April 2026 list change (median +11.7%, confirmed on Isuzu Truck UK invoices at +11.6%) gives the first usable test of how dealers react, measured on the distributors' sales to dealer. Pooled over Isuzu Truck UK, Isuzu UK (D-Max), Ireland and Portugal, a part that rose 1% more than similar parts sold 0.16% fewer units (standard error 0.23): statistically indistinguishable from no reaction. It is not precise enough to set prices part by part and is used only for volume scenarios. Isuzu UK (D-Max) is the exception: -1.08 (±0.90), and its dealer units fell 6.5% over the same months.

Before the market decisions, the EUR 1.63M becomes about EUR 1.35M if the pooled reaction applies, and EUR 0.22M in a pessimistic case using the lowest 95% bound of the measurements. The pessimistic case is a stress test, not a forecast.

Four front pad kits, one function

Four front pad kits share one description at four list prices: 8975056801 and 8975546610 at EUR 82.25, 8982447940 at EUR 89.11, 8982169210 at EUR 102.65 — a 25% spread — and each network buys almost only its own (Isuzu UK (D-Max) the cheapest, Isuzu Truck UK and the Israel trucks distributor the two dearest). None is superseded by another. Whether they are interchangeable is a catalogue question that is still open (section 14).

5Market by market: where service parts stay at today's price

In short.Twenty-one markets were read one by one on the same evidence, to find where a price rise would cost us service business. In eight markets service parts stay at today's price, and in Israel body, collision and lighting parts as well; together this gives up EUR 388,914 of the +5%.

Each market was read on what it buys, how its fleet moves, how much of its service work we win (the service share: service parts its distributor bought against what its fleet should need at the service intervals; network 28% in 2025), what it pays against our list, and what dealers and distributors say. The list stays one price per part; keeping a category at today's price in one market is applied as a distributor condition on that category.

When service parts stay at today's price. Vehicles we do not count buy in the market; or dealers measurably reacted to the April 2026 rise; or the fleet is flat or shrinking while our service share is below the network or falling; or a documented price complaint on service parts together with a low or falling share. Repair parts follow IML/IMIT in every market. Israel adds body, collision and lighting, because of the insurance total-loss rule (section 9). Armenia and Kazakhstan are left out of the market decisions.
Who gives up what: increase not taken, by market and part group

Total given up: EUR 388,914. Service parts at Isuzu UK (D-Max) alone are EUR 148k, the largest single item. The figure includes the Israel D-Max lighting decision (EUR 4,435), which the market review of 30 September named but had not yet counted.

Sources. market-by-market review of 30 September 2026: SAP parts billing 2023–August 2026, dated price lists, the service-share measure by market and category, fleet planning estimates (July 2026, corrected 2026 column), sales to dealer files for the UK, Ireland and Portugal, distributor review minutes July–August 2026, Isuzu monthly market packs; Israel body and lighting recomputed in the recovery analysis of 2 October 2026.

6How the EUR 389k is recovered: three ways considered

In short.Keeping prices at today's level in eight markets and in Israel gives up EUR 388,914. To keep the average at +5%, that amount is recovered elsewhere on the list. Three ways were compared; the decision is to recover it only on repair and body parts for which we found no aftermarket alternative, about +3.7 points on 8,447 parts, with nothing extra on service parts.
From the +5% before market decisions to the decided recovery: the total stays at +5.00%

Why (b)

  • Service parts have alternatives. Filters, pads, discs and clutches are sold by aftermarket brands, and service parts are where the complaints and the market decisions are. Way (a) would put EUR 137k back on service parts, and way (c) EUR 91k; way (b) puts nothing there.

  • Way (a) pushes hardest on parts IML and IMIT already raised most, so 56 parts reach the cap, against 5 under (b).

7Isuzu UK (D-Max): the risk, and a suggested exemption

In short.Isuzu UK (D-Max) is the only market where dealers measurably bought less after the April 2026 rise. Its service parts already stay at today's price, but the decided recovery adds EUR 91k on its repair and body parts. Exempting it is suggested, not decided: the other markets would then carry that EUR 91k.

Isuzu UK (D-Max) dealers measurably bought less after the April 2026 rise: -1.08 (±0.90) — for every 1% a part rose more than similar parts, dealers bought about 1.1% fewer — and dealer units fell 6.5% over April–July against the same months of 2025. The recovery puts EUR 91k of extra on its repair and body parts. Its body parts that rose in April fell 13.5% against its own median, on 166 parts — a noise-level reading, but it points at exactly the parts the recovery raises. The UK D-Max market is also under pressure from outside: the double-cab benefit-in-kind change of April 2025, and pickup registrations down 61.2% year on year in August 2026.

Suggested, not decided Exempt Isuzu UK (D-Max) from the recovery. Its repair and body parts keep their own IML/IMIT-based increase (through the same distributor condition as the service parts); the EUR 389k is recovered on repair and body parts in the other markets. The total still lands on EUR 1,629,793; repair and body parts with no aftermarket alternative outside Isuzu UK (D-Max) carry +4.8 points instead of +3.7. Isuzu UK (D-Max) averages 2.7% instead of 4.1% (before market decisions 5.1%). Parts at the cap stay at 5; the typical moved part rises about 11.2%.

9Collision, insurance and lighting in Israel

In short.In Israel a high parts price can make an insurer write off a repairable vehicle, and we lose the whole repair. For that reason the Israel D-Max distributor keeps body, collision and lighting parts at today's price (EUR 20k of increase given up) and the Israel trucks distributor keeps lighting (EUR 8k).

In Israel a vehicle is declared a total loss when damage reaches 60% of its pre-accident value, and the insurer may settle as a constructive total loss from 50–55% (two policy readings differ). The repair estimate is built from our parts list prices. A high parts line can therefore push a repairable vehicle over that limit and lose the whole repair — and, for a fleet on a repair-and-maintenance contract, the parts for the remaining contract years. Routine service parts are not affected.

  • The limits were verified in the policy for vehicles up to 3.5 tonnes, which covers the D-Max. The clause for heavier vehicles was not found, so for the Israel trucks distributor the exposure is unverified.
  • The same policy allows a light to be replaced by a part of "similar description or characteristic", and requires new or genuine parts only for vehicles up to two years old.
  • The Israel trucks distributor's July 2026 presentation shows the collision package rising from EUR 679 to EUR 2,144 (N-Series 7.5 t) and EUR 924 to EUR 2,314 (F-Series 10 t) between the old model and the new N-Series (P700); a rear light +1,228%; the bumper split from one part at EUR 143.37 into four at EUR 320.00. Part of the lamp gap may be a different lamp rather than a list increase. The distributor runs 1,700 trucks on five-year repair-and-maintenance contracts.
  • Its lighting is EUR 133,667 of billing (3.9% of its parts billed), already 74% in the lowest-margin class (47.5%).
Decided The Israel D-Max distributor keeps body, collision and lighting at today's price (EUR 20k of increase); the Israel trucks distributor keeps lighting (EUR 8k). This replaces the September idea of giving lighting a smaller share of the increase than bodywork. Beyond price, two levers remain: the bumper and grille packaging on the new N-Series, which is a question for IML, and a paid-on-genuine-use arrangement with insurers — in 2018 a funded rebate to the insurer, split equally between IML and ISZE, was proposed for exactly this case.

13Background that still stands

In short.The September papers carried four strands of work that this revision does not replace. They are kept here in short because each bears on the next price list; none of them changes the euro effects in this paper.

The pickup buyer is not a truck buyer

The UK operator's licence does not bind the D-Max: it is plated at 3,100 kg (the licence applies from 3,500 kg) and is exempt as a dual-purpose vehicle. The exception is towing: with a 6,000 kg train weight, a D-Max towing plant is a licensed combination, and that customer is less price-sensitive — but we cannot tell him apart in our sales data. Independent UK garages advertise D-Max servicing at up to 50% below the dealer while preserving the warranty, and the parts are visible on consumer price sites. Ireland carries the heaviest compliance burden (an annual commercial roadworthiness test and a statutory maintenance duty); Portugal buys the pickup as a tax instrument (over 90% registered with three seats). In Israel D-Maxes run 34,443 km a year, the highest of the pickup markets; 66.4% of D-Maxes on Israeli roads are privately owned, while the best-selling new trim goes mostly to leasing companies.

Refurbishment is a third price level below the aftermarket brands

A cleaned particulate filter costs roughly 3–30% of a new part, depending on market and vehicle class. Israeli regulation permits removing the filter for cleaning, repair or replacement (reported, not re-verified), and a specialised refurbishment trade serves garages, insurers and leasing companies there. Our own warranty records show the Israel trucks distributor's filter claims at EUR 696,907, 76% of the filter cost outside the stated 3-year / 100,000 km terms, and claim costs of 1.2–1.8 times our own list price (mechanism not established). The lever is timing rather than price: keep the after-treatment price flat once each vehicle's warranty ends. Isuzu Truck UK has supplied new filters at remanufactured-level prices on a new-in-lieu basis since 2017; the question is why Israel does not get that answer.

How other manufacturers price

Cost-plus by class is what most manufacturers do; the money is on parts without an alternative (practitioner bands: competitive parts 25–35% margin, parts without an alternative 55–75%), while our list earns one band around 51% for both. "Matching quality" parts from the same supplier in its own box sit at roughly 60–80% of a genuine price. Listed independent distributors earn 27–29% gross margin. Sensors and after-treatment are contested in the truck channel; the parts genuinely closed to others are modules paired and coded to the individual vehicle and, until EU Regulation 2026/699 opens it from 23 December 2026, driver-assistance (ADAS) calibration. Every European truck maker runs a lower-priced own parts line; ours is the Best Value Parts (BVP) range.

Volume response cannot be read from history

Uniform increases leave almost no price variation to measure against; the April 2026 rise was the first with enough spread, and it shows a pooled reaction indistinguishable from zero (section 4). The only route to a real measure is a field test: one family, a group of distributors, a matched control group.
