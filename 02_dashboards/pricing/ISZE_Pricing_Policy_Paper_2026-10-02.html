1Summary and decisions

The price list rises by +5.00% on parts billed: EUR 1,629,793 on EUR 32,595,853 of parts billed between July 2025 and June 2026. Every part moves with its own factory base-price change, scaled so that the book lands exactly on +5%. Twenty-one markets were then read one by one; in eight of them service parts are held at today's price, and in Israel body, collision and lighting parts are held as well. Those holds give up EUR 388,914 (23.9% of the step). The decision is to recover that amount on captive repair and body parts — parts where no competitor has been found — and not on service parts.

2How a price is set today, and what changes

Our selling price is built from the factory's yen base price, converted at the corporate exchange rate, reduced by our purchase discount, and divided by one minus the class margin. The class is a letter the factory (Isuzu Motors Limited, IML) assigns to each part, and the letter alone sets the margin. Nothing about the part's competition, market or demand enters the formula.

Purchase terms and exchange rate

From 5 January 2026 the Japan purchase discount fell from 38.0% to 32.7% (we now pay 67.3% of the yen base instead of 62.0%); Thailand is unchanged at 38.9%. The weaker yen largely offsets it. For an unchanged base of JPY 16,370, the formula cost is EUR 62.00 on the October 2025 list basis (163.7 JPY/EUR), EUR 63.83 in April–September 2026 (172.6) and EUR 61.72 at the rate announced for October 2026 to March 2027 (178.5): −0.45% against the list basis. The exchange rate moves the euro price and the euro gross profit; it never moves the margin percentage. Whether the announced rate also applies to Thailand-sourced parts is to be confirmed.

Principles agreed in September, still standing

  • Replace the automatic class margin with part-by-part decisions: reduce selectively where a credible competing offer leaves the distributor too little room, hold where the position is right, increase selectively where a genuine advantage supports it.
  • No universal 8% ceiling on a part's move. Larger moves need evidence and approval. (The +5% revision uses a per-part ceiling of the part's own factory move plus 5 points; section 5.)
  • Parts identity is live: every part sits in a three-level product tree (family, subgroup, part type), anchored on the factory catalogue. The tree identifies the part; market evidence guides the price.
  • Class T prices (20% formula margin) stay unchanged.
  • How a price is approved: compare the alternative offer (product, quality, date, availability, price level); allow distributor and dealer room; check our net economics (cost, exchange rate, selling support); record the evidence, the commercial purpose and the expected result.
  • Every priced line carries a reason, and separates our decision from a change passed through from the factory (a base-price correction, a part out of production). The revision should also state its own effect on parts billed per truck in service, split into price, volume, mix and fleet, so that next year's increase is not read as an improvement.

Two reminders on the arithmetic of a cut: a 5% lower net price needs about 11% more volume to keep gross profit at a 50% margin (an illustration, not a forecast); and a reported internal precedent — a battery-electric vehicle battery priced at about 1% margin to answer an insurance-cost concern — shows that an exception can serve the wider vehicle offer. The battery case is management-reported and not verified here.

Three rules on top of the factory move

  1. Brake pad kits stay at today's price. 29 kits, EUR 1,178,671 billed. Two kits carry large factory moves: 8982447940 (+24.9%) and 8982169220 (+21.9%). At an unchanged price their class margin falls from 45% to about 31–33%. Kit 8982447940 is the front pad kit bought mainly by ITUK and UTI; the 29 September draft had it at +17.0%. Holding it answers the leading dealer complaint in the UK truck network.
  2. Held at 0% where the distributor already loses the category: on lines where a distributor sells under 45% of what its trucks need in that category (69 part numbers affected).
  3. A small extra on 5 parts where a UK trade web shop sells the same part number dearer than our reference dealer price, capped at the evidence limit.

What dealers did after the April 2026 rise

The April 2026 list change (median +11.7%, confirmed on UK truck invoices at +11.6%) gives the first usable test of how dealers react. Pooled over UK trucks, UK D-Max, Ireland and Portugal, a part that rose 1% more than similar parts sold 0.16% fewer units (standard error 0.23): statistically indistinguishable from no reaction. It is not precise enough to set prices part by part and is used only for volume scenarios. UK D-Max (IUK) is the exception: -1.08 (±0.90), and its dealer units fell 6.5% over the same months.

On the proposal as built on 29–30 September (before the market holds), the step of EUR 1.63M becomes about EUR 1.35M if the pooled reaction holds, and EUR 0.22M in a pessimistic case using the lowest 95% bound of the measurements. The pessimistic case is a stress test, not a forecast.

Four live front pad kits, one function

Four front pad kits share one description at four list prices: 8975056801 and 8975546610 at EUR 82.25, 8982447940 at EUR 89.11, 8982169210 at EUR 102.65 — a 25% spread — and each network buys almost only its own (IUK the cheapest, ITUK and UTI the two dearest). None is superseded by another. Whether they are interchangeable is a catalogue question that is still open (section 13).

4Market by market: the 21-market exercise

Each market was read on the same evidence: what it buys, how its fleet moves, how much of its service work we win (the service share: service parts its distributor bought against what its fleet should need at the service intervals; network 28% in 2025), what it pays against our list, and what dealers and distributors say. The list stays one price per part; a hold is applied as a distributor condition on that part group, the way stock-order and customer conditions already differ today.

Rules behind a service hold. Imported vehicles we do not count buy in the market; or dealers measurably reacted to the April 2026 rise; or the fleet is flat or shrinking while our service share is below the network or falling; or a documented price complaint on service parts together with a low or falling share. Repair parts follow their factory move in every market. Israel adds collision and lighting, because of the insurance total-loss rule (section 8). Armenia and Kazakhstan (CIS) are left to their proposal move.
Who gives up what: the euro of step held, by market and part group

Total held: EUR 388,914. The exercise published EUR 388,040; the difference of EUR 874 is the UMI lighting hold, which the exercise named but did not count and which is now kept. Holding service parts in UK D-Max alone gives up EUR 148k, the largest single item.

6The IUK risk, and a suggested variant

IUK (UK D-Max) is the one market where dealers measurably bought less after the April 2026 rise: -1.08 (±0.90), dealer units -6.5% over April–July against the same months of 2025. Only its service parts are held. Option B puts EUR 91k of extra on IUK repair and body parts. And the IUK body parts that rose in April fell 13.5% against IUK's own median, on 166 parts — a noise-level reading, but it points at exactly the parts option B raises. The UK D-Max market is also under pressure from outside: the double-cab benefit-in-kind change of April 2025, and pickup registrations down 61.2% year on year in August 2026.

Suggested, not decided Option B with IUK exempted. IUK repair and body lines keep their proposal move (through the same distributor-condition mechanism as the holds); the recovery is spread over captive repair and body parts in the other markets. The book still lands on EUR 1,629,793; captive repair and body parts outside IUK carry +4.8 points instead of +3.7. IUK averages 2.7% instead of 4.1% (proposal 5.1%). 7Outside price evidence: what it can carry

An outside price is compared with our reference dealer price: the price list x 2.19, standing for what a dealer customer pays at the counter after distributor and dealer margins. The x2.19 basis is provisional (set on 2 October 2026). The September papers used x1.785 (distributor x1.40, dealer x1.275); their ratios are not comparable with the ones below and are not carried over. The index is the outside price, without VAT, divided by our reference dealer price: below 1.0 the outside seller is cheaper than us.

Outside prices against our reference dealer price (median per part)

Aftermarket brands: cheaper than us

266 parts carry an aftermarket shop price, 21.0% of parts billed. Median index 0.56x (euro-weighted 0.44x). 182 of them, EUR 6.0M billed, sit below 0.8x; only 46 parts (EUR 0.45M) have a rival dearer than us. The data mostly names parts to hold, not parts to raise.

Genuine sold outside our network: dearer than us

1,167 parts carry a price for genuine Isuzu parts sold outside our network, 50.4% of parts billed. Median index 1.17x; on 929 parts (EUR 12.8M billed) it is at or above our reference dealer price. This rests mostly on one source: an exporter's retail prices (1,093 of the 1,167 parts), and on the provisional x2.19 basis.

Is the data good enough?

  • "Captive" is mostly absence of evidence. 71% of parts (46% of the euro) pass the strict captive test; only 294 parts have any competitor evidence at all. Body parts look almost entirely captive, but pattern (non-genuine) body parts have simply not been searched. A pattern-part price check on the top 50 body parts would show whether option B's body set holds.
  • A part has a price range, not one aftermarket price. Front pad kit 8975546610 (list EUR 82.25) appears in 42 online listings in March 2026, from EUR 17.09 to EUR 213.40, median EUR 58.09, across very different brands and transaction levels.
  • We do not hold distributor or dealer resale prices and will not obtain them. Every dealer-level figure is constructed. Whether a distributor passes a change in our price on to its dealers is unknown and can only be answered by asking.
  • Israel is not a discount channel. Of the Israeli shop lines checked in September, none sat below a distributor purchase floor; the cheapest was 2.4x its floor. Parallel trade inside the EEA is lawful and cannot be closed by agreement.

8Collision, insurance and lighting in Israel

In Israel a vehicle is declared a total loss when damage reaches 60% of its pre-accident value, and the insurer may settle as a constructive total loss from 50–55% (two policy readings differ). The repair estimate is built from our parts list prices. A high parts line can therefore push a repairable vehicle over the threshold and lose the whole repair — and, for a fleet on a repair-and-maintenance contract, the parts for the remaining contract years. Routine service parts are not affected.

  • The thresholds were verified in the policy for vehicles up to 3.5 tonnes — which covers the D-Max (UMI). The clause for heavier vehicles was not found, so for UTI's trucks the exposure is unverified.
  • The same policy allows a light to be replaced by a part of "similar description or characteristic", and requires new or genuine parts only for vehicles up to two years old.
  • UTI's July 2026 presentation shows the collision package rising from EUR 679 to EUR 2,144 (N 7.5 t) and EUR 924 to EUR 2,314 (F 10 t) between the old model and the P700; a rear light +1,228%; the bumper split from one part at EUR 143.37 into four at EUR 320.00. Part of the lamp gap may be a different lamp rather than a list increase. UTI runs 1,700 trucks on five-year repair-and-maintenance contracts.
  • UTI lighting is EUR 133,667 of billing (3.9% of UTI's parts book), already 74% in the lowest-margin class (47.5%).
Decided UMI holds body, collision and lighting at today's price (EUR 20k of step); UTI holds lighting (EUR 8k). This replaces the September proposal to give lighting a smaller share of the increase than bodywork. Beyond price, two levers remain: the bumper and grille packaging on the P700, which is a question for IML, and a paid-on-genuine-use arrangement with insurers — in 2018 a funded rebate to the insurer, split equally between IML and ISZE, was proposed for exactly this case.

11UTI truck panels: no hold, availability first

Decided UTI truck panels are not held at today's price (2 October 2026). They take the normal step. The issue is availability, and it is addressed by stocking P700 panels against UTI's forecast.

Finding: UTI's panel business is held back mainly by availability, not by price. The price complaint is real on a few named parts, but billing shows no measurable volume lost to price. Holding all UTI panels at today's price would not have addressed what UTI has raised.

Availability

UTI has reported repeated back orders on body and panel parts through 2026, four to sixteen weeks from order to shipment, with the same part numbers on back order month after month, and has asked ISZE to stock P700 items to raise the fill rate. Our back-order list of 1 July 2026 shows 21 panel lines open, up to 125 days; a roof panel was open 139 days, and one front inner lid had been ordered four times, on normal, rush and vehicle-off-road terms, and was still open. The urgent (rush and vehicle-off-road) share of panel order lines rose from 5.3% in the first half of 2025 to 15.6% in January–March 2026.

Billing and price

  • Panel billing fell 18.0% in January–August 2026 against 2025, while units rose. 53% of the euro drop is a single F-series grille (8972670504), a 2025 stock build being used up. On the panels bought in both periods, units rose 9.5% and euro fell 5.3%. UTI's other parts fell 11.8%; other markets' panels rose 7.6%. UTI's own panel sales to its customers held at about 560–590 units a month.
  • On the same panels UTI paid a median +4.0% in 2026 against a median list rise of +12.4%; UTI pays about 74% of list on panels (79% in 2025). Across 123 panels, larger list rises did not lose more volume (rank correlation +0.10).
  • UTI's cost on P700 body parts is about a quarter of its own selling price (its July 2026 sales report), so a 6.4% step adds at most about 1.6% to its shelf price.
  • The price complaint names three body parts as competing with aftermarket and used parts: grille 8974289771 (+3%), front panel 8981226950 (+16%) and door 8982606020 (+16%). Two of them fit a price story: the front panel fell from 20 to 7 units and the grille from 28 to 12. The P700 collision-package increase (section 8) is a model change, not the April price change.

12Background that still stands

The September papers carried four strands of work that are not superseded by the +5% revision. They are kept here in short; each still bears on the next price list.

The pickup buyer is not a truck buyer

The UK operator's licence does not bind the D-Max: it is plated at 3,100 kg (the threshold is 3,500 kg) and is exempt as a dual-purpose vehicle. The exception is towing: with a 6,000 kg train weight, a D-Max towing plant is a licensed combination, and that customer is less price-sensitive — but we cannot tell him apart in our sales data. Independent UK garages advertise D-Max servicing at up to 50% below the dealer while preserving the warranty, and the parts are visible on consumer price sites. Ireland carries the heaviest compliance burden (an annual commercial roadworthiness test and a statutory maintenance duty); Portugal buys the pickup as a tax instrument (over 90% homologated with three seats). UMI's D-Maxes run 34,443 km a year, the highest of the pickup markets; 66.4% of D-Maxes on Israeli roads are privately owned, while the best-selling new trim goes mostly to leasing companies.

Refurbishment is a third price level below the aftermarket brands

A cleaned particulate filter costs roughly 3–30% of a new part, depending on market and vehicle class. Israeli regulation permits removing the filter for cleaning, repair or replacement (reported, not re-verified), and a specialised refurbishment trade serves garages, insurers and leasing companies there. Our own warranty book shows UTI truck filter claims of EUR 696,907, 76% of the filter cost outside the stated 3-year / 100,000 km terms, and claim costs of 1.2–1.8 times our own list price (mechanism not established). The lever is timing rather than price: hold the aftertreatment price flat once each vehicle's warranty ends. Isuzu Truck UK has supplied new filters at remanufactured-level prices on a new-in-lieu basis since 2017; the question is why Israel does not get that answer.

How other manufacturers price

Cost-plus by class is what most manufacturers do; the money is on the captive side (practitioner bands: competitive parts 25–35% margin, captive parts 55–75%), while our book earns one band around 51% for both. "Matching quality" parts from the same supplier in its own box sit at roughly 60–80% of a genuine price. Listed independent distributors earn 27–29% gross margin. Sensors and aftertreatment are contested in the truck channel; the genuinely gated parts are VIN-paired and software-coded modules and, until EU Regulation 2026/699 opens it from 23 December 2026, ADAS calibration. Every European truck maker runs a lower-priced own parts line; ours is the BVP (Best Value Parts) range.

Volume response cannot be read from history

Uniform increases leave almost no price variation to measure against; the April 2026 rise was the first with enough spread, and it shows a pooled reaction indistinguishable from zero (section 3). The only route to a real coefficient is a field test: one family, a group of distributors, a matched control group.
