Market by market:
where the +5% should land

Twenty-one markets, each read on the same evidence: what it buys, how its fleet moves, how much of its service work we win, what it pays against our list, and what dealers and distributors say. The proposal stays one list price per part; the market layer is a distributor condition, which SAP already carries.

30 September 2026 · a proposal on paper — no price, list or condition changed.

1 The answer

Service parts are where markets differ; repair parts are not. In 8 of 21 markets the evidence says keep service parts (filters, brakes, clutch) at today's price: the fleet is ageing and we are losing the service work, dealers measurably react, or the market runs on imported vehicles we do not even count. Repair parts follow their factory move everywhere — that is where older fleets spend more and have no alternative. Israel adds one more hold: collision and lighting, because of the insurance total-loss rule.

Across all markets: flat or shrinking fleets win the least service work

Service share = the service parts a market's distributor bought, against what its fleet should need at the service intervals. A growing fleet raises that yardstick, which works against it — and growing fleets still gained most. Across the 18 markets with a measure, the rank correlation between fleet change 2023-25 and share change is +0.49. The hold decisions also use the 2026 fleet forecast, which is why Belgium, Ireland and UMI can be read as ageing on their market pages.
