Why the country-level pairs fail

Scraped price vs GPS spend per unit — three points, no inference possible.

Do not quote the +0.67 as evidence that capture drives spend. GPS spend per unit is parts revenue ÷ parc. PMS capture is actual quantity ÷ a parc-derived expected quantity. Both sides carry the same parc term in the denominator and both rise with sales volume, so part of that correlation is arithmetic, not commercial. The Spearman rank version is weaker (+0.51). Treat it as a hypothesis worth a proper test with parc partialled out, not a finding.
The scraped-price-to-PMS/GPS link cannot be built from this data. Scraped prices exist for three countries; GPS and PMS are distributor-year metrics. That gives n=3 — r=+0.63 with p=0.57. Arithmetic, not evidence. Making it real needs the scrape extended to at least eight to ten distributor markets.
13 capture rows exceed their own denominator. Ireland runs up to 2.26× expected on brake rotors and breaches on 5 of 14 categories; ITUK, UTI, Cyprus, Romania and Denmark each breach on one to three. Broken expected-demand basis, not exceptional capture. Clamped to 1.0 for the correlation input only — never presented as a capture figure.
