Purpose
This document consolidates the main business direction in Masumi Hayakawa communications and separates strategic intent from execution risk. A separate key-contradictions report has also been created.
1. Executive Summary
The strongest strategic message is a shift from historic sales reporting to potential-based business planning, using UIO, GPS, distributor KPIs and sales-to-dealer data when available.
GPS is repeatedly treated as a central performance metric, but the calculation basis requires management confirmation because several possible data sources appear in the threads.
PMS developed from an ITUK-focused pilot into a broader growth and business-plan lever, with requests to quantify PMS inside GPS stretch amounts.
The lubricant project moved from survey and exploration toward UIO-based opportunity calculation and ISZE business-case development.
IDS and Kaizen work moved from evaluation and awards toward distributor-specific improvement actions, schedules and result tracking.
The main contradiction risk is methodology, not strategy: the intent is broadly consistent, while project execution rules changed over time.
Management takeaway
Before distributor conversations, the team should lock three items: GPS methodology, incentive evidence requirements and PMS target breakdown.

3. Strategic Theme: Genuine Parts Share
Masumi repeatedly frames Genuine Parts Share as the core way to understand market development. The GPS threads show requests for distributor sales targets to dealers, stretch amounts, sales potential and revised calculations using price increase assumptions.
FY2027 calculations were requested with a 5% price increase factored into parts sales per unit.
FY2028-FY2031 were requested with a 3% annual increase based on IML policy.
Work-in-progress files were considered acceptable where the purpose was management progress reporting.
The direction is to move away from simple sales history and toward a structured view of potential, demand and share.
Risk
GPS loses credibility if ITUK, UTI and other distributors are calculated using incompatible bases without clear explanation.

5. PMS Programme: From Pilot to Growth Lever
PMS is one of the most frequently recurring programmes. The April ITUK work focused on UIO, maintenance assumptions and parts sales potential. By July, the discussion included ITUK GPS stretch, PMS amounts within GPS, target quantity/amount agreement and MD approval preparation.
Early focus: ITUK modelling, dealer visits, real-world maintenance assumptions and data availability.
Later focus: target values, SPA support, MD approval timing and separation of PMS within GPS stretch.
Programme implication: PMS is no longer only a technical parts exercise. It is part of the business-plan growth mechanism.
Control implication: PMS scope and ownership must be defined per distributor to avoid overloading the same people.

7. Lubricant Project
The lubricant project starts as a centralised purchasing project and survey exercise. Masumi asks for packaging modes, distributor/dealer business distinction, stock ownership, missing distributors and ISZE genuine product results. Later she asks for UIO/demand potential and revenue/profit opportunity.
The project began with information gathering and distributor survey work.
The emphasis later moved to opportunity sizing: UIO, demand potential, revenue and profit.
The management expectation appears to shift from “collect feedback” to “prepare business case”.
The team should define current phase and owner to avoid Japan/ISZE misunderstanding.
8. Distributor Incentives and Support Funds
The incentive MOU thread is strategically important. Masumi asks how distributors use support money and whether incentives drive retail sales. She flags front-loading and inventory shifting as risks and states that incentives should be linked not only to distributor purchase amount but also to their sales amount.
Control principle
Incentives should reward behaviour that creates underlying market growth. They should not only reward a purchase spike that moves inventory from ISZE to the distributor.
Add distributor sales or dealer activation evidence to incentive closing files.
Ask each distributor how support funds will be used before paying support.
Check abnormal purchase timing around target periods.
Separate support for strategic initiatives from simple volume rebates.

10. Business Planning and FY2031 Outlook
The business planning material connects longer-term growth ambition with practical growth levers. The FY2031 plan thread discusses a 76% increase, maintaining the VP plan as presented in Kosha material and reallocating differences to IE. This sits behind the pressure to make PMS, GPS, incentives and distributor action plans concrete.
A strong long-term target requires a credible bridge from current results to future opportunity.
GPS, PMS, lubricants and distributor incentives are the practical levers that can support the plan.
If calculations are not consistent, future planning may look ambitious but not operationally grounded.
The strongest next step is to link each long-term number to a named action and accountable owner.
