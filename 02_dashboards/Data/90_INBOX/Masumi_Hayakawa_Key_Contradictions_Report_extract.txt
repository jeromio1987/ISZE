Executive Conclusion

The highest-risk item is the GPS calculation basis, because different calculation bases can produce different targets and therefore different distributor discussions.

1. GPS Calculation Methodology
This is the clearest and most material contradiction area. Jerome’s email explicitly asks for clarification because the working basis appears to have shifted more than once: first toward ITUK sales-to-dealer data to reduce assumptions, then toward ASC or ISZE sales-to-distributor logic using assumptions, and later toward sales-to-dealer data for ITUK while using sales-to-distributor data for other distributors.
Why it matters: sales-to-dealer data measures downstream distributor activity, while sales-to-distributor data measures ISZE invoicing and can be affected by stock movement or one-off orders.
Recommended resolution: create a one-page GPS methodology memo defining the preferred data source, fallback data source and approved assumptions when distributor data is missing.
Owner suggestion: Finance/Sales jointly validate the calculation basis before distributor target packs are issued.
2. Distributor Target Setting Sequence
In the KPI target thread, Masumi first asks the team to set up distributor sales targets to the dealers. She later rephrases the request and says that before setting the targets, each distributor should be asked for their target and that a schedule should be prepared for when targets will be received.
Why it matters: if ISZE sets the target first, the negotiation starts from ISZE’s model. If the distributor proposes first, the negotiation starts from the distributor’s own business plan.
Recommended resolution: use a two-step process: DB first provides target; ISZE validates against UIO/GPS/KPI model; final agreed target is recorded with both views.
3. Incentive Programme Logic
Masumi raised a direct concern that incentives linked only to distributor purchases could lead to front-loading or inventory shifting. She asks how distributors use support money and whether incentives are utilised to drive retail sales. This is not a contradiction in strategic goal but a tightening of control logic.
Potential contradiction: rewarding purchase target achievement may conflict with the strategic goal of genuine retail sales growth.
Recommended resolution: incentive MOUs should define acceptable usage of support funds, evidence of dealer/retail activation and a check against abnormal pre-buy behaviour.
4. PMS Scope Expansion
The PMS project started with ITUK modelling and visits, then progressed toward network-wide action planning, GPS contribution, SPA approval and distributor target amounts. The change is commercially understandable, but the implementation workload increased significantly.
Potential contradiction: a pilot approach requires learning and assumptions; a network-wide rollout requires standard templates, data readiness and programme governance.
Recommended resolution: separate PMS into pilot markets, ready markets and monitoring-only markets, with different expected outputs for each category.
5. Lubricant Project Ownership
Lubricants began as a centralised purchasing discussion with Tanaka-san/IML and distributor survey work. Later, Masumi asks for UIO potential, revenue and profit opportunity, which indicates ISZE is expected to convert the study into an opportunity model.
Potential contradiction: team members may still see the work as data collection, while Masumi is already steering it toward investment and business-case evaluation.
Recommended resolution: define current project phase, owner and decision gate: survey completion, opportunity sizing, distributor engagement, pilot recommendation.
6. IDS / Kaizen Action Logic
IDS initially relates to evaluation and distributor award criteria. The later requirement is much more operational: overall summary, distributor analysis, specific Kaizen recommendations, meeting schedules, confirmation dates and result dates. The contradiction risk is between award criteria and practical improvement action.
Potential contradiction: a distributor may meet a formal award criterion but still require targeted Kaizen action, or vice versa.
Recommended resolution: separate award eligibility criteria from improvement-action criteria and explain both clearly before distributor discussions.
