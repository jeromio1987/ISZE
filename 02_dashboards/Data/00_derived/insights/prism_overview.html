The one-line version

PRISM is not a dashboard that tells you what happened. It is a model that tells you what to do next. Each view below isolates a decision that today is made on gut feel, and puts a euro figure and a name against it.

How it is built — the four layers

UIOparc
How many Isuzu vehicles are on the road, by market. The ceiling on all parts demand.
GPSspend / unit
What each distributor actually spends per vehicle on genuine parts.
PMScapture
Of the maintenance demand we should see, how much we actually capture.
BVPmix
Where premium genuine loses to BVP — Isuzu's own value line — and where it can be won back.

Each layer is built on the one before it, so a single number — say ITUK's capture rate — is traceable all the way back to the vehicle parc, not assumed.

For the analysts — what's measured vs. inferred

Measured (straight from invoicing)

  • Capture rates — actual qty ÷ expected qty, per distributor / year
  • Genuine vs BVP (Isuzu own value line) mix and substitution
  • VOR / expedited premium — €753K of expedited revenue, 902 part×distributor pairs
  • Demand class — Syntetos–Boylan (ADI 1.32, CV² 0.49 cut-offs)

Inferred (model, internal only)

  • Leakage / under-service gap (the Sankey's third leg)
  • TAM ceiling above current capture
  • VOR premium net of order-size effect lands at ~5%, not the raw 7.7%
Discipline: measured and inferred are never mixed in a headline. Inference legs are labelled on every chart and are for internal decision-making, not for quoting externally.

Two leakage stories — never to be added together

97% of parts revenue is uncontested genuine. The contested slice splits into two different threats that get wrongly lumped into one "leakage" number. BVP is Isuzu's own "Best Value Parts" budget line, not third-party aftermarket — so BVP "capture" is an internal down-trade (we keep the sale, one tier down), whereas the named external-brand undercut (Eucon/AutoDoc probe) is the only genuine-€-out-the-door risk.

1 · Internal down-trade to BVP — mostly optics, not profit loss

€3.55M
Contested revenue — parts where genuine and a BVP equivalent both sell (2025)
36%
Of that contested money BVP wins network-wide — up to 92% at UTI & Kazakhstan — but it is retained at BVP margin
€107K
Real down-trade GP gap (genuine − BVP margin on the down-traded units) — oil filters are margin-accretive

The genuine ASP premium looks huge (fuel filter €31.78 vs BVP €9.82) but on a gross-profit-per-unit basis the gap collapses, because BVP carries healthy margin. Down-trade GP gap is computed on the contested PMS pairs that match into CY25 sell-in (≈€0.93M BVP revenue, 7 categories, 1:1 substitution assumed) — a floor. The "leak to BVP" is a revenue-mix question we control with our own pricing.

2 · External walk-out to named brands — the real GP at risk

€2.21M
ISZE gross profit at risk on the 25 benchmarked parts where a named aftermarket brand is priced cheaper
€1.68M
Dealer gross profit at risk on the same parts (at an assumed 27% dealer margin)

A price probe of 44 benchmarked PNs (€12.9M, 13% of revenue) against named external brands shows them undercutting genuine on 25; 8 are both contested and undercut. This is where genuine € can actually walk out of the channel.

Coverage & assumptions: the price probe covers 44 PNs only — a probe, not the full catalogue. The 27% dealer margin is an assumption, not a measured figure. ISZE GP is actual CY25 sell-in margin (distributor level). The €2.21M (external) and the €107K (internal) measure different things and must not be summed. Nothing here is attributed to a named competitor.
