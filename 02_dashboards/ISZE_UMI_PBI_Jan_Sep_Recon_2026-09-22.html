UMI, January to September — what the report says against what we billed

22 September 2026 · Power BI SALES DATA VP NEW, pages SALES HISTORY BY CAT.(qty) and (amount), customer UMI · compared with our own part-level billing for sold-to 1797, January–September 2024 against January–September 2025

Bottom line

Adjust the quantities and it compares cleanly, family by family. Adjust the euro and it does not land anywhere — but not for the reason I first thought, and I had to correct myself on that, so it is the first thing on this page.

The units tie out to the piece. The report shows 85,685 pieces for UMI in January–September 2024 and 91,518 in 2025; we compute 85,687 and 91,518 from our own billing. Better than that, the split by category matches too: his COMPETITIVE PARTS is 23,243 in 2024 and so is ours, his EMISSION RELATED PARTS is 1,300 and so is ours, and so on down the list. That is a genuine tie-out and it means his volume view of UMI is our volume view.

The euro is a different matter. The same page carries two euro tables, both internally consistent, and they differ from each other by €539,000 in 2024 and €325,000 in 2025. Both sit below what we billed.

Correction — I got this wrong first time

On my first pass I read the demand table's two columns the wrong way round and wrote that its year labels were transposed and its arithmetic impossible. That was my mistake and the table is fine. Every percentage on it equals its second column divided by its first, and the two columns sum exactly to the two totals it prints. There is nothing wrong with that table. Everything below uses the correct orientation, and the conclusions have been rewritten accordingly.

What I compared, and how I know it is UMI

The UMI on the qty page is sold-to 1797 on our side — the Israel light-commercial company. UTI is 1799 and is a different business; it never appears in these numbers. That identification is not assumed, it is forced by the figures:

  • His January–September 2025 unit total is 91,518 and ours for 1797 is also 91,518.
  • His 2024 total is 85,685 against our 85,687.
  • His 2024 category split matches ours line for line: COMPETITIVE PARTS 23,243 both sides, EMISSION RELATED PARTS 1,300 both sides, ENGINE PARTS 449 both sides, GLASS PARTS 68 both sides.

His filter panel was reading year as multiple selections and carried a stale "overview by qty" label, so the amounts table below it is what is live. I read the values off the visible table rather than relying on the label.

One caveat on dates: our extract runs to 31 August 2026, so for September 2025 I am using our own records rather than his August refresh. That is the correct choice for a September 2025 cut, and it is a small difference in the 2025 column.

The important thing in that chart is that neither gap holds still. The demand table sits 10% below us in 2024 and 24% below in 2025. The sales table sits 30% below in 2024 and 36% below in 2025. A fixed exclusion — returns, a set of customers left out, one currency screened off — would keep the same percentage in both years. These do not, so neither table is one of those. They are two different measures, and the differences are not explained by the window or by the customer, because we have already fixed both.

There is one further oddity worth recording. The sales table's pieces, 59,195 and 62,546, are 69% and 68% of the pieces on the qty page in the same two years. That is a fairly steady relationship, so it is a subset of some kind rather than a separate measure — but I could not identify which subset. I tried our lines by currency sign, by order type, excluding credit, excluding non-parts, and restricting to rows carrying a family; none of them reproduces it.

What I could not reproduce

The sales table's €1,869,601 and €1,759,060, and its pieces of 59,195 and 62,546. Neither the money nor the pieces match any cut of our data I can build. The nearest money cut is our family-carrying rows, and even that is still €265,000 above the sales table in 2024 and €516,000 above in 2025 — so the sales table is not simply a cleaner version of our number. It is a smaller one by a rule I cannot see from outside.

Two things I will not dress up. On oil and fuel filters the two systems agree closely on this market — within a fifth of a percent on oil units, within half a percent on fuel units. That is a clean comparison and it says his filter rows are usable for those two.

But air cleaners genuinely disagree, and the difference is ours, not his. His AIR CLEANER is 2,831 units in 2024 and 1,649 in 2025, and we can only place 26 and 6 of those units in our air-filter family; the rest we file under Other filter. Our Other filter moves the opposite way, 803 pieces up to 1,577, and its euro more than doubles from €34,060 to €69,082. So the thing worth chasing in UMI's filter business is our own air-cleaner mapping, and it is a €69k question.

Conclusions

1. For quantities, use the report and trust it

To the piece, category by category, on this customer. Not one of his seven displayed categories differs from our own count. That is a real result and it means the qty page is a usable view of UMI's volume.

2. For euro, do not use it, and do not spend more time trying to reconcile it

The same page gives two euro figures that differ from each other by €539,000 in 2024, and neither matches ours. The one you pick turns UMI's parts business into a 5.9% fall, a 13.5% fall or a 2.4% rise. Quote our number: ours is a complete sum of what we invoiced and credited, and the other two are not.

3. Ask the report's owner for two definitions

The sales table's measure and its filter, and whether the demand table is meant to be billed or ordered. That is the only way these numbers stop disagreeing, and it is a question about the report, not about our data. One hour of the report's source would settle it.

4. The real finding on UMI is our air-cleaner mapping

His air cleaner is 2,831 pieces and €36,574 in 2024, falling to 1,649 and €20,194. We can only place a handful of that in our air-filter family, and the rest sits in Other filter, which doubled to €69,082 instead. That is the same problem the group-wide comparison found: our air-cleaner mapping is behind the report's, and on this market it is worth €69k on its own.

5. The one thing in UMI's favour that is now measurable

Oil and fuel filters match his rows to within half a percent on units and within 2% on euro. So for the two service families that matter most to a maintenance conversation, the report and our tree already agree. That is a better starting point than the group-wide picture suggested.

What this does not settle

  • Our extract ends 31 August 2026. September 2025 comes from our own records rather than his August refresh, so the 2025 column is our version of that month.
  • Both of his euro tables are internally sound. The problem is not their arithmetic, it is that neither is the same measure as the other, and neither is ours. I could not identify either measure from the outside.
  • No month-by-month reconciliation. This is the nine-month window as a whole. If part of the euro gap is timing it would show month by month, and that is the next thing I would do.
  • You gave me the whole book to compare, not UMI. The export in Downloads carries no customer filter, so I identified UMI from the report and cut our side to match. The identification is solid on the totals and the category split, but if you meant a different customer, say so and I will redo it.
  • Units are not comparable between families. One oil filter is not one wheel rim, which is why the unit comparison is made inside each category and inside the filter basket, never as a single number.
